# Lisbon: A First-Time Visitor's Guide

### Getting Around

Lisbon's public transportation system includes trams, buses, and the metro, making it accessible to explore on foot or by transit. The historic yellow tram 28 is iconic and connects several neighborhoods. Taxis and ride-sharing services are readily available. Many central areas are walkable, though the city's hillside terrain means some streets are steep. Purchase a rechargeable card at metro stations for convenient travel across the network.

### Neighborhoods to Explore

Lisbon's distinct districts each offer different atmospheres. Baixa, the downtown grid, features grand plazas and shopping. Alfama's narrow medieval streets and azulejo tiles showcase old-world character. Belém combines monuments with riverside cafés. Príncipe Real offers contemporary galleries and restaurants. Each area rewards wandering and discovering local shops, restaurants, and viewpoints.

### Dining and Local Flavors

Portuguese cuisine emphasizes fresh seafood, olive oil, and regional specialties. Pastéis de Nata—custard tarts—are a must-try sweet treat found in bakeries throughout the city. Casual tascas (taverns) serve traditional dishes at modest prices, while the city also hosts contemporary restaurants. Markets like Ribeira remain vibrant places to observe local food culture and purchase fresh produce.

### Key Sites and Activities

The Jerónimos Monastery and Torre de Belém are UNESCO-listed landmarks in Belém. The Castelo de São Jorge offers panoramic views from its hilltop perch. Museums cover art, history, and design. Viewpoints (miradouros) scattered throughout the city provide photo opportunities and rest spots. The waterfront along the Tagus River is pleasant for walking and observing riverside life.

### Practical Information

Lisbon is generally safe and walkable for visitors. The best times to visit are spring and fall when weather is pleasant. Portuguese is the local language, though English is spoken in tourist areas. The city uses the Euro. Most establishments accept card payments, though some smaller shops prefer cash. Weather can vary, so pack layers for changing conditions.
