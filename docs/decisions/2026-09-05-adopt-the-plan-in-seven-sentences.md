# Decision: Adopt the Plan in seven sentences

## Context

The Plans-in-Real-Life design project (Claude Design `cd2e1f82`, boards 00–12) answered
five founder review passes and a holistic critique by adding mechanisms: ~90 frames,
twenty design rules, two authority proposals, twenty-two open questions. The founder
judged the result "too complex" and that the team had "tried to avoid too much
operational rules around changing, modifications, etc." A research pass (report §3m)
found the simplest successful coordination products converge on: a page plus a link,
changes told by people not propagated by systems, one proposer and one decider, and
very few notifications.

## Decision

The customer-facing Plan/Occasion design is defined by seven sentences, recorded
verbatim in the design kernel §11.15 (2026-09-05). In short: a page you read; one way to
change anything (say it); yours changes, someone else's receives your words; every
event is a sentence on its row; at most one sentence says what needs you; Vesper states
facts and recommends once, without buttons, only after a problem; failure says so once
and keeps your words.

The project is cut to the frames those sentences need (boards 01–06, 08, reduced 05/09/
11, 07 rewritten). Failure-state, arrival, stacking, peer-authority and prototype
material moves to labeled appendix boards (90–92) as engineering evidence, not design.

## Consequences

Easier: the design can be read in one sitting; engineering builds against seven
sentences; the read half (boards 01–04) ships first on existing Trip data.
Harder: Vesper says less; people must ask more; the pill's discoverability becomes the
main UX risk (report §3m recommends a contextual example phrase and one suggested next
phrase after each answer). Deferred: consequence-aware recommendation (former J2f/I1/I2/
W1 continuations) waits until the first four build steps are used. Out of scope:
PR-2 peer-occasion adoption-by-answers is withdrawn; PR-1 (two people, no named decider)
is deferred by founder ruling on 2026-09-05: the owner decides for two people until walkthroughs
show the asymmetry matters.

## Revisit trigger

Participant walkthroughs (none have been run) showing that people cannot tell what the
page is or what to say to change it; or engineering finding that a sentence cannot be
honoured without a state the customer must see. Add a new decision; do not edit this one.
