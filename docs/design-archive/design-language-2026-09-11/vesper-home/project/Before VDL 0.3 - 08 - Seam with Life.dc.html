What a flight does to Home, and does not
BOOKED · ONE RECEIPT, THEN QUIET
The booking email is brought or forwarded (Life 20F); Home’s next open shows one KEPT receipt with Undo and the trip card updated (“your flight is booked; three seats still held”). No crown: nothing operational is at stake for nine weeks. No countdown, no packing list, no “prepare for your trip” module. Between booking and the day before, the flight is a chip under the Life door and a mark on the trip row, and that is all.
THE DAY BEFORE · HOME’S CONSEQUENCE, NOT THE PASS
The crown is a commitment crown in the planning register: leave by, check-in open, bag drop, security estimate, one ask (check in). The ticket is present as one kick line with a door. Gate and seat are not shown because the gate is not posted; Home claims only what a source supports, the same law as the pass’s stub.
THE DAY OF · THE PASS IS THE CROWN
Life’s live state, in Home’s live window: the pass elevates, oxblood, provider facts replace static ones, “current as of” on the stub. Priority law from 02 Thursday applies: one ask, rows unchanged, the rest folded to reachable titles. The seat law holds: while Home owns this delivery, Life’s object page shows the same pass but no Return.
AFTER · FLOWN LEAVES HOME
Once the flight is occurrence-supported, the pass compresses under the journey in Life (01A state 3) and Home shows the next thing: Lisbon on landing. Home never shows FLOWN; the record does not congratulate itself.
PRODUCTION
Structured state (the ticket from the email, the Trip), two service reads (check-in window; gate and transit on the day), one route. No prose is generated; the truths are the provider’s and the stub says when they were last true. Every number here is a fixture.

The collision, and what held
WHY THIS ROW
Once kept objects can elevate into the crown, several objects can qualify as dominant on one afternoon. The seat law says one seat; nothing said which object wins. This row exercises the ranking on the hardest fixture we have: the same flight delayed, the group downstream of it, and a decision with a deadline arriving at the same time.
PHONE 8 · THE MERGE
Recovery and live commitment are one object, so the pass takes the recovery register instead of yielding to a separate crown. The consequence is stated as what does not move: the stay’s code, the others’ flight, Sunday. One ask: tell the five, as one message (05’s law).
PHONE 9 · THE FOLD
Dana’s train question would be a planning crown on its own (left column). Under a recovery it folds to one row with its deadline and no door. The page still has one ask, and it is the crown’s. The seam marks Saturday with her question so the deadline is visible without a second crown.
PHONE 10 · THE ASK MOVES
At 5:06 the message is sent; the crown keeps its register (the delay is still true) but its ask is spent, so the fold takes the page’s one ask: “Answer Dana” on her row. Demand budget unchanged; dominance unchanged; only the ask moved.
WHAT DID NOT HAPPEN
No second crown. No stacked banners. No “2 things need you”. The rest of the page stayed folded and reachable. Nobody in the group was told who asked for what; Dana’s question is hers and Nadia’s.
PROVISIONAL
The ranking is recorded in kernel §11.16 and decision 2026-09-05-home-borrows-life-pass-grammar as ruled on this drawing. If the founder reads these three phones and disagrees with the order or the fold, the decision record changes, not the phones.
THE PRIORITY COMPARISON · RULE A VS RULE B · AND THE WAITING WINDOW · DECISION NEEDED
Must one task be the price of another? Two rules on the same afternoon
Row three drew the provisional rule. This row draws the brief’s alternative beside it, then the state neither rule addressed: a live ticket with three safe hours in front of it.
THE COMPARISON
Two priority rules on one collision
A = kernel §11.16 as ruled provisionally · b = the brief’s alternative · decision needed

WHAT B CHANGES IN THE CANON
The one-ask rule is reread as one demanded response per page. An available action on the dominant object is a door, not a CTA, unless the object itself demands it (a boarding call, a check-in that closes). The ranking becomes a tie-break within “what would delay cost”, not the first question. Dominance and demand separate: the pass can be the largest thing on the page while Dana holds the only ask.
WHAT B RISKS
“Cost of delay” needs a judgment the composer must make from evidence: deadline, dependency, and whether an action is corrective. Where that evidence is missing, B degrades to A. A is simpler to build and to predict; B is more honest to the person’s time.
