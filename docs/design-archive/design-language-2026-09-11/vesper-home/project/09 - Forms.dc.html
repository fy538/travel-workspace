Reading order and non-visual alternatives
THE SEQUENCE
Read top to bottom: time, name, detail, then the leg to the next stop. Screen readers get the same order; the gold line carries no information the times do not.
THE TWO-LANE COMPARISON
Left lane then right lane, each as kicker, value, clause. The number is the claim; the colour is not. A text alternative: “River side 24°, wind off the water; the avenue 28°, masonry holds the day.”
THE RECONSTRUCTION STRIP
Left to right in time. Each mark names its kind and time in text; the hatched span is announced as “nothing kept until 1:20”. No hover, no precision tap: every node is also the unit’s door.
THE MAPS (06)
Announced as a list: the stops in order with their times, the flood line as a sentence, the two temperatures as a sentence. The map clarifies; it is never the only carrier.
LARGE TEXT
At 1.3× the sequence, the pass, and the rows reflow within 393px. The chip does not scale (object print); the row beside it does. The band’s object print is the one element that stays small, by the ruled exemption; it is never the only carrier of its fact.

WHAT THE CLIFF IS MADE OF
One peninsula, two ways of meeting the sea. The difference is not the view; it is the rock the view is cut from.
SORRENTOTUFF
The wall, the flat top, and the piers on the shelf at its foot
REFERENCE · WIKIMEDIA COMMONS · P. A. LECLERCQ · CC BY-SA 4.0
Volcanic ash from the Campi Flegrei, laid down across the Bay of Naples and now soft rock. The town sits on the flat top; the shelf below runs out shallow for a couple of kilometres under the water.
AMALFILIMESTONE
No shelf: the houses climb a slope that runs straight into deep water
REFERENCE · WIKIMEDIA COMMONS · G. MACLARTY · CC BY 2.0
Past Punta Campanella the peninsula’s spine takes over: the Lattari’s Mesozoic limestone and dolomite, hard and folded, rising to 1,444 m at Monte San Michele. The towns climb the slope instead of sitting on one.
HOW TO TELL WHICH YOU ARE ON
A flat-topped wall with shallow water below it: the ash. A road hanging on a slope above deep water: the limestone.
