Same plate, same body, same faces, same rows. What changes is what the page says first, and therefore what it is
The elements are the plate, the name, the body with its sources line, the faces of who has been, the verbs, and the metadata rows. Board 10 put them in one order. These are the six orders worth arguing about. The judgment under each is about reading, not taste: what a person learns in the first two seconds, and whether the page reads as written or as assembled. Hortus is real; body, hours and prices are fixture.

READING THE BOTTOM ROW
D is the most beautiful when the illustration cooperates and the worst when it does not; it also fights the canon’s no-scrim rule and the title register. E is a card, which is what we left. F asks before it tells.
RECOMMENDATION · C
Plate · name · byline of faces · body · sources · verbs · rows · stop. It is A with one element moved, and the move is the one that makes the page read as written for a person rather than about a place. The rows stay last because they are the specs, and specs belong at the end of a good piece of writing, not the beginning. D is worth one more board if the illustration system can guarantee a quiet lower third.
