WHERE PEOPLE LIVE ON THE PAGE
In the byline (faces and one label) and in the body (their words in italics, cited with their face). Never as a section. The sheet is the only section-like thing, and it appears only when a face is tapped.
THE FORM, FIXED
In the sheet: face, name, the grant in words, the date; the line verbatim; her photo if she attached one; the three answers. No like, no count, no “ask Dana”.
ABSENCE, ONE LOOK
No one wrote; she took it back; you blocked her; you are outside her audience; she granted a coarser precision. All render the same: the face is not in the byline, the sentence is not in the body. The page never counts what it hides.
WHAT CROSSES
A line can be brought into a plan (the receipt), into a Chat (the thread door), and into your keep. It never crosses into Vesper’s own sentences: the body may cite it, never absorb it.
