The laws
Drawings lead; prose supports — and a crown card keeps one voice for it: the serif deck, a single quiet line (16/22, ink-soft) under the sans title, above the instrument. Title claims, deck explains, instrument proves. One track, one gold mass, one dot — two labels, max; if it needs a legend, it’s wrong. Marks have body: filled bars and blocks, area under curves — hairlines only for the world’s baseline. Curves for physics, bars for crowds, blocks for intervals. Real data or the sentence — never false precision. World rhythms only; personal patterns stay in Life. Two instruments per viewport; they nest inside existing units. Eight categories, one instrument each: time, place (the map), quantity, parts, stages, intensity, change, and the type itself. From the research: only four shapes exist at card size — position-in-range, fraction-of-whole, count, delta; never scale an icon — repeat it; radial only for the truly cyclic; a print motif is warm only while every stamp carries real data. Every instrument keeps the sentence it replaced as its accessibility label. And an instrument is never a door — tapping one inspects its basis; it never navigates. Amended on self-review: silent ghost end-anchors are axis words, not labels (an unanchored interval repeats Apple’s documented failure); one meaning per mark — the dot-chain is footsteps only, oxblood solid is the constraint, the diamond stays the map’s; an avatar means another person — your own notes wear the note mark. And the §11.2 micro-type floor applies inside instruments: no label below 10px mono, ever.
