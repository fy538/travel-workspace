RECOMMENDATION
Content-led for the ordinary opening in a known city, on both rich and sparse supply. Map-led when orientation is the job: an unfamiliar city (05), or a spatial question typed into the line (“near Red Hook”). The map stays one tap away in the header and keeps the question, the entry and the scroll position when toggled (row below). This is an observation from rendered static frames; it is not validated by use.
WHAT REMAINS A HYPOTHESIS
That a person prefers a possibility before a map on a first open; that twelve labels read as orientation rather than clutter on a real device, and whether cluster labels read as enough; that the toggle feels like one exploration. The rendered visual review and, later, participant observation decide these.

The spatial question, and a lighter map
WHY THE MAP LEADS HERE
The person asked where, not what. The map answers that in one glance: Red Hook is across the water, the Print Room and the pier are its two marks, the ferry landing is the way in. The units below carry the names, hours and the one uncertainty; the ending offers the whole city, the other waterfront, and the same place on Saturday.
CLUSTER LABELS
The rich map-led opening with five labels instead of twelve: three near Canal Street, two downtown, two in Red Hook, three in Sunset Park, and the dated market. Less text before the first possibility; nothing named on the map. This is drawn as an alternative, not recommended over either column above.
