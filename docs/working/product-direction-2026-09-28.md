## 1. Where we landed

**What Vesper is.** A personal AI built on ordinary sharing, for yourself and
with the people in your life. It is:
- grounded in time and place;
- organized by standard artifacts;
- reaching across every area of a life;
- useful from the moment you document something.

The founder's own framing, September 26: "it's a personal ai agent, but it's
based on ordinary personal sharing, multiplayer sharing, + time and place,
organized / standardized by artifacts, extend to many areas in ones life, and
be useful from documenting."

**The center is the experience** (ruled September 25). Attention is how an
experience enters. The relationship between a person, their people and the
world is what compounds. A friend's words about a place inside a plan is one
expression of that center, not the center itself.

**The primary gesture is documenting** (ruled September 26), not saving and not
asking:
- You send Vesper what you encounter: a place, a photograph, a ticket, a menu,
  a book, a golf scorecard, a friend's recommendation.
- You send it for yourself, or for family or friends. The default audience is
  just me.
- It does not require diary-writing, prompts or streaks. The earlier framing
  called the record a by-product and emphasized looking forward. The follow-up
  in §1.1 sharpens this: making the record should be effortless, but the record
  itself is a first-class benefit, alongside what it makes possible next.

**The loop** (recorded September 27):
1. document;
2. a standard artifact;
3. collections, including threads read over time;
4. where they meet, answers to "what's right for this?" and prepared actions;
5. the person commits and lives it;
6. what happened is documented again.

Vesper prepares; the person sends or commits. Booking stays retired.
This is a complete possible path, not a funnel every contribution must finish.
Keeping, understanding, revisiting or sharing something can already be a
complete payoff; see §1.1.

**One composer, many doors** (recorded September 27; the email door amended
September 28):
- The doors are the OS share sheet (primary), inside Vesper, Chat, the connected
  inbox, and a friend.
- A share is artifact(s) + intent + audience.
- The control names the consequence: Keep, Send or Share.
- Keep first, correct after.
- A share with a question is kept and answered, with *Ask only* available.

**An open artifact catalog** (recorded September 27; mechanism R3 pending):
- About eight fixed meaning families sit over an open set of types that are
  defined as data.
- No one approves types. Personal types form on their own; shared types are
  promoted when many people's fields agree.
- Vesper recognizes what things are, and never reads the person.

**Collections are the spine** (ruled and recorded September 28):
- A thread is a collection read over time.
- Membership is many-to-many.
- Kept things are time-agnostic: been, ahead, loose or open.
- Any collection can be shared whole. A shared collection shows everything to
  everyone in it, and adding is sharing.
- Where things surface:
  - your own actions pay off in place;
  - quiet private organizing is silent, with Undo;
  - new structure goes to notifications;
  - other people's actions go to notifications;
  - insights and what matters now go to Home.

**One way in** (ruled and recorded September 28):
- Any thing through any door becomes the same artifact, and duplicates merge.
- Things arrive from three origins: you send it, Vesper finds it, or a friend
  sends it. Only the arrival differs.
- Email is a connected inbox, not forwarding.
- Entry should ask for almost nothing, and how much Vesper does on its own is
  the person's setting.

**Still open.** The MVP definition, the friction proposal, R1–R5, the October
4–7 policy items and the delivery path. See §6.

**The gap under all of it.** On September 26 the product map measured 75
capabilities:
- 61% built on `main`;
- 33% reachable in an installable build;
- about 0% of the work since mid-August delivered to a phone.

Production ran August 14 code. The direction is now much clearer than the
product people can use.
These are dated, inventory-based classifications, not percentages of product
completion or a fresh assessment of the current build.

### 1.1 September 28 follow-up: the record is worth having

These points are the founder's emphasis after the thread, reached with Codex.

- **The record is a benefit in itself.** Making it takes no effort: no
  writing, prompts or filing. But some things are worth having without a next
  action:
  - a map of the places you have been;
  - the dishes you loved and where each came from;
  - an evening with everyone's photographs.

  Memory includes what is ahead and still open. It is not a surveillance
  record.
- **The loop is a possible path, not a funnel.** A contribution can pay off in
  any of these ways:
  - it is kept and easy to find again;
  - a connection adds something new;
  - another person's view arrives;
  - a possibility appears at the right time.

  Keeping, refinding or enjoying something can complete the experience, and
  acting on it is optional. The first contribution has to pay off before any
  history exists.
- **Multiplayer adds substance, not just distribution.** A friend's
  photograph, or their account of an evening, changes what a collection lets
  people revisit. Each person keeps their own voice; Vesper does not rewrite
  them into one narrator. The shared-collection rules stand, and this grants no
  new AI use of a friend's material.
- **Compete on product shape, not capabilities.** Recognition, memory, booking
  and agent actions are becoming commodities. Every capability-led pitch in the
  thread collapsed into "sounds like Muse."
  - Invest in a capability only when the shape depends on it: the payoff in
    about two seconds, correct recognition, one identity across doors, and
    well-timed returns.
  - A shape is defensible only when it carries a stance Meta cannot adopt:
    people's words stay theirs, finite and quiet, you send, no feed, and no
    commissions.

**Working description** (proposed; it does not replace canonical positioning):

> Vesper turns what you document and share into a connected, living collection
> of your life—worth keeping and exploring in itself, and useful for what comes
> next.

**Implementation cautions** (not rulings):
- **Relating is not merging.** Only repeated captures of the same material
  merge, such as one ticket forwarded by email and also shared as a screenshot.
  - Two visits to the same restaurant stay two occurrences.
  - A friend's recommendation and your photograph of the same place stay two
    contributions.
  - Identity keeps source, authorship, occurrence and audience apart.
- **Collections are the consumer model, not a new owner of everything.** A
  ticket in three collections still has one reservation owner. The backend's
  existing `collections` table serves the editorial Discover guides
  (`travel-agent/backend/core/db/collections.py`), so personal collections
  need an owner.
- **Zero-touch types are a hypothesis.** R3 is pending. When recognition is
  uncertain, the thing needs a useful landing (*Not yet placed*) and an easy
  correction, not forced classification.
- **Product Thesis disagrees.** It still says "Memory is substrate, not the
  endpoint." Reconcile that deliberately; this note does not amend it.

### 1.2 What the research adds

The [six-question review](memory-as-product-direction-research-2026-09-28.md)
holds the full evidence. It supports the center as plausible, not as proven
demand.

- **The shape alone is taken.** Several products already offer
  self-organizing collections, private or shared: mymind, Are.na, Fabric, Day
  One's shared journals and Apple's Shared Photo Library.
  - Vesper's shape is the combination of four things:
    - the person's own material;
    - lived time and place;
    - other people's contributions, in their own words;
    - connections that change as circumstances do.
  - The combination has to show on one screen. The logo-off screenshot test
    checks for it.
- **The strongest argument against us.** In one study, people found at least
  95% of web pages again months later without having saved them (Keeping Found
  Things Found, 2004). Bringing something to Vesper must pay off at the first
  send. A "Kept" receipt alone is not value.
- **Originals and generated additions pass different tests.** A friend's exact
  words or your own photograph can be the whole value. An explanation from
  Vesper has to add something. Sometimes two attributed originals beat the
  originals plus an AI paragraph.
- **In multiplayer, persistence cuts both ways.** Lasting organization can make
  casual sharing feel less casual.
  - Measure the contributor's effort and the recipient's benefit separately.
  - Let recipients enjoy what they receive without reciprocating.
- **The architecture fits.** Intake, evidence and projection code already
  separates retries, occurrences, authorship and generated claims. Connect it
  and verify it end to end rather than restart.

**The design brief.** Three experiences in one grammar:
1. one contribution;
2. a small personal collection, including a sparse everyday one;
3. a shared collection.

Show each at first receipt, at a later voluntary return, and at a correction.
Offer practical continuation when it helps, never as the test every object must
pass.

The shape is fixed: what you bring, what you get to have, and where it lives.
Exploration happens inside it.

## 2. The thought process, phase by phase

### Phase 1 — Where are we? (September 24)

**Asked.** A deep, chat-only audit of both repositories and the docs. What was
done each day, and how the product is tracking against its vision, focusing on
the five weeks since the pivot. "We are currently in the process of
rebuilding."

**Found.**
- **The pivot returned to the April 12 vision.** The April 12 doc already said
  "Curiosity About Place Is Persistent, Not Occasional." It called travel "the
  entry point… not the ceiling." What changed:
  - the order of attack: everyday first, travel as a specialization;
  - the business model: per trip, commissions and a wallet became membership;
  - the philosophy: the agent that acts became the person who sends.
- **Scale.** About 14,400 commits since April 12 (an activity count, not a
  measure of work). About 42.5% of lines added since August 20 were docs, and
  84% in the workspace. There were 22 accepted decisions in five weeks and a
  5,700-line roadmap.
- **Nothing new could ship.** `travel-app/utils/productSystemRollout.ts`
  hard-codes `releaseEligible: false`. The four-root shell appears only in
  dev/internal builds with four flags set.
- **Other gaps:**
  - The Venture Path gate ("does a joiner on trip N become an organizer on trip
    N+1?") was never read before strategy moved on.
  - There were no real users, and PostHog was unset.
- **Strategy was declared closed and then reopened four times.** Audience, city,
  offer and price were never chosen, so nothing could fail.

**Concluded.** The vision was coherent. The constraint was merging, getting onto
a device and getting in front of people, not throughput. Six recommendations:
1. Get Home onto a device.
2. Land the recovery PRs.
3. Renew or retire the expiring flags.
4. Decide PearX explicitly.
5. Put the product in front of 5–10 real people.
6. Reconcile stale canon and cap agent lanes.

**What held.** The delivery diagnosis held all week. Two parts were revised
later: the rework claim and the "20%" estimate (Phases 4 and 5).

### Phase 2 — How do I explain it, and how is it different from Muse? (September 25)

**Pitch v1.**
- The lead line: "Vesper helps you actually do the things that catch your eye."
- A hook to test: "It's for everything after 'you have to try this place.'"
- It was a faithful translation of the canon into plain words.

**Founder:** "This doesnt' sound much different from Muse." Claude conceded that
the overlap was "real, not a wording problem." Meta's Muse turns saved reels into
plans, remembers you and books.

**Pitch v2** moved the center to what was hardest to copy in the code: private
input from several people shaping one shared result, and whose side Vesper is on.
- The line was "an AI for making plans with the people in your life… privately."
- It was code-verified:
  - `backend/concierge/privacy_redactor.py`;
  - `backend/core/public_egress.py` registers 16 guarded surfaces;
  - about 120 privacy tests.

**Founder:** "i feel like there's way more differences from a product design
standpoint." A review of ten Claude Design projects edited September 11–25 showed
that pitch v2 contradicted the founder's own recent designs:
- "Multiplayer is optional."
- "Three agreements are still not a booking."
- "Twice is not a habit."

Claude retracted v2 and named **eight design commitments**:
1. Your real things lead.
2. People's words stay theirs.
3. Evidence, not a profile.
4. Finite and allowed to be silent.
5. Vesper prepares and you send.
6. Honest about what is known.
7. A reading that depends on purpose.
8. Social without a feed or scoreboard.

Items 2, 4, 5 and 8 are structurally hard for Meta to copy.

**"Is it differentiated in product form?"** In the details, yes. In overall
shape, not yet. In what a user can use today, no. The missing piece was a
signature form, the way Beli has the ranked list and Partiful the invite link.
Claude proposed a friend's share as that form; this was superseded in Phase 3.

### Phase 3 — Life, sharing, "one app", and the center (evening of September 25)

**Life.**
- Rendering the boards overturned Claude's earlier comparison to Apple Journal.
  Life asks for no writing and builds from evidence already there. Five novel
  forms: episode pages, typed evidence, threads, people as shared records, and
  returns that add understanding.
- The weakness was supply. The demo persona has 312 items over seven years; a
  new user has a handful.

**The founder's sharing thesis:** make sharing effortless and consequential, let
AI connect things, and expand curiosity.
- Research found the founder's own policy blocked it:
  - The September 9 decision allows a friend's share only to be displayed.
  - Every mechanism that would let AI use it was an unadopted proposal expiring
    October 6–7.
- The reconciliation: the consequence happens "later and somewhere else, never
  as a line under the share."
- Sharing + artifacts + consequence is differentiated only as the core. As an
  extra feature, it reads as "Muse with a share button."

**"The magic is that it's one app."** Claude diagnosed the oscillation: each
attempt argued one part of the product against one competitor.
- The unifier it proposed: each thing keeps its identity as it moves, through
  time, between people and across screens.
- A nine-track research pass (about 600 features across ten projects) found
  "three products at once":
  - the canon describes an intelligence;
  - the designs are sharing-first;
  - the code is a trip planner with a concierge chat.
- Claude proposed the center as "a friend's own words about a place, carried
  inside a plan."

**Founder correction:** "this isn't the center btw… this is more of just one
version of the center / one expression about the center."
- Claude revised the center to **the experience**: a piece of life out in the
  world, across its whole arc from noticed to returned.
- This ties back to the August 9 decision ("experience as the human product
  unit") and the August 24 messaging decision.
- Founder: "i agree, it's actually kind of an experience."
- The resulting layers:
  - attention is how it enters;
  - the experience is the center;
  - the relationship is what builds up.

  Claude's reading was that the founder had been zooming, not oscillating.

### Phase 4 — Why is this hard to say, and have we built the right things? (September 26, daytime)

**Why it is hard to explain.** Nine reasons, among them:
- the center is an arc, not an object;
- the value arrives over time;
- many of the differences are refusals;
- the vocabulary was built to govern, not to explain;
- nobody outside has heard it;
- the company sentence changed about nine times in seven weeks.

The fix: separate *what it is* from *how you first meet it*.

**The philosophy.** "The World Is Lived, Not Searched" (August 18, 1,853 lines;
`travel-agent/docs/working/`, expired September 17, never canon) grounded the
experience framing. It added five refinements:
1. interpretation belongs in the arc;
2. most arcs should stop early;
3. a shared place is a plural experience;
4. the relationship must be allowed to forget;
5. reciprocity with places.

It also resolved a standing tension: "Sharing is the foundation of multiplayer,
not of meaning."

**Is it a new category?** In substance yes, but lead with a familiar behavior.
April Dunford warns against category creation. The August 24 decision says "the
category should follow a concrete behavior." "Personal Intelligence" is
already Google's product name.

**Engineering.** Claude's first answer: parallel agent lanes caused rework.
- **Founder:** "is this really true?… most of the work landed though right?"
- Claude measured unique unlanded changes:
  - backend: 299, of which 28% of added lines were on `main` as-is;
  - app: 236, of which 45% were.

  The remaining 36–45% was rewritten or dropped.
- The corrected diagnosis is **not merging continuously**. `main` received
  nothing from September 6 to 22.

**Sequencing.** Claude first said "narrow first, widen later."
- **Founder:** "Do you really think narrow first, widen later is the way to go?"
- Revised to **whole but thin, not narrow**: every stage of the loop, each thin,
  ladder-wise:
  1. a friend's place;
  2. your own thing;
  3. an evening together;
  4. a question that stays.
- This matches the Product Thesis's "end-to-end slices."

**The current roadmap** had been cut from 5,694 lines to 217, with one lane.
Claude judged it mostly right, with four adjustments:
1. Give H1 design references.
2. Replace synthetic evidence with a founder dogfood profile and PostHog.
3. Rescue NYC supply.
4. Put the keystone decisions on the queue.

### Phase 6 — The verb (evening of September 26, parallel branch)

**Founder:** the thesis still isn't obvious; "perhaps it's the interaction mode
with just another personal agent. maybe the primary mode is to share."
- Claude: clear products have one verb. ChatGPT and Muse ask, Instagram posts,
  Pinterest saves, Beli ranks. Vesper's de facto verb had been "ask," which is
  why it read as another agent.
- Proposed **send**, in three parts: the thing, who it is for, and an optional
  question.

**Founder:** "i want to avoid this as a diary… but the default… audience should
be me."
- Claude's line was "a diary is for documenting; Vesper is for using."
- Superseded two hours later, when the founder made *documenting* the gesture;
  see Phase 7.
- The diary guardrails survived: no writing, no prompts, no streaks, and
  organized by place, person and thread rather than date.

**Life page review.**
- Board 06, "Give once, get value back" (the pasta chain), is the thesis in one
  board.
- The diary risks: a time-first default lens, system-written meaning, and an
  archive persona.
- D0 votes: 1A 2A 3A 4A 5A 6A 7B. Superseded on September 27, when row 1 became
  peripheral.

**"Is this easier to explain?"** Yes, because there is a verb now: "You send
Vesper the things life hands you, and they come back when they matter."

**Founder's layer stack** (see §1). Claude kept it and adjusted two words:
"personal AI" rather than "agent" in consumer copy, since "agent" is Muse's
frame; and "standardized by artifacts" as the engineering spine.

**Founder:** "maximum immediate satisfaction from sharing."
- An audit of `travel-app/app/share-capture/index.tsx` and
  `ios/ShareExtension/ShareViewController.swift` found friction at every step:
  - the extension redirects out of the source app;
  - process copy and a disclaimer;
  - a review queue;
  - keeping created no saved place.
- The verdict: the caution is "correct in the backend and wrong on the screen."
- Targets: two taps, never leave the app, recognition in seconds, one specific
  true thing shown.

**Founder:** "because we share… the agent can do things based on those things,
plans, itinerary."
- Claude: "Sharing isn't storage. It's the fuel."
- Point the existing planning engine at shares; keep booking out.
- Research found **planning from what you've already shared unowned** in the
  market. Loose plans beat timed ones (Tonietto & Malkoc, 2016). Friends'
  places and names may be used, but not their words.
- It also surfaced a consent bug: the newer composition path ignores
  `inference="none"`.

### Phase 7 — Documenting, breadth, the composer, the MVP and the catalog (night of September 26 → September 27)

**Founder:** "it's not just about saving things… documenting -> standard
arficact -> organization -> agentic actions… accumulating artifacts about your
life, and the AI connecting dots… track different threads of your life, how do
they interact."
- Claude conceded it had narrowed the idea to save-to-plan.
- Saving is a wish; documenting is evidence. That makes the bookmark research
  (people rarely refind bookmarks) stop being a threat.
- The founder's own canon fenced the idea in five places:
  - no single artifact contract;
  - no model of threads meeting;
  - "a thread does not owe an action";
  - a "the record noticed" voice;
  - "asking keeps nothing."

**"Does it make sense?"** Yes, as a destination, with five concerns:
1. the magic arrives late, so the first send must pay off on its own;
2. there is no crisp question;
3. the intersection yield is unknown;
4. the growth loop is undefined;
5. every stage must work, so whole but thin.

Claude proposed a two-week test on the founder's own last 90 days.

**Founder:** Vesper's advantage "is exactly that it's more than a where should
we eat… with friends, trips, family on trips."
- The resulting line: "Beli answers 'what's good?' Vesper answers 'what's right
  for this?'"
- Day-one value comes from the situation plus friends' picks.

**September 27 morning.** The founder set the core loop: "documenting, for
either myself, or for my family or friends."
- **Founder:** "natural synergy between this and multiplayer."
  - Claude: it is the same act with a different audience.
  - The audience dial: just me → a person → a group or family → Friends.
  - A share = artifact(s) + intent + audience.
  - Document now, share later.
- **Founder:** consolidate sharing, broadcasting and documenting; Chat as an
  entrance; a horizontal capability.
  - Claude: one motion does not mean one entrance. **One composer, many
    doors**, with the share sheet primary and Chat as a door.
  - Audience is the only real choice at send, and the button names the
    consequence.
- **Founder:** "write it into the amendment decision" → the September 27
  decision.

**Tactics.**
- The biggest unknown is architectural. Capture ran on a once-a-minute pipeline;
  an instant payoff needs synchronous recognition in under about two seconds,
  and that is unmeasured.
- Four tracks: a ruling ballot, evidence (own-data test, a latency spike, an iOS
  extension spike), design, and composer v0.

**The MVP proposal** (Proposed; the founder did not answer its four questions):
- **One-liner:** "Send Vesper what life hands you — a place, a book, a film, a
  ticket, a friend's rec. It keeps each one, connects it to the rest of your
  life, and brings it back when it's right."
- **First user:** the connector, the friend everyone asks where to eat, plus
  their friends.
- **Three bars** (placeholders for the founder):
  - about three unprompted sends a week in weeks 3–4;
  - something sent comes back useful about once a week;
  - half of testers send to someone, and about a third of recipients keep or
    add.
- **Scope:** whole but thin, everyday going-out in NYC. Trips, booking, Atlas
  and Discover are hidden, not deleted.
- **The 60-second demo is the acceptance test:**
  1. share a reel;
  2. it is kept, and a friend had sent it too;
  3. a Friday offer;
  4. send it to the friend;
  5. they add one thing through a guest link.
- **Waves:**
  - wave 0: rulings, spikes, own-data test, an MVP build profile;
  - wave 1: send and keep;
  - wave 2: comes back and spreads;
  - then a cohort of 20–30 people for four weeks;
  - wave 3 decided by the results.
- **Mid-November** is a goal to test against, not an estimate.

**Founder:** "plans still restrict ourselves… how baout books? movies?"
- The loop works across kinds: a book a friend mentioned comes back at the
  bookshop, a film when it screens nearby.
- Don't compete with Letterboxd.

**Founder:** "an ever extending catalog… golf courses, scorecards, baseball
cards… standardized artifacts that can hold meanings and help system infer
meanings." Claude proposed the **open catalog**:
- Eight type layers:
  1. identity;
  2. the record;
  3. type fields;
  4. your meaning;
  5. evidence rules;
  6. relations;
  7. canonical shape;

**The founder asked about the technical standpoint.** The model has two kinds

3. resolve:
   1. your own cache;

## 3. What the trace shows

**The founder kept widening; Claude kept narrowing.** Almost every correction
the founder made undid a narrowing Claude had introduced to make the idea
demonstrable:

The stable direction is breadth with very low friction. A narrow slice is
acceptable as a *build* order (whole but thin), never as the *definition*.

**The work moved upstream.** The order was: surfaces (Home, Life) → the object
(artifacts, catalog) → the container (collections) → how Vesper's work surfaces

challenged:
- "20% built" became 61/33/≈0.
- "Lanes caused rework" became "not merging continuously."
- "Narrow first" became "whole but thin."

The product map and the unlanded-code measurement changed the plan more than any
argument did.

**The constant is delivery.** Every phase ended at the same place: the designs
and decisions are ahead of what anyone can use. That was true on September 24
and was still true on September 28.

### Other assistants and the market

**Other assistants:**
- OpenAI: group chats closed to new use after July 9, 2026, and Pulse was
  sunset in June; its lesson was that proactivity must be "steerable by the
  user." Agent mode was reportedly retired in August (secondary source).
- Google's "Personal Intelligence" launched in January 2026 and reached Ask Maps
  in August. In a test, 22 of 30 Ask Maps plans had errors.
- Only 32% of AI users let AI act without final approval (Menlo, 2026).
- Perplexity dropped ads because users "would just start doubting everything."

**Commodities and booking consolidation:**
- Extracting places from reels and screenshots is commoditized in at least eight
  apps (Mindtrip, Airial, Plotline, Rhyme, Wanderlog, Google Maps and others),

- Money is moving to booking commissions:
  - Layla was bought by Expedia (July 31);
  - Mindtrip sells flights;

  - Muse plans a transaction fee.

**Social and restaurant apps:**
- DoorDash's Zesty shut after five months.

### Behavior research (as relayed; several items are vendor surveys)

- **Saving and memory:**
  - about 16% of bookmarked pages are refound through the bookmark;
  - taking a screenshot weakens memory of its contents;
  - Pocket shut in July 2025.
- **Plans and follow-through:**
  - scheduling leisure makes it feel like work (Tonietto & Malkoc, 2016), so
    plans should stay loose;
  - implementation intentions raise follow-through (Gollwitzer & Sheeran);
  - deadlines raise redemption (gift certificates: 31% vs 6%).
- **Social proof:** naming a friend raises uptake (a Facebook study of 23M
  users).
- **Attitudes to AI:** AI picks for leisure can feel "eerie." About 7–12% would
  let AI book on its own, against about 43% with final approval.
- **Retention:** mood and journaling apps keep about 3% of users at day 30.
- **Planning accuracy:** GPT-4 produced a valid constrained itinerary 0.6% of
  the time, and over 90% with a solver.
- **Unknown:** no credible data exists on how often saved places are visited.
