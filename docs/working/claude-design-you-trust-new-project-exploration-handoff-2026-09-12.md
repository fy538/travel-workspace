# New Claude Design project — Vesper You, Identity & Trust

## 1. Assignment: explore a new experience, not an old portrait patch

Create a **new Claude Design project**. Suggested working name:
**Vesper — You, Identity & Trust**. These are design responsibilities, not a
required consumer title, three tabs or three separate screens. Explore the
language and arrangement that make sense to a person.

The founder wants visual exploration before convergence. Do not assume the
answer is a compact profile, a settings-first directory, the old long portrait
or a new “relationship with AI” dashboard. Draw materially different ways of
making the experience personal, understandable and effortless. The scenarios
below describe human needs, not a preselected section list.

**Design question:** How does a person recognize themselves, choose how they
appear to others, understand how Vesper helps, and change that relationship—
without constructing a profile or maintaining the app's model of their life?

This is not a fifth root. Home / Chat / Places / Life remain the product's
orientations. Explore how a personal/control destination is reached and how
it relates to them. No production code, route deletion or policy changes are
authorized by these drawings.

## 3. Three human needs—not three mandatory UI sections

### A. Recognition and chosen expression

“This is me, and this is what I choose to show.” A name, face, authored line,
selected artifact or temporary status can feel personal without becoming a
public performance. A person may choose to say they have returned from Nice
and Sorrento, or share a dish or place. Vesper can help prepare that expression;
the person chooses its meaning and audience.

Explore what identity adds beyond Life's record and Social's individual
contributions. A rich personal page is not prohibited, but it must have a clear
human purpose rather than merely aggregate every available field. A compact
page is not automatically better if it feels anonymous or hides useful doors.

### B. A legible, adjustable relationship with Vesper

“Help me in ways I actually want.” Someone may love learning about cooking but
dislike long explanations; enjoy choosing restaurants but want logistics taken
care of; want familiar possibilities today despite usually exploring.

Design how the person recognizes and steers that relationship without filling
out a personality inventory. They should be able to understand relevant context,
correct its subject or use, and find the effect of a change. Most visits need
not inspect the model at all. Trust is not a compulsory dashboard activity.

### C. Dependable, ordinary control

“I can change this, stop it or leave.” Account, connections, notifications,
privacy, retained material and accepted assistance need clear access. Ordinary
controls can use familiar patterns. Beauty should not make these errands harder,
and fear-driven warning copy should not dominate an otherwise helpful product.

## 4. What stays true, and what is open

### Product commitments

- Life owns the organized record; Home current value; Places spatial discovery;
  Chat expression and agency. A personal view may link or preview, but it must
  not create a competing record or ownership system.
- A public or friend-facing identity is deliberately authored/selected. Private
  context is not automatically public profile material. No inferred personality,
  compatibility score, popularity score or passive-view report as the benefit.
- Desired assistance, retention, inference, audience, notifications and action
  are different choices. A friendly “personalize me” switch cannot grant them all.
- Correction and exit are part of the product, not premium features. Receiving
  a friend's contribution does not require equivalent contribution or payment.
- The product should remain useful with no friends, little context and long
  absences. Do not turn empty sections into instructions to document more life.

### Open for visual and information-architecture exploration

- Whether the initial personal destination emphasizes identity, service
  relationship, control, or a deliberate balance. No compact/default winner.
- Whether profile and settings are separate pages, a compact hierarchy, or
  connected through another understandable pattern. Do not force one mega-page.
- How much of the person's record, authored status or chosen places should be
  previewed, and what makes that different from simply opening Life.
- Where a returning person goes for a quick setting versus a first visit to
  understand the service; what the root avatar/gear/title should do.
- Personal visual character: authored words, photography, avatar treatment,
  typography, meaningful space, groups/rows/cards and quieter control density.
- Whether wanted help is easiest to adjust conversationally, directly, or through
  both methods arriving at the same owner. Neither forms nor Chat should be mandatory.
- How relevant context/use becomes inspectable without exposing a raw fact ledger.
  Visual clarity may be a short example, a source preview or another treatment;
  do not assume a graph is necessary because the backend contains relationships.

Use the new Vesper family as a reference, not a rigid template. Explore expressive
composition without designing a second token library. Label intentional shared-
language changes and compare them beside the accepted surroundings before adoption.

## 5. Round 1 — three visual directions, not a settings checklist

Choose and name **three genuinely different directions**. At least one should
question the old portrait's organization; none must adopt our earlier compact-
identity recommendation. Differences should affect how the experience works
and feels, not just color or corner radius. Hold available facts constant.

Show the same four moments in each direction:

1. **The personal entrance:** what someone sees after opening this destination
   from one of the four roots. Include its natural full scroll, not only a hero.
2. **A deliberate public/friend-facing expression:** preview what others can see
   and make one small change. This may reuse Social rather than become a separate
   publishing workflow. Keep the audience evident without policy jargon.
3. **A quick service adjustment:** “Help with logistics, but let me choose,” or
   changing notification behavior. Show the actual result and a clear return.
4. **Little context:** the same direction with almost no record or connections.
   It should still feel complete and usable, not deficient or homework-shaped.

Use one fictional, clearly labeled fixture person across all directions: a
recent return to NYC, selected travel artifacts, a cooking question, one shared
friend note and an upcoming loose weekend. Richer context is available, but no
design should display all of it merely to prove the system knows it. Use a
separate sparse fixture for moment 4; do not fabricate its history.

Provide one cross-root inset showing the distinction from Life or Social, and
one narrow/larger-text spot check. Include concrete content, not generic blocks
labeled “personalization” or “trust.” State your recommendation and tradeoffs.

**Stop for founder selection.** Do not draw every account leaf, settle retention
duration, migrate production or build a full safety-state atlas before the
visual/structural direction is chosen. Exploration is not adoption.

## 6. Round 2 — pressure-test the selected direction with real human jobs

These are coverage obligations for the selected experience, not a requirement
for one screen per row. Reuse established controls and contextual doors.

| Human job | The experience must make clear | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “What do my friends see?” | Exact chosen material and audience; edit/preview/leave; private account and public identity remain distinguishable | AI-written biography presented as personal truth; counts as identity; a required polished post |
| “That was for my parents, not me.” | Correct the subject/context and dependent uses while preserving legitimate source material | Replacing one false standing preference with another, or temporary dismissal that lets the error return |
| “I like this topic, but these are too long.” | Change depth/presentation in the intended scope without rejecting the topic | Silently training a permanent preference from one temporary adjustment |
| “Keep this, but don't use it for recommendations.” | Held material and future influence are separable where supported; show the effective scope/result | Equating save, personalization and sharing |
| “Why did you use this here?” | Relevant evidence and influence, with a useful correction path and contextual return | An unexplained list of inferred traits or a raw event/audit log as the default answer |
| “What can that connection access?” | Supported purpose/scope, last usable state, permission loss and disconnect consequences | A speculative connector catalog or full-inbox/camera-roll import requirement |
| “Are you still helping with Saturday?” | Accepted scope, what is current, expiry/coverage loss and a reachable stop where supported | Turning a question, Keep or payment into a watch; an internal jobs dashboard |
| “I need fewer interruptions.” | App preference versus actual device permission; fallback and return | Notification permission treated as permission to act, or repeated denied-permission nags |
| “I don't want this contact/occasion anymore.” | Existing supported mute/block/leave access and actual effects, coordinated with Social | Inventing social policy or implying an unanswered invitation is an acceptance |
| “I want my material or account back/out.” | Account recovery, sign-out and supported export/deletion scope, confirmation, pending/failure and completion | Memory held hostage, vague success, or promises to delete another person's independent material |

For temporal continuity, use a small day-1/day-2/day-5 example: the person supplies
evidence, later explains it, then adds something related. Let them see or correct
the relevant relationship when needed without maintaining an evolving dossier.
An added explanation can change interpretation without proving an enduring trait.

Show both contextual and global access where they solve different errands. Life
can offer repair beside an object; You & Trust can offer the complete control.
Do not force the person to leave the object just because the control has a global owner.

## 7. Explicit policy boundaries for exploratory designs

The September 6–7 strategy accepts **optional ordinary-conversation continuity
as direction**, not a retention agreement. Explore how an understandable
agreement might feel, but label it proposed. Its material, duration, sensitive/
third-party exclusions and allowed uses must be decided separately. Include
a current-policy case; no “remember everything” default or silent backfill.

Do not treat this unresolved agreement as a ban on designing wanted continuity,
nor let an attractive toggle pretend it has been adopted. You & Trust and Chat
must show the same meaning for history, held source and future influence.

Connected services begin narrowly: selected material or invoked retrieval, not
ambient access to a person's entire digital life. Show actual supported examples;
otherwise mark the adapter as proposed. Disconnecting must not imply a made-up
deletion policy or invisible continued access.

Finite commissioned work and ongoing following are distinct. Only show an
effective service as active when its actual mandate exists. Commercial direction
is consumer-funded depth/capacity/assistance, but price and limits remain open.
Membership/account placements can be reserved; Round 1 needs no invented paywall,
credits, free-trial countdown or spending tier. Payment grants no new data/action
authority. No booking checkout, wallet or operational expense product is requested.

Use short, human explanations at meaningful boundaries. Board annotations can
carry detailed policy notes; ordinary screens should not read like contracts.

not failure to reuse it. Do not redesign those neighboring projects during this
exploration; name the receiving owner and any recommendation for later work.
