# Vesper Experience Constitution and Interaction Grammar

> Status: working proposal
>
> This document does not yet supersede canonical Product Thesis, Product Model,
> or Surfacing Strategy. It compiles their product implications into a design
> constitution and tests a four-root shell: Home, Vesper, Places, and Life.
> Canon changes require founder approval after behavioral testing.

## 1. The experience promise

Vesper helps people and the people in their life build a richer relationship
with places. The interface should make the world more legible, the day lighter,
and shared experience more coherent without turning the app into the experience.

The desired sequence is:

```text
I can bring this anything
  -> it understands what kind of moment this is
  -> it gives me something useful immediately
  -> it shows any proposed consequence plainly
  -> the physical experience becomes better or richer
  -> later, I can feel that it remembered correctly
```

The interface is successful when a person becomes more able to notice,
understand, navigate, decide, relate, remember, or imagine—not when they spend
more time operating Vesper.

## 2. Constitutional principles

These principles are decision rules, not brand copy.

### 2.1 Enhance the world, not capture the person

- Begin from the person's attention, situation, or intention.
- Return attention to the physical world and the people present.
- Never manufacture engagement through streaks, completion scores, generic
  feeds, or mandatory reflection.
- A practical answer that expires can be a complete success.

### 2.2 Accept before organizing

- The person may start with a question, photograph, screenshot, ticket, voice
  fragment, link, invitation, reservation, location, or vague intention.
- Do not ask them to classify the input, choose a workflow, or construct context
  before receiving value.
- Vesper owns classification and contextual assembly; ambiguity produces a
  bounded clarification only when it changes a material consequence.

### 2.3 Infer generously; assert conservatively

- Use inference to assemble possibilities, not to author identity or fact.
- A menu photograph supports attention and a question; it does not prove a
  restaurant visit, meal, preference, or meaning.
- Make truth states legible wherever a mistaken implication could change trust,
  memory, sharing, or action.

### 2.4 Show consequences, not machinery

- The visible unit is what became useful, changed, remained open, or was kept.
- Do not expose agent plans, database taxonomies, retrieval traces, confidence
  dashboards, or infrastructure as the main experience.
- Every durable change must leave a compact receipt with scope, owner, reason,
  and correction or undo.

### 2.5 One dominant thing per surface

- Every screen or state answers one primary question.
- Rich roots may compose several jobs, but they must still establish a clear
  attention order.
- Nested surfaces own the smallest durable truth or consequential action.
- A card pile is not information architecture.

### 2.6 Proactivity must earn interruption

- Compare every intervention with silence.
- Use the quietest surface that preserves the opportunity.
- “Why this person, place, moment, and action?” must be answerable.
- Most intelligence should first earn a place on Home before earning push.

### 2.7 Personalization must preserve becoming

- Treat prior behavior as revisable evidence, not a permanent trait.
- Use known interests as bridges into adjacent curiosity, not borders around a
  taste profile.
- Make correction, dormancy, forgetting, and reset easy to find.
- Prefer “this prior outcome changed this suggestion” over “you are the kind of
  person who…”

### 2.8 Shared experience remains plural

- Private understanding may improve a shared outcome without becoming shared
  evidence.
- A group's decision, each person's memory, and a public Place contribution
  remain distinct projections.
- Preserve human voice and bounded attribution; do not synthesize people into
  one group personality.

### 2.9 Screens own durable truth; conversation owns movement

- Vesper shapes intent, interprets, proposes, negotiates, reflects, and recovers.
- Structured surfaces own objects that must be scanned, compared, controlled,
  or revisited.
- Chat artifacts are compact interfaces into durable objects, not miniature
  replacement screens.

### 2.10 Reversibility creates freedom

- Prefer proposals, previews, undo, and origin-preserving Back.
- Make high-impact operations explicit and low-risk exploration immediate.
- Never trap the person inside a forced flow or make recovery cost their work.

## 3. Intended feeling

The product should feel:

- **Immediate** — bring what already has attention; no preparatory homework.
- **Oriented** — what is true, what matters, what can happen, and where the
  consequence lives are apparent.
- **Recognizing** — prior outcomes improve judgment without announcing a profile.
- **Holding** — Vesper carries context and open loops without making the person
  maintain a life database.
- **Quietly surprising** — occasional openings reveal something worth noticing;
  silence remains normal.
- **Consequential** — interaction can visibly change perception, action,
  coordination, or later judgment.
- **Socially safe** — private understanding stays private; shared action remains
  coherent.
- **World-facing** — places and people, not interface ornament, remain central.

The product should not feel like a dashboard, content feed, travel spreadsheet,
scrapbook, social performance, map directory, or chatbot with decorative cards.

## 4. Four-root shell under test

The roots represent stable modes of attention, not exclusive ownership of every
object. Plans, Places, people, Encounters, and artifacts may be entered from
several roots while retaining one canonical owner.

| Root | Human question | Owns | Must not become |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Home** | What matters now? | Current openings, active occasions, invitations, returns, and intentional quiet | Generic feed, chat inbox, or trip list |
| **Vesper** | What can I ask, show, or work through? | Universal input, private and group conversation, interpretation, proposals, and compact receipts | Only route into the app or owner of all durable truth |
| **Places** | What is around me, what is this place, and how can I experience it? | Spatial exploration, Place identity, terrain, conditions, perspectives, familiarity, and horizons | Generic map inventory or ratings directory |
| **Life** | What is accumulating for me and between us? | Encounters, artifacts, threads, occasions, patterns, people, shared handoffs, correction, forgetting, and privacy | Static profile, archive dump, brag feed, or universal control panel |

### 4.1 Plans and occasions

Plans and occasions are durable shared objects, not necessarily a fifth root.
They must be reachable from:

- Home through current and upcoming occasions;
- Vesper through creation, discussion, and proposals;
- Places through spatial context;
- Life through remembered and shared continuity;
- notifications and direct links when the object is the destination.

Home must include an obvious “All plans and occasions” affordance before the
existing Plans root can be removed.

### 4.2 Life scopes

Life should begin with two legible scopes:

- **Mine** — Encounters, artifacts, occasions, threads, emerging patterns, and
  private controls.
- **Together** — people and circles, shared occasions, perspective handoffs,
  shared artifacts, and plural outcomes.

Public or local participation belongs primarily to Places. Settings and account
administration remain accessible from the avatar or a dedicated settings route;
they do not define Life.

### 4.3 Cross-root transition law

- Global object details open over the current root and preserve origin.
- Back returns to the origin root and its prior state.
- “Open in Places,” “Open in Life,” or “Open occasion” explicitly changes the
  canonical workspace.
- Deep links may enter the canonical owner directly.
- A complete task should not require manual root switching merely to reconstruct
  context already known by Vesper.

## 5. Core interaction grammar

### 5.1 Inputs

```text
Ask · Point · Import · Invite · Join · Choose · Decline · Correct · Confirm
```

The default entry requires only **Ask** or **Point**.

### 5.2 Bounded outputs

| Output | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Answer** | Useful now; no retention required |
| **Thread** | A revisable question or connection worth returning to |
| **Opening** | A current possibility for attention, action, or relationship |
| **Move** | One low-commitment, expiring action |
| **Plan / Occasion** | Minimum durable structure needed for commitment or coordination |
| **Decision** | Governed shared choice with authority and state |
| **Encounter** | Sparse private-first joining of Place, moment, trace, and optional meaning |
| **Receipt** | What changed, why, under what scope, and how to reverse it |
| **Silence** | No treatment earned |

### 5.3 Human verbs

```text
Ask · Point · Continue · Keep · Mark · Connect · Share · Contribute · Correct · Release
```

- Ask and Point must be immediately useful.
- Keep authorizes a private consequence; it does not imply sharing.
- Share creates one audience projection; it does not broaden memory authority.
- Contribute creates a separately governed public projection.
- Release includes dormancy, removal from inference, forgetting, and deletion.

### 5.4 Vesper verbs

```text
Answer · Situate · Interpret · Compress · Propose · Route · Recall · Act · Abstain · Withdraw
```

Vesper may not silently convert:

- attention into occurrence;
- occurrence into preference;
- private meaning into group context;
- group visibility into consensus;
- a human perspective into universal Place truth;
- a suggestion into a commitment.

## 6. Lifecycle and consequence boundary

```text
attention trace
  -> immediate answer
  -> expire

attention trace
  -> immediate answer
  -> proposed consequence
  -> explicit or previously authorized boundary
  -> durable owner
  -> visible receipt
  -> later opening or dormancy
```

The default path may end after the answer. Preservation is earned, not required.

### 6.1 Truth ladder

The interface must distinguish:

```text
noticed
asked about
considered
intended
planned
shared
occurred or visited
liked or disliked
personally meaningful
publicly contributed
```

No state implies the next.

### 6.2 Five permission axes

Every consequential projection resolves separately:

1. **Use** — may Vesper use this evidence now?
2. **Retention** — may raw material, derived interpretation, or both persist?
3. **Inference** — may it affect later judgment, and within what scope?
4. **Audience** — who can see which fields and whose voice?
5. **Action** — may it change a Plan, route, push, handoff, or public layer?

Do not compress these into a private/shared toggle.

## 7. The canonical interaction sentence

Every intelligent feature must be expressible as:

```text
Because [evidence] entered [person/group]'s attention
in [Place + Moment + optional Occasion],
Vesper [answer/situate/propose/recall/abstain]
through [attributable perspective or operational truth],
then [expire/preserve/connect/act/withdraw]
under [scope + authority],
leaving [receipt or silence].
```

If the team cannot write this sentence, the feature is not ready for interface
design.

## 8. AI behavior matrix

Every prototype and implementation covers four timescales.

| Moment | Required design behavior |
|---|---|
| **Initially** | Demonstrate one low-risk useful act; communicate capability and meaningful limits through use, not a feature tour |
| **During interaction** | Use current Place, task, attention, relationship, and occasion to choose relevant content and timing |
| **When wrong** | Support immediate dismissal, correction, scoping, recovery, and an inspectable reason |
| **Over time** | Show what was retained, how feedback changed future behavior, cautious adaptation, global controls, and the ability to reset or forget |

Every AI state includes:

```text
belief or proposal
evidence and truth mode
why now
primary action
dismissal
correction
retained consequence
audience
expiry
```

## 9. Proactivity ladder

```text
silence
  -> available in canonical object
  -> Home opening
  -> passive notification
  -> active notification
  -> time-sensitive interruption
  -> bounded action with review or undo
```

Admission questions:

1. Why this person or group?
2. Why this Place or occasion?
3. Why now?
4. What can happen next?
5. What evidence and authority support it?
6. Is likely value greater than attention, privacy, and social cost?
7. Why is silence worse?

Time-sensitive interruption is reserved for near-term material consequences,
not recommendations, curiosity, or engagement.

## 10. Surface and component grammar

### 10.1 Roots

- **Home:** editorial composition with one dominant current opening, bounded
  occasion state, invitations, and quiet continuation.
- **Vesper:** open composer, camera/import affordances, private and group
  conversations, typed artifacts, and immediate receipts.
- **Places:** map and place interpretation, spatial feasibility, familiarity,
  nearby horizons, and attributable human perspectives.
- **Life:** longitudinal Mine/Together composition, not a reverse-chronological
  event log.

### 10.2 Nested surfaces

Use a route-level screen only when an object:

- is durable and revisited outside its creation moment;
- requires spatial, chronological, financial, or comparative scanning;
- owns consequential, legal, permission, privacy, or irreversible work; or
- is a substantial reading artifact.

Use conversation for interpretation, recommendation, negotiation, proposal,
reflection, short status, and recovery. Use a sheet for focused editing,
comparison, provenance, uncertainty, and confirmation.

### 10.3 Initial primitives

The first design system should cover:

- app shell and four-root tab bar;
- page header and contextual status line;
- universal composer and capture/import controls;
- opening;
- typed chat artifact;
- consequence receipt;
- source artifact;
- Place opening and relationship state;
- occasion summary and shared decision;
- person or perspective handoff;
- evidence and truth disclosure;
- correction, dismissal, undo, and release;
- intentional quiet and empty state;
- global object detail overlay.

Do not build a component for every content type before these primitives prove
their ability to compose the canonical loops.

## 11. Visual constitution

### 11.1 Compositional rules

- One dominant object or proposition per screen.
- Use hierarchy and whitespace before containers.
- Cards are bounded interactive objects, not the default layout primitive.
- Original material—photographs, ticket typography, route lines, handwriting,
  menu texture—may provide character when provenance remains inspectable.
- Beauty should emerge from Place, movement, time, people, planned-versus-lived
  divergence, and graceful sparsity.
- Avoid generic AI gradients, excessive pills, status-chip confetti, miniature
  dashboards, and ornamental maps.

### 11.2 Three directions to test

1. **Editorial Field** — quiet, spatial, photographic, and typographic; the
   current opening or artifact dominates.
2. **Living Correspondence** — an ongoing exchange among person, people, and
   Place; conversational without becoming a generic chat transcript.
3. **Personal World Instrument** — relationships among Place, traces, occasions,
   people, and time become spatially inspectable without exposing machinery.

The expected synthesis is not an equal blend:

- Editorial Field supplies the default calm and hierarchy.
- Living Correspondence supplies the interaction model.
- Personal World Instrument supplies selected Places and Life inspection modes.

### 11.3 Native foundation

- Use platform navigation, safe-area, gesture, type scaling, focus, haptic, and
  accessibility conventions.
- Customize content composition and semantic objects rather than rebuilding
  every control.
- Keep labels, controls, and state changes legible outdoors, in motion, and
  under divided attention.

## 12. Representative design loop

All visual directions must render the same Sorrento menu loop:

1. **Point:** photograph a menu and ask for translation.
2. **Immediate value:** translate the dish and explain why the pasta texture is
   different without requiring preservation.
3. **Consequence:** offer to keep one private Encounter; state that a menu photo
   is not proof of a restaurant visit.
4. **Return:** later surface an earned opening connecting the Encounter to an
   adjacent cooking technique or Place relationship.
5. **Social extension:** optionally share the human observation with a named
   person without broadening private memory authority.

The loop must also render expire, dismiss, correct, and silence.

## 13. Accessibility and situational usability

- Meet WCAG contrast and non-color state communication requirements.
- Prefer at least 44×44 point targets for consequential mobile actions.
- Support Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, Reduce Motion, high contrast, and one-handed
  operation.
- Test in bright sun, noisy transit, walking interruption, poor connectivity,
  and foreground location loss—not only in a desktop design tool.
- Keep notification previews free of sensitive personal or relational meaning.

## 14. Design critique rubric

Score every meaningful prototype against these questions:

### Purpose

- What human situation is improved?
- Is the immediate value visible before contribution?
- Does the product deepen the encounter or become the encounter?

### Orientation

- What is the dominant thing?
- What is true versus inferred?
- What can happen next?
- Where will the consequence live?

### Agency and trust

- Can the person dismiss, correct, undo, release, or reset?
- Is the consequence proportional to the evidence?
- Are audience and authority legible?

### Composition

- Can anything be removed without losing the job?
- Is a card being used where hierarchy or whitespace would be stronger?
- Is the screen expressing a durable object or duplicating another owner?

### Continuity

- What becomes useful later?
- Is the retained consequence inspectable?
- Would a second occasion be materially better?

### Social safety

- Whose voice is this?
- Who may see it?
- Does shared action preserve distinct personal meanings?

## 15. Research and validation program

Use the lowest fidelity that answers the current question:

| Question | Prototype |
|---|---|
| Where does this state belong? | State sort and tree test |
| Can the person predict the route and Back behavior? | Clickable IA prototype |
| Which visual philosophy expresses Vesper? | Same-loop high-fidelity comparison |
| Does AI timing, correction, and consequence feel trustworthy? | Wizard-of-Oz or behaviorally realistic prototype |
| Does the relationship compound? | Multi-occasion longitudinal dogfood |
| Does the implementation survive real conditions? | Narrow React Native production prototype |

Run small recurring rounds rather than one conclusive study. Record first
choice, time, confidence, wrong destinations, manual root switches, privacy
mistakes, expected Back destination, correction success, and where the person
expects the object to live tomorrow.

## 16. Governance

- The founder owns the product philosophy and final product judgment.
- One named design owner owns composition, visual language, interaction
  consistency, and critique resolution.
- Engineering implements from behavioral and visual contracts and surfaces
  technical constraints early.
- Agents may generate variants, trace states, implement components, and verify
  contracts. They do not independently invent the product language screen by
  screen.
- Every approved pattern joins one shared library with examples, negative
  examples, accessibility states, and code ownership.

## 17. Acceptance gates

The constitution is ready to become canonical only when:

- four-root IA testing shows no systematic Home/Life or Vesper/Life confusion;
- cross-root details preserve origin and canonical destinations remain legible;
- the Sorrento loop works across all five consequence states;
- participants can distinguish attention, occurrence, meaning, and sharing;
- correction and release are discoverable without a settings scavenger hunt;
- the selected visual direction remains legible in light/dark mode, Dynamic
  Type, bright sun, and one-handed use;
- the same grammar composes one travel occasion, one ordinary local occasion,
  one private Encounter, and one multiplayer handoff;
- product, design, and engineering can trace every durable UI state to an
  authority, truth, and receipt contract.

## 18. Explicit non-goals

- Redesigning every current screen before the shell and grammar are validated.
- Treating visual novelty as product differentiation.
- Recreating Google Maps, Instagram, a journaling app, or ChatGPT with tabs.
- Making every input durable.
- Adding a screen for every canonical noun.
- Using engagement, notification volume, or archive size as proof of value.
- Allowing a design system to fossilize unproven product structure.

## Closing principle

> Vesper should feel less like software that organizes a person's world and
> more like a perceptive companion that helps the world become legible, carries
> only what earns consequence, and knows when to withdraw.
