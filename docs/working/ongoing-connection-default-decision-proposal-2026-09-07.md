# Decision proposal: an understandable optional connection

## 1. Status and recommendation

**Proposed, not adopted or implemented.** This September 7 revision replaces
the photo-or-line-only capability and “hide first, disconnect second, block
third” recommendation. Existing pair circles are the leading owner candidate,
not proof of a complete consumer connection.

Recommend mutual, explicit ongoing contact: exchanging eligible material and
addressing future invitations through a known contact path. A photo, map,
recipe, article, place or short message can be equally ordinary. This is not a
proposal for unrestricted messaging attachments or a new social root.

Receiving an invitation, joining dinner, enjoying a photo and creating an
account never establish the connection by themselves.

## 2. What connecting means

- Either person may initiate; the other may accept, decline or leave it.
  No reminders that diagnose silence or pressure either side to respond.
- An object/person context can expose the action when sought. Do not
  automatically append a connection request after every received photograph.
- Connection establishes the stated future-contact capability, not attendance,
  agreement, private history access, precise location, availability, editing,
  AI source use or enrollment into future occasions.
- Sharing to Friends is a deliberate author choice among eligible connections,
  not another invitation/acceptance ritual. A share's effective recipients and
  rights remain explicit under its own owner.
- Existing legitimate external contact can remain the best route. No account
  or Vesper connection is required merely to remember a person.
- A non-account guest's ongoing identity is unresolved until the guest policy
  establishes a verified principal and supported ownership path. Do not
  represent an unverified phone number as a working pair-circle member.

## 3. Attention controls are alternatives, not prerequisites

| Control | Proposed meaning | Must not imply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mute in Home/Places | Reduce a person's ambient material in those surfaces; keep the relationship and an accessible conversation. | Silently discard their direct messages while calling this a feed mute. |
| Conversation/invitation notification controls | Reduce interruptions in the explicitly named channel; preserve the underlying eligible material. | Read/acceptance, delivery to another person, or a block. |
| Disconnect | End ongoing contact eligibility according to its stated scope; prior records/grants follow their own policy. | Rewrite history or silently settle all old-source permissions. |
| Block | Immediately available; prevent prohibited future contact/access through relevant authenticated and capability paths. | A requirement to mute or disconnect first, or automatic abuse reporting. |
| Report | Optional, explicit submission with a known recipient and material scope. | Automatic reporting whenever someone blocks, or a hidden expansion of shared content. |

No private mute notification goes to the other person. Block must be evaluated
across messages, invitations, handoffs and bounded links, including known
identity and unverified-link limits. Do not promise universal prevention before
these paths and alternate-identity limits are implemented and tested.

## 4. Ownership and cross-root effects

Relationships/pair circles remain the first implementation candidate. Trust
owns enforcement/reporting; delivery owners enforce controls at their own
boundaries. Life owns legitimate past records, not a public relationship score.

An initiated request may appear as addressed Home material under its existing
grouping rules. Places may show deliberately shared material under the effective
audience; neither a connection nor a Life person entry widens that audience.
No new settings dashboard or friendship hierarchy is required.

## 5. Acceptance cases

1. Sam receives the photo and leaves; no connection request is manufactured.
2. Sam chooses to request contact; Nora leaves it unanswered without a debt cue.
3. Both connect; a recipe and later dinner invitation can be exchanged, while
   private history and future participation remain closed.
4. Nora mutes Sam in Places; his eligible conversation remains findable and the
   chosen notification behavior is accurately stated.
5. Nora blocks immediately, without first muting or giving a reason; no report
   is filed unless she separately chooses it.
6. Disconnect does not erase an independently held dinner record or claim
   everyone has lost access to every old source.

## 6. Open decisions

Precise initial contact scope; consented audience defaults; old-source access
after disconnect; guest identity; interruption controls; and blocking across
capability links. The UI can be simple while the underlying permissions remain
distinct. No adoption, schema change or feature enablement follows from this doc.
