## Outcome: control without a second Life

Give people a short, discoverable way to inspect and change their relationship
with the service. Preserve useful existing settings; remove the need to scroll
through a portrait to reach them. No personality inventory or life-maintenance
homework is required. The app's relationship supports human agency and human
relationships rather than claiming to know someone's identity better than they do.

Start with a **keep / move / reconcile / later** map of the old route structure:

| Responsibility | Current owner / design direction |
| --- | --- |
| Private account identity, security and service controls | You & Trust; short access from the four-root shell |
| Held material, journeys, people and personal record | Life; do not rebuild Atlas/archive as a second portrait |
| Deliberately shared status/profile and audience | Social, linked to private controls without exposing private context |
| Object-specific correction, exclusion or removal | Existing object owner; contextual controls in Life/readers |
| Global eligible-use and service settings | You & Trust under existing contracts |

This mapping is a design recommendation, **not authority to delete routes,
migrate data, change ownership contracts or revive old follower mechanics**.

## Bounded design packages

### A. A short control entrance and current account

Reuse productive settings rows. Show entry from the shell, account identity,
back/return and the applicable recovery/sign-out states. Keep a deliberate
public/friend profile distinct from the private account surface and Life record.
Coordinate Social's self-authored status rather than designing a third biography.

### B. What the app may use and what remains held

For currently supported imports/connectors, show purpose, access scope, last
usable state, permission loss and disconnect consequences. No speculative
connector catalog or mandatory camera-roll/inbox import. State retained versus
newly inaccessible material accurately under the owner contract.

Show current constraints and wanted assistance without a questionnaire.
Temporary steering is not automatically durable preference. Link to inspection
of retained material and eligible later use; exercise correction, exclusion and
removal with meaningful confirmation/result/failure. Object-local repair stays
with its owner rather than forcing a global settings detour.

Visible conversation history and permission for reuse across contexts remain
different. The optional broader conversation agreement is **not adopted**.
Do not ship a “remember everything” default or invent a privacy switch whose
effects are undefined. Name unresolved decisions beside proposed alternatives.

### C. Continuing help, notifications and fair exit

For actually accepted help, make scope, current status, expiry and stop findable
without a task dashboard. Separate finite requested work from ongoing following.
Reuse Plans 12's donors, keeping proposed service scope conditional.

Show notification preferences alongside actual device permission state and
denied-permission recovery. Home owns useful receiving and exact destination;
this owner supplies control, not a second inbox. Reuse existing preview/privacy
and tap-only contracts, with in-app access even when push never arrives.

Show account recovery and supported export/deletion outcomes: scope, confirmation,
pending or failed state and the practical result. Never promise removal of
another person's independent material or use a spinner as completion evidence.
