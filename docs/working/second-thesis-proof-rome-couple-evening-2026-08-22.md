# Multiplayer Evidence Program: A Rome Evening Works for Both of Us

## Bet

When two people are in a dense, unfamiliar, physically demanding place, Vesper
can use their distinct private context and present condition to make one part of
the world legible, reduce practical burden, and help one coherent shared outcome
happen without flattening their perspectives or turning the evening into a
curriculum.

This is an integrated multiplayer value proof. It complements the narrower
[Sorrento menu proof](first-thesis-proof-sorrento-menu-2026-08-21.md), which
tests a personal doorway and multiplayer safety boundary but not the value of
shared perception or governed coordination. The two programs may run in
parallel or either order. Neither one gates the cross-portfolio architecture.

It instantiates the working [Experience Constitution and Interaction Grammar](vesper-experience-constitution-and-interaction-grammar-2026-08-22.md)
through product journey families CJ02, CJ04, CJ05, CJ07, CJ08, CJ10, CJ12,
CJ14, CJ16, CJ17, and CJ18 in the [CJ registry](canonical-product-journey-families-2026-08-22.md).
Its claims remain governed by the [Product Grammar Hypotheses and Validation Register](product-grammar-hypotheses-and-validation-2026-08-22.md),
especially H02, H03, H06-H09, and H12.

## 1. Situation

Fei and his girlfriend are in Rome in August after visiting the Colosseum.
They are hot, tired, and surrounded by far more historically significant places
and bookable tours than they can meaningfully absorb.

Their perspectives overlap but differ:

- Fei is intrigued by the connection among a recent *Odyssey* film, the Trojan
  War, Aeneas, and Rome.
- His girlfriend may care more about comfort, food, atmosphere, architecture,
  or simply having an enjoyable evening together.
- Both want Rome to feel worthwhile without accepting another demanding tour,
  long walk, or sequence of obligations.

The immediate job is not “teach us Roman history” or “find the highest-rated
attraction.” It is:

> Given who we are, how we feel, where we are, and what has caught our
> attention, help this evening become easier and richer for both of us.

## 2. Product behavior under test

The smallest successful arc is:

```text
two private perspectives + current physical state + place and route truth
  -> one group-safe judgment
  -> immediate practical relief
  -> at most one grounded, relationship-relevant opening or conversation cue
  -> one optional Move or deliberate stop
  -> plural private outcomes + one shared occurrence
  -> optional later consequence
```

The response might identify a shaded or indoor stop already compatible with the
route, explain the Aeneas connection in under ninety seconds while the pair is
seated, invite the girlfriend's different observation, or conclude that dinner
and silence are the right outcome. It must not fabricate a compromise that
serves neither person.

## 3. What this proof must demonstrate

| Promise | Observable behavior |
|---|---|
| See more | At least one person notices, understands, or asks something they otherwise would not, possibly by borrowing the other's lens. |
| Live it well | The choice fits heat, energy, route, time, access, and the actual evening better than a generic recommendation or tour. |
| Carry it forward | Any continuity is authorized, source-bound, and later changes judgment or capability; non-retention remains valid. |
| Experience it together | Both people receive independent value, private context stays private, shared action is coherent, and subjective experience remains plural. |

Multiplayer success is not merely absence of leakage. The pair should be able to
name how Vesper helped them understand one another, combine distinct attention,
or avoid organizer labor while producing a better shared outcome.

## 4. Experience contract

The interaction should feel:

- **attentive, not demanding:** one bounded intervention, not an evening feed;
- **personal, but expansive:** prior mythology interest is one bridge, not the
  definition of either person;
- **practical and meaningful together:** physical comfort and interpretation
  reinforce rather than compete with one another;
- **calmly competent:** route, conditions, timing, and consequence are carried
  without requiring manual reconciliation;
- **groundedly surprising:** the Troy–Aeneas–Rome connection is legible because
  the movie, current Place, and exhibit or source support it;
- **capability-building:** the pair can later recognize or discuss something
  without replaying the original answer;
- **socially perceptive:** Vesper facilitates attention and choice, then
  withdraws when the human conversation takes over;
- **human-authored:** Vesper may connect an artifact, Place, or perspective to
  the other person, but it does not supply either person's emotional meaning or
  relational voice; and
- **continuous, not clingy:** no recap, memory, or push is created merely
  because the interaction occurred.

## 5. Authority and plurality

- Each person may provide private context to Vesper.
- Group-safe reasoning may say that a cooler, shorter, or lower-cost option fits
  the group; it must not attribute the private reason unless authorized.
- Expertise, enthusiasm, organizer effort, or message volume may influence whom
  Vesper consults. They do not increase whose welfare counts.
- Shared truth may include the accepted destination, timing, route, reservation,
  and confirmed occurrence.
- Interest, emotional meaning, enjoyment, memory, and interpretation remain
  personal unless each relevant person deliberately shares them.
- One person accepting an opening does not write a durable interest for the
  other.

No Plan is required if a lightweight Move or answer is sufficient. If a shared
commitment is created, it must reconcile through the current Plan authority and
produce an inspectable receipt.

## 6. Prototype sequence

### Phase A — human-in-the-loop comparison

Use 5-8 pairs in a live or realistically staged city evening. Compare:

1. generic top recommendation;
2. individual preference matching;
3. situated Vesper judgment using both private perspectives and current state;
4. deliberate silence.

This research phase does not require new schema. That economy is a property of
the study, not a prohibition on separately authorized architecture work.

### Phase B — bounded product simulation

Provide:

- coarse current Place and route;
- heat, time, energy, and access conditions;
- one private perspective per person;
- one relevant artifact or attention trace;
- one optional Move; and
- a separate personal and group-safe outcome capture.

The simulation must show the viewer-safe explanation for each person and the
shared consequence, including hold and silence branches.

### Phase C — second occasion

On a later Roman moment or a return home, test one authorized consequence:

- recognize the Aeneas thread elsewhere;
- choose or explain a place differently;
- navigate through a spatial anchor rather than passive turn-following;
- reproduce a practical lesson; or
- use what was learned about the pair to reduce coordination on another
  evening.

The later result must cite the prior evidence and identify what changed. Mere
resurfacing is not success.

## 7. Measurement

Record separately for each person and for the pair:

- time and work required before useful value;
- whether practical burden decreased;
- what became newly legible;
- whether either person borrowed or appreciated the other's perspective;
- whether Vesper made a natural topic or reason for connection easier to find
  while the resulting interpretation and expression remained the people's own;
- whether the resulting Move was accepted, modified, ignored, or regretted;
- whether anyone felt averaged, exposed, socially managed, or assigned
  homework;
- whether Vesper withdrew at the right time;
- what, if anything, each person wanted retained;
- whether the later occasion was materially different; and
- the participants' own language for the value.

Do not combine individual satisfaction into one average score. A coherent group
outcome can coexist with divergent private reactions, and one person's delight
must not hide another person's exclusion.

## 8. Pass and falsification conditions

Pass only if repeated pairs show all of the following:

- less practical or coordination burden than the comparison;
- a better shared outcome than optimizing for either person alone;
- independent value for the thinner participant;
- no private-context leakage or false consensus;
- an intervention small enough to preserve presence;
- a relationship-relevant opening that produces voluntary human attention,
  conversation, or action without synthetic intimacy; and
- at least one observed change in perception, action, conversation, or later
  capability.

Reshape or reject the bet if:

- pairs prefer an ordinary message plus Maps;
- the group-safe explanation feels evasive or manipulative;
- interpretation competes with comfort or companionship;
- private context does not improve the shared decision;
- only the already-curious person benefits;
- the product increases negotiation rather than relieving it; or
- continuity feels creepy, repetitive, or unnecessary.

## 9. Scope boundary

This proof does not authorize background location, automatic companion
inference, public contribution, persistent group identity, a new social root,
generalized recipient selection, or a new Occasion aggregate. It validates a
human outcome first. Existing Plan, relationship, evidence, and receipt owners
remain authoritative until the proof exposes a missing boundary that cannot be
represented honestly.

## 10. Founder pilot decision

The next run is a **Wizard-of-Oz founder pilot**, not a product launch or a
thesis-validation claim. It uses the present Rome trip to debug the journey,
treatment choices, privacy boundaries, and measurement language before testing
additional pairs.

The pilot should take one real evening and no more than:

- 8 minutes of separate intake before leaving or while resting;
- 2 minutes to review the shared synthesis;
- one practical intervention;
- zero or one interpretive or relational opening;
- 4 minutes of separate outcome capture; and
- a 3-minute next-day check.

The pilot fails operationally if research interaction becomes the dominant
activity of the evening. The facilitator may stop at any time and record that
the product treatment should have been silence.

### 10.1 Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Participant A** | Provides one private perspective and current condition; controls what may be shared |
| **Participant B** | Provides an independent private perspective and current condition; controls what may be shared |
| **Vesper operator** | Uses authorized context, current provider truth, and this protocol to prepare treatments |
| **Facilitator/observer** | Times the flow, records behavior and failures, and does not advocate for the treatment |

In the founder pilot, the operator and facilitator may be the same person, but
the role boundary must remain explicit. The operator does not improvise
relationship advice or persuade either participant to continue.

### 10.2 Minimum materials

- two private intake views or separate notes inaccessible to the other person;
- one operator worksheet containing only coded private briefs;
- current Maps, weather, transit, opening-hours, and provider truth;
- one shared treatment view;
- two separate outcome forms;
- one pair receipt; and
- one next-day form.

Do not build schema or production infrastructure for this pilot.

### 10.3 Quick run card

Use this checklist during the founder pilot; the later sections provide the
copy and branch details.

```text
[ ] Assign run ID and prototype/grammar revisions
[ ] Verify coarse Place, weather/heat, routes, hours, and current commitments
[ ] Obtain consent and choose private-mediation or shared-context-only mode
[ ] Collect A intake privately
[ ] Collect B intake privately
[ ] Write prohibited disclosures before generating shared copy
[ ] Apply safety and receptivity gates
[ ] Generate once and run the operator review checklist
[ ] Show one group-safe judgment and at most one alternative
[ ] Hand operational action to Maps/provider; do not imply occurrence
[ ] Offer zero or one opening only after practical relief
[ ] Withdraw when the pair or external system takes over
[ ] Collect A outcome privately
[ ] Collect B outcome privately
[ ] Record shared consequence as confirmed, false, or unknown
[ ] Run next-day checks separately
[ ] Delete raw private intake or record explicit research-retention permission
[ ] Choose repeat, revise, stop, or expand_pair_study
```

## 11. Consent, custody, and pilot data handling

Before intake, show both participants:

> This is a product prototype. You may provide private context to the operator.
> Only the parts you mark shareable may be attributed to you. Vesper may use a
> private constraint to shape the shared suggestion without revealing it. You
> can stop, correct, or ask for deletion at any time. No relationship score or
> permanent profile will be created.

Each private input has one of three use scopes:

```text
private_to_treatment
  may affect judgment but may not be revealed or attributed

shareable_with_partner
  may appear in the shared explanation with attribution

research_only
  may inform analysis but not the live treatment
```

Pilot handling rules:

- Assign participant codes `A` and `B`; do not put intimate free text in the
  shared run log.
- Raw private intake expires after the next-day debrief unless the participant
  separately authorizes research retention.
- The pair receipt records the shared occurrence, not each person's private
  evaluation or meaning.
- A participant may delete their private intake without deleting the other's
  input or the provider-sourced shared occurrence.
- If either person does not consent to private mediation, run the public-context
  or deliberate-silence branch instead.

```text

Facts explicitly shareable and attributable:
Public provider or Place facts:
Unknowns that must remain unknown:
```

If the operator cannot explain the shared treatment without exposing a private
reason, it must use a neutral operational explanation or abstain.

## 13. Treatment decision procedure

Run the gates in order. Later gates cannot override earlier ones.

### Gate 1 — Safety and operational truth

Resolve immediate heat, mobility, navigation, timing, access, and provider
uncertainty first. If a material operational fact is unknown, verify it or
de-afford the dependent action.

### Gate 2 — Receptivity

Choose the highest treatment that both people's current state can support:

| Condition | Maximum default treatment |
|---|---|
| Crossing, navigating, acute discomfort, conflict, or urgency | Silence, haptic, or operational cue |
| Walking, warm, tired, or actively conversing | One line or defer |
| Seated, safe, mutually receptive | Short shared cue or one bounded proposal |
| Explicitly asks for depth | Deeper explanation, still with an exit |

The less receptive participant sets the shared attention ceiling. This does not
give them unilateral control over the whole evening; it limits what Vesper may
ask both people to attend to together.

### Gate 3 — Independent value

The treatment must offer plausible value to both people without pretending the
value is identical. Practical relief can be one person's main benefit and an
interpretive opening the other's.

Reject options that:

- maximize one person's interest while merely tolerating the other;
- manufacture a compromise neither wants;
- require hidden organizer labor to make them feasible; or
- use private context only decoratively.

### Gate 4 — Shared action

Use the lightest sufficient consequence:

```text
silence
answer only
one shared attention cue
one Move
one lightweight Occasion adjustment
```

Do not create or restructure a Plan unless commitment truth requires it.

### Gate 5 — Relational opening

Offer at most one opening only when it has:

```text
truthful source
+ present Place or situation relevance
+ specific reason it may matter to this pair
+ attention capacity
+ a natural human handoff
```

The opening may end with “ask what she noticed,” “compare one detail,” or no
prompt at all. It may not author either person's emotional interpretation.

### Gate 6 — Withdrawal

Withdraw when:

- the practical job is solved;
- the pair begins talking;
- the Move has been handed to Maps or the provider;
- one person disengages;
- no opening clears the attention cost; or
- another response would turn the evening into product testing.

## 14. Operator generation contract

The operator may use an LLM, but the output must conform to this structure and
be reviewed before participants see it.

### 14.1 Operator prompt template

```text
You are simulating Vesper for a consenting adult pair in a real city evening.

Your job is to reduce practical and coordination burden, preserve plural private
perspectives, and offer at most one grounded opening. Do not diagnose the
relationship, reveal private input, average the pair into a group personality,
or optimize engagement.

PUBLIC SITUATION
{public situation and checked provider facts}

PRIVATE BRIEF A — use for judgment; reveal only fields marked shareable
{coded brief}

PRIVATE BRIEF B — use for judgment; reveal only fields marked shareable
{coded brief}

PROHIBITED DISCLOSURES
{private facts that may not be expressed or attributed}

Return:
1. treatment: silence | operational_cue | one_line | shared_prompt | proposal
2. why_this_treatment_now: one operator-only sentence
3. group_safe_judgment: maximum 45 words
4. primary_action: one action or null
5. optional_alternative: at most one or null
6. relational_opening: maximum 30 words or null
7. provider_uncertainty: what remains unknown
8. private_leak_check: list every private input used and confirm it is not exposed
9. withdrawal_condition: the exact point Vesper should stop
10. consequence_and_receipt: none, Move, or Plan mutation requiring review
```

### 14.2 Operator review checklist

Before release, answer yes to all:

- Is every operational claim checked or clearly uncertain?
- Does the group-safe explanation avoid revealing private reasons?
- Can each person plausibly receive independent value?
- Is the treatment within the less-receptive person's attention ceiling?
- Is there no more than one primary action and one alternative?
- Is the opening grounded and optional?
- Does the copy leave human expression to the people?
- Is the withdrawal point explicit?
- Is any consequence proportional and reviewable?

If any answer is no, revise once or choose silence. Do not repeatedly generate
until a persuasive recommendation appears.

## 15. Exact pilot storyboard

This storyboard is surface-neutral. It can be rendered as paper, Figma, a local
web prototype, or two private forms plus one shared phone.

### State R0 — Consent and current situation

**Who sees it:** both people.

**Visible:** pilot explanation, stop/delete control, coarse Place and current
conditions.

**Actions:** `Continue privately`, `Use only shared context`, `Stop pilot`.

**No consequence:** consent does not create a Plan, memory, or relationship
profile.

### State R1 — Private perspective

**Who sees it:** one participant at a time.

**Visible:** the short intake from Section 12.2 and a scope selector.

**Actions:** `Use privately`, `Okay to attribute`, `Skip`.

**Receipt:** “Private for this treatment · expires after tomorrow's debrief.”

### State R2 — Holding state

**Who sees it:** both people.

**Visible:** “Finding the lightest thing that could improve this evening.”

**Actions:** `Never mind`.

**Must not show:** profiles, internal reasoning, private inputs, or a progress
theater longer than the actual operator work.

### State R3 — Shared judgment

**Who sees it:** both people.

**Composition:** maximum 45-word group-safe judgment, one recommended action,
one alternative at most, checked operational facts, and “why now.”

Illustrative structure:

> You have enough time for one comfortable stop, not another route of sights.
> Sit somewhere cool near your dinner direction; if you still want a thread
> into Rome, there is one short connection worth bringing with you.

**Actions:** `Do this`, `Modify`, `Just help us get comfortable`, `Not now`.

### State R4 — Action handoff

**Who sees it:** both people.

**Visible:** destination or operational instruction, walking burden, shade or
transit implication, checked-at time, uncertainty, and provider owner.

**Actions:** `Open in Maps/provider`, `Choose alternative`, `Cancel`.

**Receipt:** distinguish proposal from commitment. A handoff is not proof that
the Move occurred.

### State R5 — Situated opening

**Admission:** only after practical relief and only if both remain receptive.

**Visible:** one source-grounded cue, no more than 30 spoken seconds or roughly
60 readable words.

Example shape:

> The story has switched sides: the film followed a Greek trying to return from
> Troy; Rome traced its ancestry to Aeneas, a Trojan escaping it. What did each
> of you notice here that the other might have missed?

**Actions:** `Hear a little more`, `Ask each other`, `Done`.

The preferred success is that the pair starts talking and Vesper disappears.

### State R6 — Intentional quiet

**Who sees it:** nobody unless they reopen Vesper.

No follow-up question, push, recap request, or retention prompt appears during
the live interaction.

### State R7 — Separate personal outcome

**Who sees it:** each participant privately after the natural stopping point.

**Visible:** the four-minute outcome form in Section 17.

**Actions:** `Submit privately`, `Share one observation with partner`, `Delete`.

One person's private outcome cannot populate the pair receipt.

### State R8 — Pair receipt

**Who sees it:** both people.

**Visible only if a shared consequence occurred:** what was proposed, what was
confirmed, provider/source evidence, and correction.

```text
Shared: walked to [Place] at approximately [time]
Source: participant confirmation / provider fact
Not recorded: private reasons, enjoyment, meaning, or inferred preference
[Correct] [Let expire]
```

### State R9 — Next-day continuity check

**Who sees it:** each person privately, once.

**Visible:** a neutral research check, not a personalized resurfacing card.

**Actions:** `Answer`, `Skip and delete pilot data`.

No future product continuity is created from completing the research form.

## 16. Required failure and recovery branches

| Branch | Trigger | Required behavior |
|---|---|---|
| `R.B01 private conflict` | Private constraints make a proposed option unsuitable | Use a neutral group-safe reason or abstain; do not reveal whose constraint controlled |
| `R.B02 no joint receptivity` | One person wants depth and the other wants quiet | Cap shared treatment at quiet/practical; allow individual continuation later |
| `R.B03 wrong Place or route` | Participant corrects location, entrance, or burden | Stop action, acknowledge, correct source, regenerate once, preserve draft |
| `R.B04 provider unknown` | Hours, availability, or execution status cannot be verified | Mark unknown, hand off for confirmation, do not imply success or retry |
| `R.B05 no fitting option` | Every option violates a constraint or attention ceiling | Say no option earned; offer rest, direct question, or silence |
| `R.B06 opening rejected` | Either person says irrelevant, forced, or homework-like | End interpretation immediately; do not defend relevance or store disinterest |
| `R.B07 human conversation begins` | Pair starts discussing or observing together | Withdraw without a follow-up prompt |
| `R.B08 request to expose reason` | One person asks why Vesper avoided or favored something | Give group-safe reasoning; invite the owner to share their own reason |
| `R.B09 consequence disputed` | Participants disagree whether Move occurred | Keep state unknown or plural; do not resolve by majority |
| `R.B10 participant withdraws` | Either person stops consent | Stop mediation, delete their private input, preserve only independently authorized facts |
| `R.B11 connectivity loss` | Maps/provider or prototype becomes unavailable | Preserve drafts, offer source-labeled offline facts only, never show fake success |
| `R.B12 operator leak` | Shared copy reveals or strongly implies private input | Stop pilot, show affected participant, delete treatment, record critical failure |

`R.B12` is a stop condition, not a recoverable cosmetic defect.

## 17. Outcome instruments

### 17.1 Private immediate form

Each participant answers independently on a 1-7 scale unless noted.

```text
PRACTICAL
The intervention made the evening easier.
The proposed action fit my actual energy and constraints.

PERCEPTION AND CAPABILITY
I noticed or understood something I otherwise might have missed.
I could explain, recognize, or use any new distinction again.

MULTIPLAYER
The outcome worked for me, not merely for the pair in aggregate.
I understood something about how my partner saw the situation.
My own perspective remained distinct rather than averaged.
The product reduced work one of us would otherwise have carried.

RELATIONAL INTEGRITY
Any conversation or expression still felt like ours.
I felt free not to respond or continue.

ATTENTION
The intervention arrived at an appropriate moment.
It felt like homework. [reverse]
Vesper stopped when it should have.

TRUST
I understood what private information could affect the suggestion.
I felt exposed or socially managed. [reverse]
I understand what, if anything, was retained.

OPEN TEXT
What became easier?
What, if anything, became more interesting or legible?
Did your partner help you see something differently?
Where should Vesper have said less, said more, or stayed silent?
What felt most like a product rather than part of the evening?
What, if anything, should be remembered for another occasion?
```

Do not calculate one pair satisfaction average. Report A, B, the least-served
participant, and the pair consequence separately.

### 17.2 Pair debrief

After both private forms are complete, ask together:

1. What did Vesper understand correctly about the kind of evening this was?
2. Where did it flatten or misread the two of you?
3. Did either person's perspective help the other notice something?
4. What coordination work disappeared, and did any new work appear?
5. When should Vesper have left the interaction sooner?

Do not ask the pair to reconcile differences in their private ratings.

### 17.3 Observer log

Record behavior rather than inferred emotion:

```yaml
time_to_first_useful_treatment_seconds: null
number_of_choices_presented: null
number_of_operator_revisions: null
private_leak_or_implication: false
action: accepted | modified | rejected | silence
opening: accepted | ignored | rejected | not_offered
conversation_began_without_prompt: false
vesper_withdrew_within_15_seconds_of_handoff: false
organizer_actions_required_after_treatment: null
screen_attention_seconds_after_practical_handoff: null
correction_or_recovery_used: null
shared_consequence_confirmed: true | false | unknown
```

## 18. Next-day continuity check

Ask each person separately:

1. Without reopening the treatment, what—if anything—do you remember noticing,
   understanding, deciding, or doing differently?
2. Did anything from last night affect what you noticed, discussed, navigated,
   chose, or wanted today?
3. Did the other person's perspective remain useful or interesting?
4. Would resurfacing the source now be helpful, unnecessary, or intrusive?
5. Is there one consequence worth keeping for a later Occasion? Why?

Code the result as:

```text
no_present_delta
practical_delta
perceptual_delta
conversation_delta
coordination_delta
capability_delta
relationship_delta_reported_by_participant
```

Recognition or delight without a present delta is not continuity success.

## 20. Founder-pilot decision rubric

After the first run, choose exactly one:

- **Repeat:** protocol was usable; another founder run is needed before a
  structural change.
- **Revise:** the journey, copy, treatment ceiling, privacy boundary, or
  measurement instrument failed but the bet remains plausible.
- **Stop:** a critical authorship, privacy, attention, or practical-value failure
  undermines the current treatment.
- **Expand pair study:** the protocol produced independent value for both people,
  no critical failure, useful withdrawal behavior, and interpretable measures.

The founder pilot may advance to a 5-8-pair study only if:

- both participants received some independent value;
- the less enthusiastic or more burdened person was not treated as a constraint
  around the other person's experience;
- private-to-treatment context remained undisclosed;
- the practical intervention succeeded or honestly remained uncertain;
- any opening remained optional and human-authored;
- Vesper withdrew before competing with the evening; and
- both private forms produced understandable, non-coerced evidence.

Do not require a second-occasion success before expanding the study; record it
as a separate longitudinal signal.
