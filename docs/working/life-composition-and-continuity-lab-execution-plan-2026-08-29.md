# Life Composition and Continuity Lab Execution Plan

> Status: active working plan
>
> This plan does not establish new product canon, approve a production Life
> root, authorize a new backend owner, or select final visual design. It turns
> the accepted Life direction into a controlled sequence of complete
> experiences, founder decisions, projection contracts, and native evidence.

## 0. Executive decision

> **2026-08-29 value-gate correction:** Pause visual composition and projection
> extraction until the four complete outcomes in
> `life-outcome-storyboard-pack-2026-08-29.md` and their integrated post-return
> manuscript pass founder review. Recover, Re-enter, Carry, and Together
> replace the earlier five-label gate; Re-enter
> deliberately joins reconstruction with new representational or epistemic
> value. The first composition round made the semantics more legible but
> exposed a more fundamental weakness: retrieval structure was being presented
> as user value. Timeline, clusters, Map, Sources, and scope remain valid
> enabling work; they no longer authorize the shape of the default page.

Build the **Life Composition and Continuity Lab** in this revised order:

```text
accepted semantics and six controlled worlds
  -> renderer-neutral composition dataset
  -> four complete outcomes: Recover, Re-enter, Carry, Together
  -> one integrated post-return New York experience manuscript
  -> founder value-gate decision
  -> complete visual compositions in a dedicated visual tool
  -> founder comparison and selection
  -> LifeIndexProjectionV0 + LifeEpisodeProjectionV0
  -> native fixture lab in the existing app/dev harness
  -> continuity, authority, accessibility, and performance evidence
  -> current-owner mapping and production go/no-go
```

Do **not** start with a production API, a universal Life table, a generalized
component schema, or a replacement of the current root navigation. Those
choices should be consequences of a selected complete experience.

The first product-design target is not one isolated behavior loop or one
finished page. It is a value-complete set of experiences that proves what Life
does for the person before the system is composed. The later coherent Life
system must simultaneously show:

- a complete index containing all six evidence worlds in their correct
  postures;
- a rich Journey episode for L01 Europe;
- a lifecycle-aware, plural social Occasion for L02 Brooklyn dinner;
- a longitudinal Place relationship for L06 Red Hook;
- subordinate but useful treatment for L03, L04, and L05;
- Combined, Mine, and Together scope;
- Timeline, Map, and Sources as ways into the same identities;
- correction, revocation, block, reconciliation, and deletion;
- continuation into Home, Chat, and Places; and
- return to the same semantic position in Life.

This is a **product-definition environment**, not a market-proof environment.
It makes the new product experience concrete enough to judge and implement.
It does not claim that a user behavior, retention loop, or market thesis has
been proven.

## 1. Why this is the next step

The program has already answered several foundational questions:

1. Life is a governed continuity and return layer, not an archive, journal,
   media library, or retrospective feed.
2. Its four human jobs are Find, Return, See anew, and Continue.
3. Its stable spine is periods and episodes; Timeline, Map, and Sources are
   contextual lenses over the same corpus.
4. Its stable detail unit is an episode projection with a common semantic
   anatomy, not a universal artifact card.
5. Generated value belongs in a bounded Returns region and inside eligible
   episodes. It cannot replace or rewrite the record.
6. Social history is viewer-relative and lifecycle-aware; Mine and Together
   are authority scopes, not styling filters.
7. The index and episode need two cooperating renderers over one semantic
   identity system.

The unresolved risk is no longer a lack of broad ideas. The first composition
attempt showed that the more important risk precedes visual coherence: the
system may organize a rich corpus without delivering a legible human payoff.
Even if that value gate passes, individually reasonable rules may still
compose into a product that feels:

- like a card feed;
- too retrospective;
- too dense;
- administratively heavy;
- visually generic;
- fragmented across Map, Timeline, and Sources;
- privacy-complicated; or
- disconnected from Home, Chat, and Places.

Value-complete, comparable experiences must expose the payoff failure first.
Only then can complete visual compositions expose hierarchy, density,
continuity, and interaction failures. Additional research is useful only when
it resolves a named value uncertainty or a failed counterfactual.

## 2. Program outcomes

### 2.1 Required outcomes

By the end of the program, the founder should be able to answer concretely:

1. What does a person see when opening a rich, quiet, or sparse Life?
2. What earns a stable entrance, and what remains nested or search-only?
3. How do a Journey, social Occasion, and Place relationship feel related
   without becoming the same template?
4. How does a person move between index, episode, Timeline, Map, and Sources
   without losing place?
5. How does Together enrich Life without turning it into a social feed?
6. How do corrections and privacy changes remain understandable without
   making Life look like a policy dashboard?
7. What substantial value does Vesper add beyond a factual recap?
8. How does an episode open a present possibility without becoming homework?
9. Which semantic fields are actually required by the chosen experience?
10. Which current backend owners can supply each field, and which aggregate
    read capabilities are genuinely missing?

### 2.2 Concrete deliverables

The program produces five classes of deliverable:

| Deliverable | Purpose | Authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Renderer-neutral controlled dataset | Keeps all composition explorations grounded in the same truth and identity | Fixture evidence only |
| Dedicated visual-tool composition set | Explores hierarchy, density, medium, rhythm, and visual language | Design exploration only |
| Founder decision receipt | Records selected composition rules and rejected alternatives | Working decision input |
| `LifeIndexProjectionV0` and `LifeEpisodeProjectionV0` | Captures the minimum semantic contract implied by the selected experience | Lab contract only |
| Native Life lab and evidence | Tests real layout, transitions, scope, accessibility, failure, and performance | Implementation evidence only |

### 2.3 Non-goals

This program does not:

- replace Plan/Trip with Home;
- add the production Life root to navigation;
- create a universal artifact, episode, relationship, or memory table;
- implement production search, Map, Timeline, or Sources aggregation;
- build a public profile, follower graph, or generic friend activity feed;
- introduce Storybook, a new list engine, or shared-element navigation by
  default;
- settle automatic ingestion or background collection;
- prove ranking quality, retention, or willingness to pay;
- permit generated psychographic interpretation;
- make Continue a task list or reminder queue;
- require users to organize, caption, classify, or answer retrospective
  prompts; or
- design every object and edge that Life may eventually support.

## 3. Authority and dependency boundaries

### 3.1 Upstream authority

The lab must treat these as fixed unless a contradiction is discovered:

- the accepted Life continuity and return decision owns entrance, re-finding,
  source identity, social historical access, return, and Continue policy;
- canonical domain owners retain durable truth;
- the Life anatomy working contract owns the current semantic hierarchy;
- the expression canon owns medium and projection doctrine;
- the contribution contract owns what input may create or change;
- the four-root direction owns Home, Chat, Places, and Life responsibilities;
  and
- current app contracts retain production implementation authority.

If composition work reveals a contradiction with an accepted decision, stop
and record the exact counterexample. Do not silently bend the fixture or let a
visual treatment invent new authority.

### 3.2 The lab may decide

The lab is allowed to decide:

- visible hierarchy and folding;
- how much detail appears before opening an episode;
- which regions lead, support, collapse, or disappear;
- visual kinship and legitimate divergence among episode kinds;
- where scope and lens controls live;
- how generated value is distinguished from record truth;
- how Continue appears without pressure;
- transition and restoration behavior;
- native component boundaries; and
- the smallest semantic projection needed to drive the accepted composition.

### 3.3 The lab may not decide

The lab may not decide:

- truth ownership;
- audience expansion;
- new inference or retention rights;
- that engagement changes semantic eligibility;
- that a planned item occurred;
- that a shared occurrence implies a shared Outcome;
- that a generated composition is canonical memory;
- backend component trees, pixel geometry, navigation, or arbitrary layout
  blobs; or
- a production schema before field-to-owner mapping.

## 4. Operating model: three coordinated artifacts

The work uses three distinct artifacts because no one environment can prove
everything.

### 4.1 Artifact A — renderer-neutral controlled composition dataset

This dataset is the shared source for visual exploration and the later native
lab. It describes evidence, identity, authority, lifecycle, and eligible
semantic regions. It contains no coordinates, font sizes, colors, platform
components, gesture definitions, or arbitrary visual block tree.

Conceptual types:

```ts
type LifeLabWorld = {
  worldId: "L01" | "L02" | "L03" | "L04" | "L05" | "L06";
  canonicalRefs: ResourceRef[];
  sources: SourceFixture[];
  truth: TruthFixture[];
  memberships: MembershipFixture[];
  scopes: ScopeFixture[];
  states: WorldStateFixture[];
};

type LifeIndexFixture = {
  projectionIdentity: ProjectionIdentity;
  scope: "combined" | "mine" | "together";
  semanticAnchor: SemanticAnchor;
  sections: LifeIndexSection[];
};

type LifeEpisodeFixture = {
  projectionIdentity: ProjectionIdentity;
  episodeRef: ResourceRef;
  episodeKind: "journey" | "occasion" | "place_relationship";
  lifecycle: LifeLifecycle;
  anatomy: EpisodeAnatomy;
  eligibleReturns: ReturnFixture[];
  continueOptions: ContinueFixture[];
};

type ProjectionIdentity = {
  resourceId: string;
  revision: string;
  viewerId: string;
  audienceEpoch: string;
  schemaVersion: string;
};

type LifeRestorationAnchor = {
  resourceId: string;
  sectionId?: string;
  itemId?: string;
  localReadingOffset?: number;
};
```

These names are working vocabulary, not approved API types.

### 4.2 Artifact B — dedicated visual-tool composition set

The dedicated visual tool owns exploration of:

- full-scroll rhythm;
- type, image, map, sequence, comparison, evidence, audio, and editorial
  hierarchy;
- stable index density;
- episode-level expressive folding;
- the relationship between record and generated return;
- quiet, rich, and sparse aesthetics;
- cross-unit visual kinship;
- scope and lens affordances; and
- progressive detail and transition intent.

Every composition must name the fixture IDs and state axes it renders. A
beautiful frame that cannot be traced to the controlled dataset is not valid
product evidence.

The visual tool should explore **whole compositions**, not isolated cards. It
may propose new component forms, but every form must identify its semantic job,
source, authority, and fallback.

### 4.3 Artifact C — native fixture lab

The existing `travel-app/app/dev` infrastructure owns validation that static
visual composition cannot provide:

- scroll and mount behavior;
- real text reflow;
- accessible reading order;
- focus and screen-reader recovery;
- Combined/Mine/Together mutation;
- lifecycle transitions;
- correction and revocation with zero unauthorized frames;
- semantic-anchor restoration;
- offline and stale states;
- old-client fallback; and
- release-build performance.

The native lab begins only after the founder selects the first complete visual
composition family and the team extracts the projection contract it requires.

## 5. Controlled evidence portfolio

### 5.1 Canonical posture of all six worlds

| World | Required role in the complete Life system | Independent stable door? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **L01 Europe Journey** | Rich bounded Journey across Nice, Sorrento, Amalfi, Rome, and return to New York | Yes in rich state |
| **L02 Brooklyn dinner** | Social Occasion from prospective through live, happened, and historical | Yes |
| **L03 unused museum ticket** | Truthful Plan/source that did not become an Occurrence | No; nested or search-only |
| **L04 film/book relevant in Rome** | Source-grounded bridge that may support See anew | Nested; earned saved composition may become a door |
| **L05 Rome–Paris friend comparison** | Addressed plural social juxtaposition with revocable Together content | No generic friend door; contextual return only |
| **L06 Red Hook relationship** | Recurring person–Place relationship across time | Yes only with supported recurrence |

All six must appear in the same product world. The lab fails if it renders only
the three visually attractive stable units and ignores subordinate truth.

### 5.2 Three full episode compositions

#### L01 — Journey richness and expressive folding

L01 must prove:

- an episode can contain tickets, stays, photographs, movement, Places,
  authored notes, cultural Sources, planned-but-not-lived material, and a
  source-grounded composition without becoming a scrapbook or itinerary;
- rich evidence improves folding and synthesis instead of increasing card
  count linearly;
- the occurred route leads while planned, changed, and unresolved truth stays
  inspectable;
- L03 and L04 are discoverable in their proper context;
- Map, Timeline, and Sources preserve the same Journey identity; and
- one Continue possibility can open present New York life without implying a
  travel personality or asking for more reflection.

#### L02 — social plurality and lifecycle

L02 must prove:

- one Occasion remains the same object across prospective, live, recent, and
  historical phases;
- people, contributions, logistics, photographs, dishes, messages, and
  personal Outcomes do not collapse into one consensus story;
- Together is useful without becoming a generic social feed;
- joining, leaving, blocking, contribution withdrawal, audience revocation,
  and reconciliation remain legible and safe;
- continuation may help another gathering work without producing homework;
  and
- record, correspondence, receipt, and generated afterglow remain distinct.

#### L06 — longitudinal relationship with a Place

L06 must prove:

- one isolated save remains searchable without earning a major entrance;
- supported recurrence can become a stable Across time door;
- the episode shows how visits, people, Sources, and current Place truth relate
  without declaring identity or taste;
- Life and Places hand off without duplicating each other's responsibility;
- a changed restaurant, event, route, or neighborhood condition can reopen the
  relationship through complete current value; and
- the ordered accessible alternative remains complete when a map or visual
  history leads.

### 5.3 Subordinate-world requirements

- **L03** must be findable as the unused ticket and remain visibly not-lived.
- **L04** must add something not already said by the person; restatement fails.
- **L05** must preserve attribution and two perspectives; generic social proof
  or a friend leaderboard fails.
- None may be promoted merely because its visual treatment is engaging.

## 6. Complete visual composition set

The visual exploration should produce comparable full-scroll or full-detail
frames rather than a component catalog.

### 6.1 Index frames

| Frame | Required state | Primary question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **I-01** | Rich Combined index with L01–L06 | Does the whole system feel coherent, valuable, and navigable? |
| **I-02** | Same corpus in Mine | Does scope change without visual replacement or private leakage? |
| **I-03** | Same corpus in Together | Does social context enrich rather than dominate? |
| **I-04** | Quiet mature account | Is low pressure still immediately valuable and explorable? |
| **I-05** | Sparse/new account | Does Life offer truth and utility without asking for homework? |
| **I-06** | Search result for a vague L03 cue | Does target-first plus Around this outperform archive browsing? |

### 6.2 Episode frames

| Frame | Required state | Primary question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **E-01** | L01 rich Journey detail | Can richness fold into a legible, expressive episode? |
| **E-02** | L01 Map lens with semantic target | Does spatial exploration remain the same Journey? |
| **E-03** | L01 Sources lens with L04 bridge | Are Sources inspectable without becoming a source feed? |
| **E-04** | L02 prospective | Can the Occasion help reality work without becoming a project manager? |
| **E-05** | L02 recent/happened | Does afterglow have value without recap administration? |
| **E-06** | L02 Together after correction/revocation | Is plurality and withdrawal understandable without policy chrome? |
| **E-07** | L06 supported recurrence | Does a relationship across time feel earned and concrete? |
| **E-08** | L06 handoff into Places and return | Do roots feel complementary rather than duplicated? |

### 6.3 Continuity frames or motion boards

The visual tool should also specify intent for these paths:

```text
index door -> episode -> Timeline -> same episode
index door -> episode -> Map -> same semantic Place/Occurrence
search target -> Around this -> parent episode -> back to query target
episode -> Continue in Home -> return to episode anchor
episode -> Ask/Correct in Chat -> receipt -> corrected episode anchor
episode -> current world truth in Places -> return to episode anchor
Combined -> Mine -> Together -> same surviving semantic anchor
```

These may be motion boards or annotated state sequences. Final transition
engineering belongs in the native lab.

### 6.4 Composition constraints

Every frame must satisfy:

1. It begins by delivering value, not asking for reflection or maintenance.
2. It contains no unsupported interpretation of personality, emotional state,
   relationship quality, or identity.
3. A generated return must contribute new, source-grounded substance rather
   than repeat what the person already noticed.
4. The stable record and dynamic return layer remain visually distinguishable.
5. Sources appear where they support truth; source counts are not value.
6. Continue is contextual, optional, and complete enough to judge.
7. Richness changes folding, medium, and synthesis—not just card quantity.
8. Social content is attributed and addressed, not ambient friend activity.
9. Planned, occurred, unresolved, generated, and corrected state remain
   distinguishable without teaching internal ontology.
10. Every rich visual region has a plausible linear reading equivalent.

## 7. State and mutation matrix

The controlled dataset and native lab must vary axes independently.

| Axis | Required values |
| --- | --- |
| World | L01–L06 |
| Entrance posture | stable, nested, contextual, search-only, pinned |
| Scope | Combined, Mine, Together |
| Lifecycle | prospective, live, recent, historical, dormant |
| Evidence density | sparse, standard, rich |
| Account posture | new, quiet mature, active rich |
| Lens | Periods/index, Timeline, Map, Sources |
| Truth | planned, occurred, unresolved, corrected, deleted, generated |
| Social transition | join, leave, withdraw contribution, revoke audience, block, reconcile |
| Connectivity | online, offline-authorized, stale, partial, failed |
| Location | available, denied, unavailable |
| Client compatibility | current, unknown section, unknown treatment, old schema |
| Accessibility | 320-point width, 150% text, screen reader, Reduce Motion |

The lab does not need a screenshot for every Cartesian permutation. It needs
deterministic structural fixtures for the full matrix, visual goldens for
representative families, and focused transition evidence for high-risk
authority changes.

## 8. Execution phases and hard gates

### P0 — Baseline and language reconciliation

**Purpose:** establish a clean working boundary without a repository-wide
rewrite.

Tasks:

1. Record current root naming and the intended Home/Chat/Places/Life mapping.
2. Confirm the six fixture IDs, canonical ResourceRefs, revisions, viewers,
   audience epochs, and source roles.
3. Identify the exact existing visual-tool project or create a bounded new
   Life exploration file outside production app code.
4. Record current native labs, screenshot routes, Maestro helpers, query-cache
   behavior, and release profiling scripts to reuse.
5. Freeze a fixture version for the first comparison round.

Deliverable:

- one baseline receipt appended to this plan or a tightly linked fixture
  manifest;
- no broad canon rewrite;
- no app code.

**Exit gate:** every subsequent artifact can name one fixture version, one
viewer, one scope, one audience epoch, and the relevant accepted policy.

### P1 — Renderer-neutral composition dataset

**Purpose:** give every design direction the same semantic material.

Tasks:

1. Encode L01–L06 as a portable JSON or TypeScript-neutral fixture document.
2. Encode the complete Life index hierarchy and three episode anatomies.
3. Encode subordinate membership for L03, L04, and L05.
4. Encode current and prior states for lifecycle and social transitions.
5. Encode eligible and suppressed Returns and Continue options with reasons.
6. Add human-readable expected-result annotations for visual designers.
7. Add a linter or deterministic validation script if the representation is
   executable; otherwise validate it against an explicit checklist.

Required checks:

- stable identity is separate from revision;
- viewer and audience epoch are explicit;
- no resource has two canonical owners;
- Plans cannot appear as Occurrences without evidence;
- generated compositions cite claim-local Sources;
- Outcomes remain personal unless explicitly shared;
- entrance posture is explicit and policy-derived; and
- every suppression has a reason.

**Exit gate:** all six worlds can generate one coherent textual index outline,
the three complete episode outlines, and the expected Mine/Together semantic
diff without any visual assumptions.

### P2 — Complete visual composition round

**Purpose:** compare whole-product possibilities at the correct altitude.

**Entry gate:** the four outcome storyboards—Recover, Re-enter, Carry, and
Together—have each identified what Vesper adds, what changes for the
person, and why the result beats the same material in a photo library, map,
notes, and stateless AI chat. The integrated post-return manuscript has also
shown how they coexist under one finite hierarchy without becoming four
permanent sections. A storyboard may be deliberately silent when the evidence
cannot support its intended value.

Tasks:

1. Produce I-01 through I-06 and E-01 through E-08 in a dedicated visual tool.
2. Explore two or three genuinely different composition strategies for I-01,
   E-01, E-05, and E-07—not mere color or card-shape variants.
3. Keep other frames as stress tests of the strongest strategies.
4. Annotate each major region with semantic job, evidence, authority, source,
   action, and fallback.
5. Produce motion intent for the seven continuity paths.
6. Reject any strategy that depends on hidden truth, unsupported inference, or
   maintenance prompts.

The alternatives should vary meaningful composition choices, such as:

- period-led editorial folio versus spatial-temporal field;
- stable episode spine with interleaved earned Returns versus a separated
  Returns region;
- compact social lanes versus episode-integrated attributed contributions;
- mode-switching lenses versus contextual lens entrances; and
- progressive episode folds versus one long continuous narrative.

They must not become six unrelated visual languages.

**Exit gate:** at least one complete direction passes the semantic constraints
for all six worlds and feels plausibly like one Life product, not a collection
of individually attractive cards.

### P3 — Founder convergence and decision receipt

**Purpose:** turn visual exploration into product decisions before code.

The founder reviews complete compositions, not isolated details. The review
records:

1. selected index hierarchy;
2. selected relationship between Lived periods, Across time, and Returns;
3. selected episode kinship and allowed divergence;
4. scope-control placement and behavior;
5. lens entrance and return behavior;
6. generated-value prominence;
7. Continue prominence and handoff posture;
8. repair/provenance disclosure level;
9. quiet and sparse posture;
10. rejected alternatives and the reason each failed.

Use this four-way verdict for every decision:

- **adopt** — carry into projection extraction;
- **revise** — composition is directionally right but fails a named gate;
- **defer** — valuable but unnecessary for the first native lab;
- **reject** — contradicts product philosophy, authority, or coherence.

**Exit gate:** the selected composition is sufficiently complete that every
visible region can be described without visual-tool-specific language and
every unresolved choice is either blocking or explicitly deferred.

### P4 — Projection-contract extraction

**Purpose:** derive the smallest renderer-neutral contract from the selected
experience.

Create lab-only contracts for:

```text
LifeIndexProjectionV0
LifeEpisodeProjectionV0
LifeProjectionIdentityV0
LifeRestorationAnchorV0
LifeSectionV0
LifeTreatmentBriefV0
LifeTransitionFixtureV0
```

The server/read layer may specify:

- semantic identity and revision;
- viewer and audience epoch;
- job and semantic region;
- truth/lifecycle state;
- canonical references and source roles;
- allowed actions and destination envelopes;
- section ordering and omission;
- medium affordances and evidence availability;
- freshness and fallback posture; and
- correction/deletion state.

The client retains:

- native component choice;
- geometry, typography, spacing, and color;
- exact medium treatment;
- local progressive disclosure;
- animation and gesture behavior;
- accessible layout and focus behavior;
- virtualization and caching implementation; and
- platform-specific degradation.

Tasks:

1. Map every selected visual region to a semantic field or client-owned rule.
2. Delete fields that merely reproduce pixels or visual-tool structure.
3. Add explicit unknown-section and unknown-treatment fallbacks.
4. Define stable keys, revisions, viewer identity, and audience epoch.
5. Define semantic restoration anchors rather than raw scroll offsets.
6. Produce an ownership hypothesis for each field without yet changing the
   backend.

**Exit gate:** the chosen visual composition can be reconstructed from the V0
projection plus client rules, and the V0 projection contains no arbitrary
component tree, coordinates, typography, gesture, or navigation instruction.

### P5 — Native Life composition lab

**Purpose:** determine whether the selected experience survives a real mobile
renderer.

Proposed files, subject to child-repo status review when this phase begins:

```text
travel-app/app/dev/life-composition.tsx
travel-app/constants/mocks/lifeCompositionFixtures.ts
travel-app/utils/lifeComposition.ts
travel-app/utils/lifeProjectionIdentity.ts
travel-app/components/life/index/*
travel-app/components/life/episode/*
travel-app/__tests__/life-composition*.test.tsx
travel-app/maestro/life-composition/*
```

Tasks:

1. Add strict V0 projection decoding and safe fallback.
2. Build separate index and episode renderer boundaries.
3. Use a stable section registry with explicit supported kinds.
4. Mount all six worlds and the three complete episode families.
5. Add independent controls for scope, lifecycle, posture, evidence density,
   environment, and mutation.
6. Preserve the current fixture when one axis changes.
7. Add a screen-reader transcript and semantic-order inspector.
8. Add screenshot mode and deterministic deep links to high-value states.
9. Instrument section mounts, rerenders, restoration anchors, and projection
   identity.

Do not replace the existing list engine until release profiling demonstrates a
real need. Do not turn the current `CanonicalArtifactCard` lab into a larger
prop matrix; reuse plumbing only where it respects the two-renderer boundary.

**Exit gate:** a reviewer can run every representative frame and high-risk
transition in the existing app/dev environment, vary one axis at a time, and
observe stable identity without whole-screen replacement.

### P6 — Continuity, trust, accessibility, and performance certification

**Purpose:** prove the product feels like one governed continuity system rather
than a set of screenshots.

Required journeys:

1. Life index → L01 → Timeline → same L01 target → back to index anchor.
2. Life index → L01 → Map → L04 source bridge → back to L01 anchor.
3. vague search → L03 target → Around this → L01 → back to L03.
4. L02 prospective → live → recent without changing Occasion identity.
5. L02 Together → leave/block/revoke/reconcile with no unauthorized frame.
6. L06 → Places current truth → return to L06 semantic anchor.
7. L01 Continue → Home possibility → return to the originating episode.
8. episode → Chat correction → receipt → corrected episode at the affected
   section.
9. offline relaunch into an authorized Mine episode.
10. offline relaunch into a now-unauthorized Together target.

Required evidence:

- all fourteen social-lifecycle transition oracles;
- all fifty re-finding query oracles;
- stable-key uniqueness;
- no duplicate ResourceRef ownership;
- synchronous Together purge on authority loss;
- no whole-index remount for a leaf revision;
- focus and semantic-anchor recovery;
- complete linear alternatives for map, collage, comparison, and audio;
- 320-point/150%-text evidence;
- Reduce Motion evidence;
- offline, stale, denied-location, partial, and unknown-version fallback; and
- release-build traces for the founder-visible journeys.

Performance is evaluated through time to useful content, dropped frames, JS
and UI stalls, memory, media/network overfetch, rerender count, and semantic
anchor error. Thresholds are selected only after baseline release traces exist.

**Exit gate:** the native lab passes semantic, privacy, accessibility,
continuity, compatibility, and founder-experience review. A polished screenshot

## P2 correction — cluster, timeline, thread, and editorial plan

### 1. Revised canonical composition hypothesis

Life should be tested as a **timeline of evidence-rich clusters connected by
earned threads**:

```text
Life
├── Find and scope
├── Period spine
│   ├── bounded clusters
│   │   ├── Journey
│   │   ├── Occasion
│   │   ├── supported episode
│   │   └── nested Plans, Sources, and corrections
│   └── overlapping semantic lanes inside an opened cluster
├── Threads across time
│   ├── Place relationships
│   ├── cultural or practical relations
│   ├── people and Occasions
│   └── earned recurrences or anomalies
├── bounded editorial Returns
└── contextual utilities
    ├── Sources
    ├── Timeline depth
    └── Map only inside a bounded Place, city, route, or episode
```

This sharpens, rather than replaces, the accepted period-and-episode spine:

- a **cluster** is the visible composition of a bounded episode or period;
- a **lane** is one ordered dimension inside a cluster;
- a **thread** links several governed clusters across time;
- an **artifact/Source** is evidence inside those structures, not normally a
  top-level category card;
- an **editorial Return** is one admitted contribution derived from the record;
  and
- a **Map** is a local explanatory instrument, not the whole-life index.

### 2. Why artifact-type categories are not the primary structure

`Photos`, `Tickets`, `Books`, `Restaurants`, `Receipts`, and `Articles` are
useful retrieval facets. They are weak primary entrances because they organize
by storage form rather than the life context in which the material became
useful.

Life may expose these facets through Sources or search. Its main visible
clusters should remain human-scale:

- a Journey;
- an Occasion;
- a bounded period;
- a recurring Place relationship;
- an earned cross-time thread; or
- a deliberately saved composition.

The system may compile categories automatically, but should not require the
person to file, tag, caption, or maintain them.

### 3. Period spine and overlapping lanes

The default index should compress evidence into chronological clusters. It
must not show a raw event stream.

Opening a rich cluster may reveal several aligned lanes:

```text
PLACE       Nice ─ Sorrento ─ Amalfi ─ Rome ─ New York
PEOPLE      girlfriend ─ shared chapters ─ friends elsewhere
ATTENTION   cliffs ─ pasta ─ heat ─ Trojan/Roman thread
SOURCES     tickets ─ photographs ─ film ─ note ─ citations
TRUTH       planned ─ changed ─ happened ─ unresolved ─ corrected
```

These lanes are a semantic model, not a mandatory five-row visualization. The
composition selects only the lanes that improve reconstruction, comparison,
re-finding, or continuation. A lane that merely repeats the episode summary
stays folded.

Required behavior:

1. The same semantic target persists across cluster, lane, source, and lens.
2. Planned, occurred, corrected, and generated content never share an
   indistinguishable mark.
3. Social lanes retain author and audience; they are not activity streams.
4. Several lanes may overlap without implying causal connection.
5. An inferred line or bridge is itself a claim and requires evidence.
6. The default view remains composed and legible; detailed lanes are
   reader-driven depth.

### 4. Thread eligibility

A thread is earned only when it adds return value beyond its nearest episode.
It is not an inferred interest folder.

A candidate thread must have:

1. at least two independently governed anchors across more than one cluster or
   period;
2. one explicit, computational, or source-grounded relation;
3. a neutral, repairable identity;
4. value beyond a keyword or artifact-type query;
5. current viewer-safe authorship and audience;
6. no identity or emotional claim; and
7. a reason to continue existing after one generated expression expires.

Initial examples:

| Thread | Eligible posture | Failure to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Red Hook** | Supported visits, routes, Occasions, and explicit evaluations across time | `You love Red Hook` or a frequency badge |
| **Film → Rome → later encounters** | Source, authored connection, and later sourced mechanisms | `Mythology person` or restating the Colosseum observation |
| **Coastal movement and cliffs** | Several supported Place/movement anchors plus a real geographic mechanism | A broad travel-style story |
| **Pasta texture and reproduction** | Authored sensory observation, grounded technique, later cooking or comparison | `You are becoming a discerning eater` |
| **Rome–Paris heat** | Two current grants and one substantive common axis | Generic friend activity or pooled history |

L04 may remain a nested relation or saved composition until it meets the
thread gate. L05 remains addressed and revocable rather than a general friend
thread.

### 5. Map boundary

The global Life Map is demoted from a canonical peer view to an optional
orientation utility. Whole-life geography usually compresses lived structure
into distant clusters of dots and suppresses time, people, Sources, Plans, and
meaningful relations.

Map is preferred when it can reveal:

- movement within a city or bounded Journey chapter;
- reachability and route choice;
- repeated visits within a neighborhood;
- the spatial relation between an Occasion and surrounding Places;
- two permissioned lanes within comparable geographic scope; or
- current world truth during a Life-to-Places handoff.

Map is rejected when it produces only:

- countries or cities visited;
- a world heatmap;
- scattered pins with no explanatory relation;
- frequency presented as importance;
- stale provider truth; or
- retrospective location surveillance.

Every spatial treatment requires a complete ordered alternative.

## P2 editorial workstream

### 6. Canonical editorial boundary

The [Vesper Editorial and Content
Canon](../../travel-agent/docs/product/Vesper%20Editorial%20and%20Content%20Canon.md)
is upstream of Life copy and composition. It establishes that Vesper's job is
not to make records sound literary. The person supplies attention and sparse
observation; Vesper must add reconstruction, explanation, differentiation, or
transfer.

Life must keep four value lanes distinct:

1. **Record/canonical state** — factual labels, occurrence truth, current
   membership, Plan state, and source custody;
2. **Operational instrument** — route, current Place handoff, prepared change,
   repair, or other working consequence;
3. **Attributed human contribution** — a note, photograph, perspective,
   decision, or evaluation under its author's scope; and
4. **Vesper editorial contribution** — one admitted piece of new epistemic
   work.

The first three do not need generated prose to feel authored. A cluster title
such as `Nice to Rome · Aug 14–27` may be useful orientation without pretending
to be insight.

### 7. Content-production sequence

Copy is the final compression step, not the starting point:

```text
authorized evidence
  -> known-to-person set
  -> prior exposure check
  -> candidate editorial operation
  -> new claim and mechanism
  -> claim authority and uncertainty
  -> smallest adequate medium
  -> headline and proof
  -> optional consequence or silence
```

For every promoted Life contribution, first complete the canonical contribution
brief:

```yaml
contribution_id: stable fixture identifier
cluster_refs: []
evidence_refs: []
known_to_person_claims: []
prior_exposure_refs: []
new_claim: ""
operation: reconstruction | explanation | contrast | relational_mapping |
  recurrence_anomaly | social_mosaic | consequential_translation
mechanism_or_bridge: ""
claim_authority_tier: occurrence | evidence_world_relation |
  personal_meaning | shared_meaning | operational_consequence
audience_and_scope: ""
provenance: []
uncertainty_and_limits: []
why_now: ""
surface: life
lead_medium: evidence | sequence | comparison | spatial | prose | instrument
supporting_medium: null
visible_trust_footprint: ""
complete_on_view: true
optional_consequence: null
expiry_or_revisit_condition: ""
```

Generation may begin only after the structured brief identifies exactly what
Vesper added.

### 8. Life content anatomy

An accepted contribution may contain five progressively disclosed layers:

1. recognizable context or provenance;
2. one bounded new takeaway;
3. the smallest visible proof;
4. the mechanism or bridge Vesper contributed; and
5. an optional continuation.

These are not five mandatory copy slots. If an annotated receipt, route strip,
comparison, or sequence makes the claim perceptible, do not narrate it again in
prose.

The headline law is:

```text
recognizable premise + new relationship or mechanism + bounded claim
```

Reject:

```text
topic label + poetic mood + personality verdict
```

### 9. Information-gain and anti-parroting gate

Every Vesper-authored contribution must pass:

> If the person could produce the headline and takeaway directly from the
> material they supplied, Vesper has probably not added enough value.

The candidate must perform at least one operation:

- **restructure** fragments into a useful representation;
- **explain** a mechanism or condition;
- **differentiate** cases on a consequential axis; or
- **transfer** earned understanding into another situation.

Hard failures include:

- counts, chronology, or category labels presented as insight;
- a polished restatement of the person's own observation;
- a poetic title without a proposition;
- a generated question that asks the person to supply the missing value;
- a collage whose only message is `this happened`;
- a psychological or identity inference;
- a social element removable without changing understanding or consequence;
- a contribution whose value appears only after a click or reply;
- the same prior contribution under a new headline; or
- prose that narrates Vesper's memory, restraint, or coherence.

### 10. Content roles on the revised Life surface

| Surface region | Content role | Copy posture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Index cluster door** | Orientation and truthful return identity | Descriptive, compact, no claim of insight |
| **Cluster preview** | One reason this cluster is useful to revisit | Prefer visible structure or evidence over summary prose |
| **Overlapping lane** | Reconstruction, distinction, or provenance | Labels and marks carry meaning; no duplicated narration |
| **Thread entrance** | Neutral relation across clusters | Name the relation, not a trait or inferred interest |
| **Editorial Return** | New epistemic operation | Specific headline, visible proof, bounded mechanism |
| **Human contribution** | Attributed attention or perspective | Preserve author and audience; do not synthesize a group voice |
| **Continue** | Prepared present possibility | Concrete effect and destination; optional and complete enough to judge |
| **Source/repair depth** | Trust and correction | Factual, direct, progressively disclosed |
