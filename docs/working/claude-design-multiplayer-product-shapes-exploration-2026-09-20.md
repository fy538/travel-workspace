## 1. Purpose, authority, and how to use this brief

Explore the full multiplayer opportunity discussed on September 19–20, then
identify the smallest coherent product shape capable of delivering the breadth.
The decision this brief enables is **which experiences and interactions deserve
adoption, consolidation, further exploration, or rejection**. It does not select
ten features for implementation or create another engineering roadmap.

Working ambition, not replacement positioning:

> Help people enjoy more of life with—and through—the people they know.

The founder explicitly asked to explore all the directions, rather than narrow
the work to the earlier three proposed canvases. Cover every direction before
making a portfolio-level recommendation. Depth can vary; omissions must be named,
not hidden by a polished showcase of the strongest idea.

## 2. What we are trying to avoid

Three biases would shrink the opportunity prematurely:

- **Artifact bias:** treating participation as only photographs, posts, or saved
  objects. Judgment, effort, hospitality, presence, and help also count.
- **Organizer bias:** assuming someone must become a host before multiplayer is
  useful. Pairs, loose intentions, and welcome company matter too.
- **Utility bias:** requiring every interaction to lead to planning, learning,
  saving, or another action. Enjoyment and feeling involved can be the whole value.

Conversely, breadth must not create social administration. Do not add a new noun,
collection, profile, dashboard, or required onboarding step just because the
strategy can name a behavior. The directions are experiences, not schema entities.

Do not assume a universal funnel from sharing to invitation to activity to memory.
Every direction must have a satisfying independent ending. Seeing a photograph,
receiving advice, declining an invitation, or enjoying a joke can be complete.

## 3. Portfolio map

| Theme | Direction | Starting point | Immediate human payoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Feeling involved | D1 — A little window into friends' lives | Someone shares an ordinary moment | Enjoy awareness without performance or reply debt |
| Feeling involved | D2 — A living shared world | A continuing pair or small circle | Belonging and access to things that matter to us |
| Expanding my world | D3 — Borrowing experience and perspective | A friend's observation or experience | Better understanding or a more usable possibility |
| Expanding my world | D4 — Small acts of help | A bounded offer or need | Give or receive practical care without a big undertaking |
| Being together | D5 — A possibility becomes something we do | An appealing activity | Make the idea happen together with little coordination |
| Being together | D6 — Me and you | A desire to see a particular person | Find something that works for these people now |
| Being together | D7 — Happy for company | Someone is doing something anyway | Allow spontaneous participation without organizing |
| Being together | D8 — Help us enjoy this moment | People are already doing something | Remove friction or enrich the experience, then recede |
| Being together | D9 — Something playful between us | A playful impulse or shared material | Fun, surprise, or a conversation worth having |
| Carrying forward | D10 — The next time is better | A present intention meets useful past evidence | Continuity without starting from zero or keeping a journal |

Important contrasts to preserve during exploration:

- D1 is enjoying a moment; D2 is inhabiting a continuing shared context.
- D5 begins with an activity; D6 begins with a person; D7 begins with willingness
  to have company. They may reuse components without becoming identical flows.
- D3 adapts or connects another person's experience; D4 facilitates a human act
  of assistance. Knowing something does not authorize volunteering its source.
- D8 helps an existing activity; D9 may itself be a deliberately chosen activity.
- D10 changes a future experience; an archive or retrospective alone is not the
  distinguishing payoff, although those can independently be valuable.

## 4. Shared design method

### 4.1 Common worlds, not ten unrelated demos

Use a small recurring cast from the existing fixture world and five situations:

- An ordinary New York weekend with limited time and several plausible options.
- A casual dinner or cooking experiment with uneven participant effort.
- Friends experiencing different places, including the Italy/Paris juxtaposition.
- A walk or cultural outing with differing interests, energy, and attention.
- A later return to a place, recipe, question, or shared activity.

Do not invent precise current opening hours, availability, booking access, or
real-world attendance as verified facts. Label supplied fixture evidence and any
simulated current-world checks. Do not quietly merge inconsistent earlier fixtures.

Across the portfolio, demonstrate credible value with one friend and little shared
history. Do not impose that requirement on every direction: a recurring circle
may genuinely need several people, and accumulated continuity needs prior evidence.
Name the minimum context each benefit requires and distinguish immediate value
from what richer context unlocks. Do not simulate day-one knowledge to make a
mature benefit appear instantly available. Sparse-context users still receive a
complete useful experience elsewhere; they are not defective or unfinished.

### 4.2 Experience canvas first; review notes alongside

The **experience canvas** should make four things tangible: the starting situation,
the actual received value, the essential interaction from the involved people's
perspectives, and a natural ending. Use complete example content, not labels such
as "personalized recommendation" or "AI insight." Show necessary sender effort,
but do not turn each canvas into a nine-part procedural poster.

Keep **review notes** alongside it: known/unknown inputs and their permitted use;
setup and effort for each person; state/audience consequences; low-participation
outcomes; one meaningful change or failure; an honest ordinary-app comparison;
and the unresolved question. Notes can refer to shared controls or reused frames.
Draw a separate failure/decline screen only when the interaction itself needs
exploration, rather than illustrating every audit item on every board. Keep D3's
distinct value variants and D7's three outcomes legible without duplicating setup.

Count effort for each participant separately: input, reading, choosing, correcting,
setup, and coordination. Do not call an interface effortless because it transfers
all the work to a host. Any interaction count is a fixture walkthrough estimate,
not measured user performance. Unequal effort is acceptable; unpaid obligation is
not a hidden success condition.

### 4.3 Three ways value arrives

Across relevant directions, compare:

- **Already there:** useful, authorized value is present in Home or Places when
  opened. Receiving it does not require another question, selection exercise,
  contribution, or invitation.
- **Asked for:** a person actively asks and receives a useful answer or result.
- **Changed situation:** new information makes an existing shared situation
  materially different; Vesper returns an appropriate update or prepared option.

Do not force all three into every story. Make D2's unprompted value and D8's
changed-situation response concrete so the exploration does not become ten ways
to ask a chatbot. Explain the trigger, eligible evidence, and why now in review
notes. Showing value on opening is not automatically permission for push alerts.
Proactive value does not authorize contacting friends, inferring willingness,
revealing private reasons, changing commitments, or widening an audience. A fresh
private option and an applied shared change are different consequences.

### 4.4 Composition constraints

- Preserve Home / Chat / Places / Life. Use only the surfaces relevant to a story.
- Home and Places return substantial value; Chat remains an easy place to ask,
  bring, and steer; Life makes retained things findable. These are dominant roles,
  not exclusive capabilities or one grammar per tab.
- Show the original contribution prominently where it is the value. AI context
  is optional and must add substance; original-first does not mean original-only.
- Preserve author voice and distinguish source observation, verified world fact,
  adaptation, and hypothesis. No inferred personality narration.
- No automatic sharing of a private question, inferred attendance, inferred
  willingness, merged group taste profile, or involuntary durable memory.
- Ordinary replies and optional explicitly sent prepared messages need not become
  a new conversation system. Private Ask must remain distinct from human Reply.
- Do not add viewer reporting, public response scores, contribution quotas,
  streaks, friendship health scores, or pressure to repay a contribution.
- Richness need not mean long text. Choose photographs, maps, short answers,
  compact comparisons, practical objects, or audio when the medium earns its cost.
- Silence or a closed app can be a successful ending. Do not require a save,
  invitation, feedback response, or permanent artifact to count the value.

## 5. The ten exploration directions

### D1 — A little window into my friends' lives

**Desire:** I want to know what my friends are experiencing without the performance
or obligation. **So what?** An ordinary moment makes me feel a little more involved
without needing a full catch-up. This is a consumer hypothesis, not a universal need.

**Proposed scene:** Maya shares a photograph of an imperfect cake and one line.
Sam shares a small cinema discovery. Home returns recognizable people and their
actual contributions, not several AI essays about what their lives mean.

**Explore:** direct photo sharing and sharing an existing Chat-supported object;
recipient/audience selection; recipient's immediate view; optional Reply and
private Ask; a return visit where nothing requires attention. Compare original
alone with one genuinely useful optional context layer. A funny photograph can
win without any AI addition.

**Both sides:** sharing is motivated by humor, relevance, affection, or wanting
someone to know—not by maintaining a feed. The recipient may simply enjoy it.
No reply is a normal complete outcome; the sender gets no private-use report.

**Baseline and rejection test:** a group message may be equally good for the cake.
Do not invent superiority. Vesper's additional value may appear only when Sam's
place becomes useful later, and even that cannot justify making the original
exchange harder. Reject a copy of a conventional feed with generic AI commentary.

**Likely surfaces:** Home, contextual sharing/Chat, optionally Places or retained
Life material under its existing rules. **Question:** can this feel socially alive
without pushing people to produce more?

### D2 — A living shared world for a small circle

**Desire:** Some things belong to our continuing friendship, not one event or post.
**So what?** Things we care about remain available without reconstructing message
history or assigning someone to curate a shared workspace.

**Proposed scenes:** a few friends have mentioned places, shared a dish, and had a
casual dinner. In one scene, someone asks, "What were those places we were talking
about?" In a second, nobody asks: Home or Places already contains something worth
receiving. For example, a friend's deliberately shared variation on the dish
appears beside the original, with the actual difference and a short, usable method.
Or new shared place knowledge makes a previously awkward afternoon possible.
Show the contribution and its present payoff, not "new activity in your circle."
No one needs to search, maintain a collection, or arrange another gathering to
benefit. Retrieval alone does not demonstrate an inhabited shared world.

**Explore:** a view assembled on demand versus a persistent circle doorway; an
ordinary Home/Places composition enriched by shared contributions; finding an
original from Life; and what several people's contributions add beyond a pair.
Compare whether an explicit group surface improves enjoyment or usefulness,
rather than assuming a persistent group destination is necessary.

**Both sides:** one person can contribute once; another can browse or act. No one
has to label every item, fill a circle profile, or maintain a group database.
Show an inactive circle without guilt copy and an audience/membership change
without assuming newcomers can see all historical material.

**Baseline and rejection test:** messaging search, pinned items, and shared lists
already help. The design must improve retrieval, relevance, or enjoyment, not
simply relocate them. Reject a "friendship workspace" whose upkeep exceeds value.

**Likely surfaces:** Life relationship/context views, Home, Places, Chat retrieval.
**Question:** does the circle feel inhabited, or administered? Membership and
historical access policy remain explicit dependencies, not decisions made by a mockup.

### D3 — Borrowing experience and perspective

**Desire:** Show me something I would not find, understand, or confidently try alone.
**So what?** A known person's contribution supplies a perspective or usable detail;
Vesper does work beyond repeating it.

**Variant A, practical:** "Which of Maya's shared places would work for a relaxed
afternoon with my parents?" Use her actual observations, recipient constraints,
and clearly labeled current information. Preserve what Maya did not establish.

**Variant B, perceptual:** a user's Sorrento cliff observation and a friend's
coastal photograph become a compact, researched comparison of visible features
and their formation. The payoff is something the viewer did not already notice,
not "you both love dramatic landscapes." The Italy/Paris example can also explore
contrasting experiences without claiming false equivalence.

**Explore:** original alone; useful context beside one original; two originals
with an earned connection. Include a case where no meaningful connection exists
and the product simply presents the original. Show private recipient adaptation
without exposing that person's constraints to the original contributor.

**Both sides:** a friend's one-time contribution can help without making them a
permanent adviser. The recipient need not report using it. A voluntary human
return message remains possible, not a repayment step.

**Baseline and rejection test:** saved pins plus a standalone AI answer are capable
alternatives. Identify exactly what source-specific detail changes the result.
Reject superficial matches, stale practical claims, or borrowing beyond audience.

**Likely surfaces:** Places, Home, original reader, contextual Chat.
**Question:** does the connection justify the additional attention and generation?

### D4 — Giving and receiving small acts of help

**Desire:** I can offer or receive something useful without a big undertaking.
**So what?** Participation can be care, knowledge, effort, or hospitality—not content.

**Proposed scenes:** "I made extra sauce; anyone want some?" and "Has anyone taken
their parents somewhere nearby that was genuinely comfortable?" Compare a bounded
offer and a bounded request to a chosen audience.

**Explore:** initiate from ordinary language; select actual recipients; show useful
receiving; allow a short human answer or acceptance; settle only necessary pickup
or coordination details. Mark an offer no longer available without requiring
everyone to revisit a management screen. Do not volunteer a knowledgeable friend.

**Both sides:** the contributor can give one useful answer, then stop. A recipient
can benefit without equivalent contribution. No answer must remain a socially
ordinary possibility; no escalation or reminder on the recipient's behalf.

**Baseline and rejection test:** a group message may already solve the problem.
Vesper must remove real detail-reconstruction or practical work, not create a
request queue. Food examples do not imply verified safety or suitability.

**Likely surfaces:** Home receiving, Chat initiation, contextual object and optional
Place details. **Question:** can this be warmly useful without becoming a marketplace
or a permanent obligation attached to someone's expertise?

### D5 — A possibility becomes something we do

**Desire:** That looks worthwhile; I would like to do it with someone.
**So what?** An appealing possibility survives contact with another person's needs.

**Proposed scene:** a relaxed Sunday walk and market stop looks useful alone. The
user shares "Fancy this?" A friend answers, "Yes, but I need to be back by four."
The existing possibility becomes a workable arrangement without restarting.

**Explore:** valuable solo option; optional invitation; actual recipient response;
the smallest necessary clarification; a concrete mutual arrangement; a changed
condition. Show exactly when an idea becomes a commitment and who agreed to what.
Loose timing is appropriate until a reservation, deadline, or mutual reliance
requires precision. Do not turn ambiguity into an invisible promise.

**Both sides:** count the initiator's input and the friend's effort. Declining does
not spoil the original option or cause repeated invitations. Interest does not
silently create an Occasion, calendar entry, or confirmed attendance.

**Baseline and rejection test:** people can send a link and a message. Vesper's
advantage must be useful assembly, adaptation, or continuity—not merely rendering
that message as a card. Reject instant event forms and an itinerary bureaucracy.

**Likely surfaces:** Home or Places → addressed object/Chat → necessary Plan view.
**Question:** does this reduce both imagining and arranging work?

### D6 — Me and you

**Desire:** I want to see this particular person; help us find something good.
**So what?** The relationship comes first, rather than adding a friend to a solo
recommendation after the fact.

**Proposed scene:** "What would be good for Alex and me on Saturday?" One wants
novelty, the other something undemanding. Present a coherent option, not an average
of inferred preferences or a compatibility score.

**Explore:** a sparse-history version using what the initiator actually knows;
a richer explicitly shared-context version; optional private contribution from
the other person; group-safe options; and a case where no mutually acceptable
option emerges. Vesper cannot know a friend's private current needs just because
the initiator named them. Clearly distinguish likely fit from mutual interest.

**Both sides:** each may steer without publicly explaining sensitive constraints.
No compulsory bilateral interview. Show a lightweight path when only one person
uses the app and clearly mark any external/guest path as supported or conditional.

**Baseline and rejection test:** "What do you fancy?" often works. The result must
be more useful than that conversation, not an opaque optimization replacing it.
Reject relationship dashboards, lowest-common-denominator recommendations, and
private-reason leakage through overly specific public explanations.

**Likely surfaces:** Chat, Home possibilities, Places, a shared object when needed.
**Question:** what changes because these particular people are involved?

### D7 — Happy for company

**Desire:** I am doing something anyway; company could be nice.
**So what?** Express willingness without recruiting guests or becoming a host.

**Proposed scene:** "Getting coffee and wandering Sunday afternoon. Happy for
company." Selected friends see an expiring, bounded opening, not a live tracking
signal or calendar-derived claim of availability.

**Explore all three outcomes:** someone joins; someone is interested but cannot
come; nobody responds. In the first, show what becomes agreed and how details are
shared. In the third, the person simply enjoys the original activity. Compare
a contextual shared expression with a separate status control; do not assume
we need a new persistent availability object.

**Both sides:** audience is deliberate; responses can be private; precise location
is not exposed by default. The sender can close or revise the opening. Explain
expiry without silently canceling a separately confirmed commitment.

**Baseline and rejection test:** this can be an ordinary message. The potential
benefit is useful timing and bounded receiving, not amplifying reach or publicly
displaying interest. Reject viewer lists, response counts, inferred willingness,
or an unanswered opening that reads as social failure.

**Likely surfaces:** Home, contextual sharing/status, private response, Places only
where relevant. **Question:** does this lower pressure or merely move rejection
anxiety into a different interface? Audience/expiry policy must be reviewed.

### D8 — Help us enjoy what we are already doing

**Desire:** Help with this moment, then let us continue.
**So what?** A useful intervention removes an obstacle or returns attention to the
world and the people present. Curiosity and practical assistance are one experience.

**Proposed scenes:** fixing a sauce while cooking; understanding a detail on a
walk; finding somewhere to rest; splitting briefly at a museum and rejoining.
At least one scene must combine enrichment with practical conditions rather than
showing a detached trivia answer next to a separate logistics product.

**Explore:** compare a short initiated question with a response to a material
change in an already established situation. For example, a relevant closure
changes an agreed outing: Vesper presents a usable alternative based on eligible
current information, rather than waiting for another request or silently changing
the agreement. Show the actual result, optional depth, and a natural ending.
Show differing needs without compulsory synchronization.
One person can operate the app while several benefit. Demonstrate the appropriate
shared consequence when an arrangement changes, not just a new suggestion.

**Both sides:** avoid synchronized onboarding or everyone carrying an active UI.
A person may choose not to hear the explanation. Practical assistance must still
work when there is no interesting content to generate.

**Baseline and rejection test:** ordinary search and maps may already help. Show
what existing context and coherent follow-through save. Reject persistent
narration, unsolicited tasks, or keeping everyone on their phones.

**Likely surfaces:** contextual Chat, Places, Home and the existing arrangement.
**Question:** does the intervention improve the activity enough to justify its
attention cost? A separate "together mode" is a hypothesis, not a requirement.

### D9 — Something playful between us

**Desire:** That was fun and gave us something to enjoy together.
**So what?** Enjoyment can be the complete outcome; no learning or planning required.

**Proposed scenes:** contrasting what two people noticed on the same walk; a
small deliberately left surprise; amusing differences from a cooking experiment.
Compare play emerging from existing material with a short activity people opt
into. Do not claim an untested activity is fun because its presentation is charming.

**Explore:** the invitation or emergence, the enjoyable content itself, another
person's participation, and a clean ending. Include a recipient who does not
participate. Keep it possible to enjoy without submitting a matching photograph.
Surprises must not widen audience or expose location without authorization.

**Both sides:** asymmetric participation is normal; no homework such as mandatory
collections, quizzes, or repeated captures. More involved play can be deliberately
chosen, but must not become the default tax on a normal outing.

**Baseline and rejection test:** friends already joke and play in messages. Identify
what the medium or capability contributes. Reject streaks, leaderboards, forced
icebreakers, and an engagement mechanic disguised as friendship.

**Likely surfaces:** contextual object, Home receiving, optionally Places/Chat.
**Question:** would someone voluntarily do it a second time without a reminder?
That requires human evidence eventually; the design alone cannot answer it.

### D10 — The next time is better

**Desire:** We should not have to start from zero every time.
**So what?** Useful or meaningful pieces of shared life remain available when they
can improve the present, without maintaining a shared journal.

**Proposed scene:** another dinner uses the actual recipe variation, a friend's
substitution, and a previously mentioned timing adjustment. Alternatively, a return
to a neighborhood surfaces something not tried last time and a new friend's tip.

**Explore:** sparse evidence from the earlier experience; permitted retention;
a later trigger; the specific difference in the new experience; source inspection
or correction; and an ending where nothing is renewed. Show both emotional and
practical continuity without making every memory an action prompt.

**Both sides:** one photograph or useful message can suffice. Sources remain
attributed and interpretations can differ. Past attendance, availability, guest
lists, and preferences do not silently become current commitments. A new group
does not automatically inherit the old group's private material.

**Baseline and rejection test:** searching messages and photo albums is capable.
Show how Vesper makes the relevant lesson easier to use, not merely a prettier
recap. Reject compulsory debriefs, comprehensive capture, and identity claims.

**Likely surfaces:** Life for retained originals/context; Home for earned relevance;
Chat for retrieval and use; Places where spatial. **Question:** what is actually
better next time, and what would still work with only one small retained piece?

## 6. Cross-root and object coherence

| Root | Multiplayer contribution | What not to turn it into |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home | Enjoyable receiving, shared possibilities, invitations, relevant developments | A queue demanding replies or ten permanent social sections |
| Chat | Easy asking, bringing, expressing intent, proposing, steering, and resolving | A form-driven setup funnel or every friend's compulsory AI conversation |
| Places | Human perspectives, spatially practical options, appropriately shared place context | Live friend surveillance or a generic map with avatars |
| Life | Findable originals, shared occasions, relationship context, and useful continuity | A friendship CRM or a mandatory collaborative journal |

Reuse existing objects and focused views where they suffice. A contribution can
appear on several roots without becoming several copies. A proposal can remain
lightweight until a real commitment needs its owner. A circle is not automatically
a new durable object. A playful exchange need not persist at all.

The four product moves—Make sense, Open possibility, Help it work, Carry forward—
are cross-cutting capabilities, not four stages everyone must complete and not
four tab assignments. D1 may stop at enjoyment. D8 may be almost entirely practical.
D3 may produce understanding without an invitation. Preserve that freedom.

## 7. Questions for the design review

For each direction, answer in plain language:

1. **So what?** What did the person receive, feel able to do, or avoid doing?
2. **Would someone want this without AI novelty?** Name the credible motivation
   and the equally credible reason to ignore it.
3. **Why this product?** Is this a basic interaction, a differentiating benefit,
   or part of a whole-product advantage? Use the comparison standard below.
4. **Who pays the effort?** Include setup, contributing, reading, deciding, and
   correcting. Does a beautiful receiving screen conceal a burdensome sender path?
5. **What participation and context are necessary?** Identify the minimum for this
   benefit, the portfolio's low-context value, and what happens without a reply or
   installed app. Do not fictionalize unavailable services or rich history.
6. **Does the AI add substance?** Or is a human original the better output?
7. **Does it preserve autonomy?** Can people decline, diverge, correct, or stop
   without social penalties or new administrative work?
8. **Does it fit the whole app?** Identify shared components and owners, not just
   the attractiveness of one isolated screen.
9. **What remains a hypothesis?** Distinguish design coherence, user preference,
   implemented behavior, service policy, and long-term demand.

Evaluate the ordinary-app comparison at three levels:

- **Basic interactions** such as sending a photograph or replying should be easy,
  familiar, and no more burdensome than the alternative. They need not be unique.
- **Differentiating benefits** such as a substantive perspective connection or a
  well-adapted shared option must demonstrate what Vesper adds. A restyled card
  does not establish an advantage.
- **System advantage** must be visible across experiences: less reconstruction,
  better continuity, and useful context improving what someone receives or does.
  Do not assume this advantage merely because the components share a database.

Credit capable messaging, maps, and photo tools with search, saved information,
and human judgment. But do not reject a necessary familiar exchange simply because
it could also happen in a group chat. Keep per-direction rejection tests focused
on the distinctive benefit being claimed, not novelty in every button or message.

Use a qualitative disposition: **retain**, **merge**, **rework**, **defer**, or
**reject**, with a reason and the next unanswered question. Do not create arbitrary
numeric psychology scores or an overall score that hides a serious tradeoff.

Promising design evidence includes a recognizable benefit, readable composition,
clear consequences, and a believable low-effort path. Actual adoption, friendship
benefits, spontaneity, retention, and repeat enjoyment remain empirical questions.
More messages, more artifacts, and more time in the app are not sufficient success
measures. Recipient benefit without a reply is not failure.

## 9. Research basis and limits

The following sources informed the preceding discussion; they support mechanisms
and questions, not a claim that these ten designs have product-market fit. Most
direction-level psychological explanations above are design hypotheses.

- [The surprise of reaching out](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35816566/):
  preregistered experiments found people underestimated appreciation of contact.
  Relevant to D5–D7's hesitation, not authorization for automated outreach or
  friendship-maintenance nudges.
- [The Calendar Mindset](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmr.14.0591):
  13 studies found scheduling could make studied leisure activities feel work-like;
  rough scheduling avoided the effect. Relevant to progressive commitment, not a
  blanket claim that precise plans are undesirable.
- [Wanting without enjoying](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6472755/):
  preference for sharing an experience did not reliably imply increased enjoyment.
  A social version must earn its value rather than assume together is always better.
- [CHI 2021 proceedings: MomentMeld](https://chi2021.acm.org/proceedings):
  cross-generational photographic juxtaposition is a relevant precedent for D3.
  It does not validate arbitrary AI connections or generalize automatically to
  young friends, tourism, or every kind of shared image.
- [Revisiting the Visit](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~beki/c22.pdf): museum-guide
  research shows both enriched shared experience and attention/interaction costs.
  Supports exploring independent attention, reconnection, and differing visit styles.
- [From Memory to Meaning, CHI 2026](https://www.mingmingfan.com/papers/CHI26-ReminiscenceReview.pdf):
  a review of 60 papers identifies comparatively less emphasis on memory's practical
  problem-solving role. Relevant to D10; an underexplored topic is not proof of demand.
- [Remini, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03355v1): a 48-participant mutual
  reminiscence study provides useful relational hypotheses, but sessions averaged
  about 107 minutes versus 54 for baseline. Do not import a lengthy reflection
  protocol into an effortless everyday promise or attribute effects solely to AI.
- [Generative AI and social media, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-40110-8):
  a 680-participant forum experiment found that some AI tools increased activity
  while reducing perceived quality/authenticity. This is not close-friend app
  evidence, but cautions against equating generated conversation with social value.

### 12.5 Research and competitive corrections

Research checked September 21, 2026. Product descriptions establish advertised
capabilities, not independent effectiveness. Studies support questions and
mechanisms, not adoption forecasts for Vesper; the examples above are hypotheses.

- **Low-pressure sharing is not unique.** [Marco Polo](https://www.marcopolo.me/)
  positions around personal communication without ads or likes, and
  [Locket's product listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/locket-widget/id1600525061)
  describes friends' photos and uncounted reactions. Compare our ordinary-sharing
  experience on ease and human value; do not claim its absence of public metrics
  alone is a moat.
- **Private answers and post-event connection already exist.**
  [Partiful questionnaires](https://help.partiful.com/en-us/articles/15525422-can-i-poll-or-survey-my-guests)
  can be visible only to the host; its
  [event experience](https://help.partiful.com/en-us/articles/15525594-why-use-partiful)
  includes shared photos and ways to connect afterward. Correct claims that event
  tools stop at the door. Host-visible answers are not the same as hidden-source
  constraints shaping a safe result; explain the actual difference.
- **A friendship may span multiple tools.**
  [Lehmann and Utz](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/14614448251351282),
  online 2025 / journal issue 2026, found high connectedness across different
  media-use patterns in friend groups. The survey is observational. Our inference
  is to support existing channels and unequal adoption, not require friends to
  move their communication into Vesper.
- **Shared activity can be the social medium.**
  [Spotify Jam](https://support.spotify.com/us/article/jam/) lets people shape a
  shared listening queue. [CHI 2026: Characterising Gaming Group Experiences](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791223)
  reports interviews with fifteen long-term players whose groups shift between
  task- and people-oriented motivations. This is a useful analogy for shared
  making, not proof that a Vesper project feature is wanted.
- **Together need not mean simultaneous.**
  [Queue Player, CHI 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3714293) explores
  distributed co-listening among established friends/family. Treat it as a design
  precedent, not broad market validation or a requirement to build playback.
- **Help has value and pressure.**
  [Zhao and Epley](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36067802/) found that help-seekers
  underestimated willingness and positive feelings about helping across studies
  involving 2,118 US adults. [Bohns's research overview](https://www.vanessabohns.com/research)
  also cautions that requesters underestimate difficulty declining. Support
  bounded requests and real refusal, not automated requests or presumed goodwill.
  [Meal Train](https://www.mealtrain.com/features/) is an adjacent precedent for
  coordinating useful help with recipient preferences, not a service to copy whole.
- **Authored response differs from telemetry.**
  [Gable and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15301629/) associate sharing
  positive events and constructive human responses with personal/relational
  benefits. This does not validate synthetic congratulations, reply pressure or
  passive-use reporting.
- **A prompt alone may not resolve social hesitation.** A
  [2024 study of contacting old friends](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39242759/)
  found fewer than one-third contacted an old friend even in favorable conditions
  in two studies. Do not present a suggested introduction or reconnection prompt
  as a proven relationship outcome.

### 13.5 Research basis and limits for these refinements

Sources were reviewed for the September 21 discussion. These findings motivate
design comparisons; they do not prove adoption, improved friendships, or that a
specific friend welcomes contact.

- [Liu and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35816566/) found people
  underestimated recipients' appreciation of reaching out. The
  [liking-gap studies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30183512/) found people often
  underestimated conversation partners' positive impressions. Provide truthful
  context and human-authored openings, not AI assurances that someone likes you.
- [Givi and Kirk](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38079463/) found invitees
  overestimated negative consequences of declining. Explore clear refusal and
  partial participation; do not claim every refusal is harmless or silent expiry
  is appropriate after agreement.
- [Burke and Kraut](https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article/21/4/265/4161784)
  associated targeted composed communication from strong ties with better
  well-being, unlike the same pattern for broadcasts or one-click feedback.
  This is not proof that small acknowledgments are worthless. In
  [Huh's 2025 experiment](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0326189),
  260 participants rated hypothetical friend replies with emojis as more
  responsive than text alone. These are perceptions of imagined exchanges, not
  longitudinal friendship outcomes. Explore private warmth without public metrics.
- [Digital stress and friendship conflict, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11959014/)
  examines availability expectations, digital stress and conflict in adolescents.
  Treat adult generalization as unresolved. Receiving should not establish an
  immediate response obligation, and silence must not trigger relationship repair.

products already support real friendship, discovery and coordination; compare

The four candidate gaps in §14.4 are not proven market whitespace or an exclusive
launch wedge. Their priority is for this design comparison, not a new roadmap.

### 14.2 Research findings translated into design questions

Research reviewed September 21, 2026. Keep empirical findings, product hypotheses
and advertised capabilities distinct. Small qualitative studies and adolescent
samples do not establish general adult demand; associations do not establish
causation or certify Vesper's outcomes.

- **Receiving is not failed participation.** A [2024 meta-analysis of 141
  studies, approximately 145,000 participants](https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article/29/1/zmad055/7595758)
  found most active/passive-use associations with well-being were negligible;
  context, including group versus general use, mattered. Do not equate posting
  with health or browsing with harm. Draw enjoyment, awareness, exchange and
  shared action as distinct valid benefits, not an engagement ladder everyone
  must climb.
- **Small human exchanges need not prove practical usefulness.** [Meme-sharing
  research](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/12789) associated
  sharing for enjoyment with relationship satisfaction in an online survey.
  Preserve a joke, photograph or small affectionate reply as a complete result.
  Demand substance from added AI output, not productivity from friendship.
- **Remembering who shared what is a useful social function.** [Three
  preregistered experiments, N=445](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/ambient-awareness-of-who-knows-what-spontaneous-inferences-of-dom/)
  support spontaneous inferences about others' expertise from posts. They do not
  prove those inferences are accurate. Recover the relevant shared note and its
  author, not an inferred expert label, personality profile or automatic request
  for that person's help.
- **Shared activity can be a medium for connection.** [Co-design research with
  23 people aged 15–24, revised January 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06696)
  contrasts conventional social platforms with meaningful connection participants
  described in Minecraft/Discord-like spaces. Explore playful involvement in
  something together, not another virtual world or compulsory collaborative
  project. This is qualitative inspiration, not demonstrated adoption.
- **Authenticity must not become a performance assignment.** [Interviews with
  29 adolescent BeReal users](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02883) found reduced
  self-presentation pressure alongside limitations and sometimes negative
  experiences. Do not require posting to receive, impose a daily candid moment,
  shame lateness, or treat a carefully chosen photograph as inherently inauthentic.
- **Audience comfort changes what people share.** A [replication in the 2026
  Journal of Communication issue](https://academic.oup.com/joc/article/76/2/174/8177321)
  reproduced audience-related findings about disclosure and privacy-setting use.
  Keep audience legible without repeated list-management work. Compare a bounded
  circle share with a direct send: the hypothesis that a direct message can feel
  more demanding needs testing, not a universal one-to-one default.
