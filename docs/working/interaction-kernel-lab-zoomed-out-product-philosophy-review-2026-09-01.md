# Interaction Kernel Lab — Zoomed-Out Product-Philosophy Review

## 0. Executive verdict

**The design is on the right track as an interaction subsystem. It is not yet
the right center of gravity for Vesper as a product.**

The lab has found a strong grammar for what happens when a person wants to
shape, authorize, correct, or recover something already present:

```text
visible semantic context
  -> brief human expression
  -> smallest typed owner command
  -> proportional consequence boundary
  -> owner-backed readback
  -> local repair, takeover, or leave
```

That grammar is unusually well aligned with Vesper's ambitions. It avoids both
form-heavy administration and chat-only state ambiguity. It takes multiplayer
authority, partial execution, correction, and refinding seriously. Those are
architecture-bearing strengths.

The philosophical overreach is treating this as the whole product's
interaction grammar. The most differentiated post-pivot Vesper experience
begins earlier:

```text
the person lives, notices, asks, brings, or encounters
  -> Vesper performs intellectual or practical work
  -> Vesper returns complete, substantive value in a native form
  -> only then, when useful, the person may shape or authorize what follows
```

Home and Places should frequently succeed without any interaction. Life should
make continuity, reconstruction, and refinding valuable without assigning
reflection work. Chat should make contribution and agency unusually easy. The
current lab begins primarily at the third line—after useful state or a candidate
object already exists—and therefore studies the collaboration cost more than
the quality of Vesper's contribution.

The correct ruling is:

> **Keep the kernel. Narrow its authority. Put value return before it.**

The sentence **“Point at the thing. Say only what is missing.”** is excellent
for editing, coordination, and consequence. It is incomplete as the product's
opening philosophy. A fuller experiential sentence is:

> **Vesper should first return something worth having. If the person wants to
> shape or use it, they can point at what matters, say only what is missing,
> see the exact consequence, and leave without setup debt.**

## 1. What changed in the product philosophy over the last two weeks

The lab can be judged correctly only against the sequence of the August pivot.
The conversations and documents did not simply add more features. They changed
what the product is for.

### 1.1 From travel task completion to lived-world intelligence

The earlier center was trip planning and operational assistance. The emerging
center is the evolving, permissioned relationship among a person, other people,
places, and the world across time.

The four product moves became the north star:

1. **Make sense.** Add reconstruction, explanation, contrast, or clarified
   judgment rather than repeating the person's trace.
2. **Open possibility.** Make a grounded way of noticing, entering, doing, or
   continuing newly available.
3. **Help it work.** Absorb practical and coordinative burden without absorbing
   human authorship or authority.
4. **Carry forward.** Preserve only evidence, intent, outcomes, or authored
   meaning that can materially improve what comes next.

These are value responsibilities, not interface steps and not four separate
features.

### 1.2 From inputs and stored artifacts to an editorial transaction

The Europe stories sharpened a key asymmetry:

- the person is already doing the work of living and directing attention;
- their photos, tickets, questions, observations, messages, books, films, and
  Places are source material;
- Home is part of the return on that contribution, not another request for
  reflection or classification.

The Granola analogy became important: the person participates in life and
leaves incomplete traces; Vesper should return a better account or a useful
consequence. Unlike a meeting transcript, lived experience is incomplete,
plural, and permissioned, so Vesper must be conservative about provenance,
personal meaning, and memory.

This is why “What do you not want to lose?” failed. It outsourced the editorial
work back to the person. It is also why “Rome adopted the survivor's side of
the Trojan War” failed: it restated a connection the person already made rather
than adding a mechanism they did not know.

### 1.3 From cards and features to expression, medium, and projection

The recent dynamic-artifact work separated:

- canonical truth and Sources;
- Vesper's admitted contribution;
- semantic result family;
- medium and composition;
- viewer-relative projection;
- durable owner;
- optional consequence.

An Artifact is something brought, captured, received, or made. A Composition is
a viewer-safe return assembled from authorized evidence. An Instrument is
stateful and consequential. Direct State, Receipt, Owner Link, Short Prose, and
Silence remain distinct result families. None should be forced into one
universal card or one universal interaction model.

This is directly relevant to the lab: the lab's “shared object” is sometimes a
Composition, sometimes an Instrument, sometimes direct owner state, and
sometimes only a projection. Calling all of these “the object” hides important
differences in persistence, freshness, authority, and interaction.

### 1.4 From one interface to four stable root postures

The current root model is:

- **Home:** temporal composition and selective value return in relation to life
  now;
- **Chat:** contribution, questioning, challenge, authoring, and agency;
- **Places:** spatial legibility, current world truth, and ways into the world;
- **Life:** evidence-rich continuity, organization, reconstruction, refinding,
  and correction.

Plans and Occasions own bounded consequence and participation. They are not
additional product roots. The same semantic identity can circulate through
several projections without copying truth or turning every root into the same
card surface.

### 1.5 From social features to permissioned multiplayer intelligence

The multiplayer work rejected a social feed, Occasion profile, and generic
shared workspace as the center. Instead:

- people contribute ordinary evidence and perspective;
- authorship stays visible;
- private context can safely improve a shared result without being revealed;
- host, contributor, participant, and provider authority remain distinct;
- withdrawal and correction invalidate only dependent expressions;
- one occurrence can have several subjective Outcomes;
- a friend's trace matters only when it changes understanding, possibility,
  coordination, or relational continuity.

B2 captures some of this well, but only in one narrow dinner coordination
case. It does not yet represent the broader social thesis: attributed
juxtaposition, asynchronous Place knowledge, relationship-conditioned
openings, or plural memory.

### 1.6 From “reduce taps” to “reduce total collaboration cost”

The HCI research corrected the optimization target. Ease includes:

```text
expression + orientation + representation + verification
+ coordination + correction + consequence risk
```

Mechanical and schema friction should be eliminated aggressively. Commitment,
social, and reflective friction should remain only when it constitutes real
human judgment. The lab embodies this distinction well—but its current metrics
overweight the measurable interaction path and underweight whether the returned
value was substantive, novel, timely, and worth the person's attention.

## 2. What the lab gets philosophically right

### 2.1 It refuses the false choice between forms and chat

The hybrid hypothesis is sound:

- touch is good at reference, scope, ordering, and binary choice;
- language is good at motive, ambiguity, exception, relation, and tradeoff;
- native expression is good at current truth, comparison, action, and recovery;
- Vesper should absorb translation and structure.

This fits the product better than either a form-centered itinerary editor or a
chat transcript pretending to be the durable plan.

### 2.2 It treats semantic continuity as more important than visual continuity

The requirement is not that Chat and direct controls remain visible together.
It is that both address the same owner command and that the changed owner
becomes the readback. This is exactly the right architectural seam for Home,
Chat, Places, Life, Plan, and Occasion to feel like one intelligence rather
than adjacent products.

### 2.3 It preserves friction at real boundaries

The lab correctly distinguishes:

- private reversible shaping;
- shared or audience-changing action;
- provider and money consequences;
- partial execution;
- Stop and takeover;
- correction after a consequence.

The “one exact preview at the material boundary” rule is one of the strongest
parts of the design. It avoids both ritual confirmation and dangerous ambient
delegation.

### 2.4 It makes current truth recoverable

D2's core question—whether consequential truth should rest in a transcript or
in a live owner-backed instrument—is genuinely important. Vesper will often
operate across several providers and people. A fluent assistant message is not
enough. The product must keep verified, pending, failed, proposed, and
user-owned states legible at rest.

### 2.5 It takes withdrawal and causal repair seriously

B2's distinction between removing advice and withdrawing an entire
recommendation is philosophically aligned with the multiplayer canon. A later
withdrawal should not rewrite history, erase independent truth, or silently
collapse a settled plan. This is more mature than ordinary collaboration UI.

### 2.6 It lets a person leave

“Repair or leave” matters. Vesper should not create setup debt, completion
pressure, or a sense that a person has failed to maintain their life in the
app. The lab repeatedly protects ignoring, leaving, stopping, and taking over
as legitimate endings.

## 3. Where the lab is philosophically too narrow or mispositioned

### 3.1 It starts with collaboration before proving contribution

The lab mostly assumes that a useful shape, Occasion, or recovery plan already
exists. That means it studies how the person edits or acts on Vesper's work
without testing whether Vesper's work deserved to exist.

This misses the hardest product questions:

- Did Vesper add something the person did not already know?
- Was the contribution grounded in their actual attention and current life?
- Was it immediately useful without a click or reply?
- Did it open the world without becoming a generic recommendation feed?
- Did social evidence add genuine perspective rather than decoration?
- Would silence have been better?

Those are not content-polish questions. They determine whether the product has
earned the right to invoke the interaction kernel at all.

### 3.2 “Point at the thing” cannot be the universal opening

Many high-value moments begin with no visible object:

- Home returns a grounded New York possibility after the Italy trip;
- Places reveals a non-obvious spatial mechanism;
- a friend's Paris trace creates an authorized juxtaposition;
- Vesper reconstructs a day from tickets, photos, and receipts;
- a provider change requires an unsolicited but bounded recovery instrument;
- the correct result is a concise answer or silence.

The kernel is strongest after a referent exists. Chat remains the universal
address bar when none exists. Home and Places remain value-return surfaces when
no action is needed.

### 3.3 “Shared object” is too generic

The research uses “shared object” as a helpful contrast with chat-only and
form-first interaction. As product vocabulary, it is too broad. The visible
thing may be:

- direct canonical state;
- an ephemeral proposal;
- a generated Composition;
- a live Instrument;
- an attributed human contribution;
- a Receipt;
- an Owner Link;
- a saved version with dependency lineage.

These do not share one interaction, persistence, or authority contract. V2.2
should use **visible semantic context** or the exact result family instead of
turning “object” into a new universal noun.

### 3.4 The fixtures skew operational

A2, B2, and D2 strongly cover shaping, coordination, action, and repair. They
weakly cover editorial intelligence, curiosity, social meaning, retrospective
reconstruction, and present-life projection.

This can accidentally train the product toward a gentle project-management
tool: plans, choices, participants, status, execution, and undo. That would be a
better planning app, but not the full post-pivot Vesper.

### 3.5 The comparison matrix can become false rigor

The product is not yet at a stage where fine-grained treatment metrics deserve
more confidence than the scenario, model, or rendered value. The multiplayer
authority research already found diminishing returns from testing imagined
interfaces with deterministic labels. The same warning applies here.

The lab is valuable as:

- a falsification device;
- a semantic state harness;
- a way to expose boundary and refinding failures;
- a vocabulary stress test.

It is not yet a reliable behavioral experiment because there is no real model
resolver, no native product surface, no participant evidence, and several
remaining integrity faults. More treatment matrices would increase ceremony
faster than knowledge.

### 3.6 It underweights output quality and attention cost

The scorecard measures time, taps, words, refinding, boundary comprehension,
and ownership. It should also evaluate:

- novelty to this person;
- evidence strength;
- epistemic yield;
- present relevance;
- completeness on view;
- cumulative attention burden;
- creepiness or identity overreach;
- whether the social element materially changes value;
- whether the medium is smaller than prose;
- whether silence wins.

Without these, the lab can select the easiest way to manipulate a low-value
result.

### 3.7 It does not yet prove the four-root product feels whole

Page 04 draws four projections, but no person can actually move through a
generated Composition from Home to Chat or Places, reshape it, save it, return
exactly, and later refind its evidence in Life. That continuity is central to
the product, not a compatibility footnote.

## 4. Detailed source and experimental defect ledger

The following defects are present in the audited export at
`~/Downloads/vesper-interaction-kernel-lab/project/`. They are separate from the
philosophical ruling above. Fixing them does not by itself validate the product
direction.

### 4.1 P0 — blocks participant or comparative claims

#### IK-P0-01 — B2 participant checkpoint entry does not reconstruct checkpoint state

The protocol instructs the moderator to open per-actor sessions directly at:

- Dana: `d3 / c4 / h4`;
- Sam: `d7 / c6 / h6`;
- host decision: `d9 / c8 / h8`;
- withdrawal: `d11 / c10 / h10`.

See `01 Protocol and Scorecard.dc.html:46`.

But any explicit `step=` entry resets state to `initialState` in
`lab-state.js:216-224`. B2's initial state has an unsent Occasion, no attendance,
no private constraint, no contribution, and no selected Place
(`fixtures.js:31-53`). Therefore:

- Sam can enter a contribution segment before the invitation exists;
- the host can enter a decision with no contributed Place;
- a participant can enter withdrawal with no contribution and no settled
  dinner;
- rendered receipts can be blank or semantically impossible.

This invalidates the protocol's claim that B2 runs four independent,
semantically seeded micro-sessions.

**Required correction:** define explicit, validated checkpoint snapshots for
each actor job, or replay a canonical setup path before participant entry. A
step identifier is not a semantic checkpoint.

#### IK-P0-02 — D2 renders verified owner truth before state says verified

Both treatments set `flight:committed` and `hotel:released` when the bundle is
approved. The next static screen says the airline is confirmed and the hotel is
released with confirmations (`D2:91-103` and its instrument twin), while the
state does not become `flight:verified;hotel:verified` until the person presses
“Put the phone away.”

The screen and its `aria-live` announcement therefore outrun the semantic
state. The announcement “Done — each part confirmed by its owner” is also
false because transfer is explicitly unconfirmed.

**Required correction:** owner callbacks must update state before rendering
verified readback. The button to leave should not cause verification.

#### IK-P0-03 — D2 makes the user's phone action cause external-world facts

In the gap step, “Pick the phone back up” applies
`transfer:confirmed_720;interview:moved_10` (`D2:106-116`). This conflates an
observation action with the arrival of external facts. Picking up the phone
should reveal state already changed by the fixture clock/provider simulation;
it should not cause the driver to confirm or the office to move the meeting.

This matters philosophically: Vesper must distinguish occurrence, observation,
system knowledge, and user action.

**Required correction:** apply world updates at the controlled-delay boundary
or fixture clock, then let re-entry read them.

#### IK-P0-04 — D2's time-to-useful measurement cannot measure refinding

`firstUsefulAt` is one global session timestamp (`lab-state.js:121, 514-517`).
Both treatments mark the shared boundary step `p2` useful before the refinding
phase. The transcript also marks `t5` useful immediately on opening, even though
the participant has not yet scrolled or asked (`D2:121`).

Consequences:

- the refinding comparison inherits the earlier boundary timestamp;
- the transcript earns “useful” before the current state has been recovered;
- the instrument and transcript cannot be compared on time to recovered truth.

**Required correction:** define phase-specific outcomes such as
`refinding_started_at` and `current_truth_recovered_at`, triggered by a verified
state comprehension event rather than screen entry.

#### IK-P0-05 — the transcript “read back” path does not require reading or scrolling

The participant can press “I've read back — continue” immediately
(`D2:152`). No condition requires a user scroll, exposure to the relevant
messages, or a correct answer. The trace counts scroll distance but the route
does not bind successful refinding to it.

**Required correction:** treat scrolling as behavior, not proof. Ask a neutral
state question or require the participant to identify the open item before
marking refinding successful.

#### IK-P0-06 — participant authorization is broader than the resolver supports

The protocol blocks only A2/B2 language-only treatments for open-language
claims (`01 Protocol:33-36`). But meaningful hybrid paths also depend on the
same deterministic recognizer machinery:

- A2 hybrid qualitative reshaping;
- B2 hybrid Occasion creation;
- B2 hybrid Sam contribution;
- D2 transcript asking for current state.

Fail-closed behavior makes unsupported text honest; it does not make keyword
recognition evidence about natural interaction. Direct treatments can be
walked as direct-manipulation prototypes. Hybrid and transcript-language claims
must remain scripted until a real resolver is used.

**Required correction:** authorize by route and capability, not treatment
label. Mark every recognizer-dependent route as scripted-only.

#### IK-P0-07 — Page 04 is implementation-stale

Page 04 says native semantic rendering and exact cross-root return are future
lanes (`04:124-125`). The repository has progressed since the page was written:

- a strict backend `CompositionBriefV1` admission seam is executable;
- generated root-v2 carries typed evidence, sequence, comparison, spatial, and
  prose anatomy;
- native Home/Places semantic renderers and conformance ratchets exist dark;
- exact return context and restoration are implemented dark.

The honest remaining gaps are different:

- real model-authored composition quality;
- visual and human acceptance;
- end-user inspect/challenge/reshape/save behavior;
- production saved-composition writer;
- multiplayer causal invalidation owner;
- public promotion.

**Required correction:** update the board's implementation evidence without
promoting dark architecture into human or product proof.

### 4.2 P1 — blocks trustworthy internal study interpretation

#### IK-P1-01 — the self-check validates trace shape, not scenario truth

The eight assertions in `lab-state.js:794-845` check totals, field presence,
monotonic timestamps, consequence labels on owner changes, physical-action
accounting, unresolved-state stability, the current top snapshot, and session
key isolation.

They do not check:

- participant checkpoint prerequisites;
- rendered text against current state;
- treatment-equivalent starting digests;
- legal state transitions;
- every route and ending;
- owner-result coverage after partial execution;
- phase-specific success;
- participant token leakage;
- prior snapshots against their corresponding history;
- source withdrawal and dependent invalidation;
- accessible truth parity.

Calling it an integrity self-check creates false assurance.

**Required correction:** separate `trace-schema check`, `state-machine check`,
`rendered-truth check`, `comparison check`, and `participant-mode check`.

#### IK-P1-02 — combined display conditions are not composed with AND semantics

`bindState` processes `data-show-if`, `data-show-if-gte`, and
`data-show-if-lt` in separate passes (`lab-state.js:473-488`). When one element
contains two condition attributes, the later numeric pass overwrites the
earlier display result.

B2 uses exactly this pattern for private-limit eligibility copy at lines
226-227, 472-473, and 708-709. The amount comparison can make private-limit copy
visible even if `constraint_active` is not `yes`.

**Required correction:** collect every condition on an element and evaluate one
conjunction before setting display.

#### IK-P1-03 — runtime mutation accepts unknown state keys

URL state overrides validate keys, but `applySet` and `applySetFrom` do not
(`lab-state.js:251-277`). A typo in an HTML command silently creates a new key
and can leave the intended state unchanged.

**Required correction:** reject unknown keys and invalid enum transitions in
all runtime mutation paths, not only URL parsing.

#### IK-P1-04 — restored stack and state stack can become incoherent

When a persisted `stateStack` length does not equal the navigation stack,
initialization preserves the full navigation stack but replaces the state stack
with one snapshot (`lab-state.js:216-218`). A later Back can underflow or restore
the wrong predecessor.

**Required correction:** reject the persisted session or reconstruct both
stacks together; never retain one without its paired history.

#### IK-P1-05 — no reproducible route/state/render test suite accompanies the export

The execution report records manual browser verification. The export contains
no durable replay runner that can re-execute every legal route and assert state
plus visible truth. A static route check can show that targets exist; it cannot
show that semantic prerequisites or rendered claims are correct.

**Required correction:** add deterministic scenario replays for every authorized
path, including entry checkpoints, Back/Undo, partial results, and participant
mode.

#### IK-P1-06 — the experimental unit is still ambiguous

B2 mixes a whole Occasion lifecycle with four purported micro-sessions. D2
mixes a shared boundary prelude with a refinding comparison. A2 mixes value
return, qualitative shaping, comparison, saving, and dissolution.

The protocol says phase-scoped metrics solve this, but participant learning,
scenario memory, and checkpoint construction remain coupled.

**Required correction:** state precisely whether the unit is a route, actor job,
phase, or end-to-end experience. Do not aggregate across them as one treatment
score.

### 4.3 P2 — code, markup, and research-hygiene defects

#### IK-P2-01 — duplicate IDs across treatment templates

A2, B2, and D2 reuse many HTML IDs across hidden treatment templates. The
runtime usually scopes queries to the active clone, but the source document is
invalid HTML and creates avoidable accessibility and selector risk.

Examples in B2 include `send-invitation`, `send-opening`, `dana-im-in`,
`sam-edit`, and `withdraw-constraint` across treatments. D2 duplicates every
shared-prelude action ID across transcript and instrument.

**Required correction:** use unique DOM IDs and keep semantic target identity in
`data-target-id`.

#### IK-P2-02 — duplicated visible markup exists inside B2

The direct Sam receipt duplicates the contribution-place line around B2
`190`, and the language Sam receipt duplicates the page chrome around `430`.
These are visible composition errors, not merely shared identifiers.

**Required correction:** remove the duplicated elements and include a rendered
snapshot assertion for each state.

#### IK-P2-03 — recognizer parsing is semantically brittle

B2 language contribution splits the first sentence into Place and everything
else into advice. Amount extraction recognizes only a number. Place selection
matches words from the contribution. These are acceptable scripted-lab devices
only if the UI and report do not imply semantic understanding.

**Required correction:** show a research-only resolver label per route and keep
all such evidence out of product or participant conclusions.

#### IK-P2-04 — global counters obscure outcome quality

The trace reports actions, words, screens, scroll, and first-useful time. It does
not connect those quantities to a verified task outcome. Fewer actions can
therefore look better even if the participant chose the wrong state, missed an
open consequence, or merely pressed through.

**Required correction:** pair cost measures with task-specific semantic outcome
checks and comprehension. Never rank a route from cost alone.

## 5. The corrected relationship among product, artifacts, and kernel

The product architecture should read in three layers.

### Layer 1 — value return

Vesper decides whether it has earned attention and returns one of:

- Direct State;
- Composition;
- Instrument;
- Receipt;
- Owner Link;
- Short Prose;
- Silence.

The contribution should be complete enough to matter without interaction.
Editorial admission, novelty, evidence, present relevance, medium, and surface
composition are decided here.

### Layer 2 — collaboration and consequence kernel

If the person wants to shape, challenge, save, share, or use the result:

```text
FOCUS
  visible semantic context + owner + selection + moment + viewer authority

EXPRESS
  direct control, brief language, voice, or ordinary contribution

RESOLVE
  referent + semantic intent + smallest affected owner command

ACT
  apply if private/reversible
  preview once if another person, audience, provider, money, or world changes

READ BACK
  changed owner state + per-owner outcome + exact return

REPAIR OR LEAVE
  Undo, Correct, Stop, take over, or leave without debt
```

This layer does not decide whether a candidate deserved to appear or which
medium best expresses it.

### Layer 3 — continuity and causal learning

The system carries forward only authorized evidence and outcomes that can
change a later judgment or experience. Life preserves evidence and lineage;
Home and Places deliver present consequence; Plan and Occasion own bounded
commitment; Chat remains an aperture into the same owners.

This layer does not persist every generated projection or infer personal
meaning from passive behavior.

## 6. How the kernel should distribute across the roots

| Root or owner | Default posture | Kernel role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Home** | Return selected, complete value in relation to life now | Optional focus, reshape, ask, or consequence; never prompt-first by default |
| **Places** | Make the world spatially, practically, historically, and relationally legible | Compare, route, contribute, correct, or prepare an action from visible context |
| **Chat** | Receive attention and intent; support challenge, depth, authoring, and cross-object agency | Full expression/resolution aperture when no visible referent exists or thought is conversational |
| **Life** | Organize and preserve evidence-rich continuity, refinding, versions, and plural Outcomes | Inspect, correct, detach, reshape a saved version, or re-enter an owner without reflection homework |
| **Plan / Occasion** | Own commitments, participants, unsettled questions, and shared consequences | Strongest direct state, boundary, receipt, withdrawal, and repair behavior |
| **Push** | Interrupt only for time-bound or explicitly requested value | One bounded route into the relevant owner; never a miniature workspace |

The kernel is therefore unevenly distributed. It is central to Chat and live
owners, available but subordinate on Home and Places, and conservative in Life.

## 7. Recommended V2.2 scope

Do not add another broad three-treatment matrix. First repair integrity, then
add one missing product-level case.

### 7.1 Phase A — repair the current research instrument

1. Add semantic checkpoint fixtures for every B2 participant entry.
2. Separate external world updates from user observation in D2.
3. Make rendered D2 truth derive entirely from state.
4. Replace global first-useful with phase-specific semantic milestones.
5. Restrict participant authorization to direct routes; mark every deterministic
   language route scripted-only.
6. Compose conditional visibility correctly.
7. Reject unknown state keys and invalid transitions.
8. Repair stack restoration, duplicate IDs, and duplicate visible markup.
9. Add route/state/render/accessibility replay tests.
10. Update Page 04 to the current repository implementation boundary.

### 7.2 Phase B — add the missing value-to-collaboration fixture

Add one end-to-end generated-Composition scenario, preferably the post-return
New York/Sorrento case because it exercises the recent product philosophy:

```text
Evidence
  Sorrento cliff observation
  lift receipt + ferry ticket + photo
  present New York weekend
  one authorized friend's trace

Vesper return
  a substantive, evidence-backed mechanism or comparison
  complete on Home without a prompt
  one present opening or social juxtaposition only if it earns admission

Possible human actions
  inspect why it appeared
  challenge one claim
  remove one weak claim without regenerating the whole
  ask for a comparison in Places
  reshape the expression in Chat
  save one exact version to Life
  ignore it with no debt

Continuity proof
  exact return to the originating Home unit
  saved version preserves evidence and revision
  removed or withdrawn Source invalidates only dependent claims
```

This should not compare form, chat, and hybrid globally. It should test whether
the three product layers remain coherent in one experience:

1. Vesper contributes value.
2. The person can collaborate with it effortlessly.
3. The result has truthful consequence and continuity.

### 7.3 Phase C — evaluate at the right altitude

Evaluate two independent questions:

**Contribution quality**

- What did Vesper add?
- Did the person already know it?
- Was it evidence-backed and relevant now?
- Was the medium immediately legible?
- Did it feel invasive or falsely interpretive?
- Did social evidence materially change the contribution?
- Would silence have been better?

**Collaboration quality**

- Could the person address the exact thing without restating context?
- Did the result remain legible at its owner?
- Was the consequence preview proportional?
- Did correction stay local?
- Could the person leave without debt?
- Could they refind the result later?

Do not collapse these into one score. A high-value result with awkward editing
and a low-value result with elegant editing fail for different reasons.

## 8. What not to do next

- Do not rename the whole product around the Interaction Kernel.
- Do not turn Focus → Express → Resolve → Act into a mandatory user-visible
  funnel.
- Do not make Home a canvas of editable objects or visible prompts.
- Do not universalize “shared object” into a persisted schema.
- Do not add a generic AI edit mode or always-visible assistant.
- Do not extend the direct/language/hybrid matrix to every product scenario.
- Do not run participant comparisons on deterministic language routes and call
  them natural-language evidence.
- Do not treat more taps or longer time as failure when real human judgment is
  required.
- Do not let operational fixtures crowd out editorial, spatial, relational, and
  continuity value.
- Do not mistake dark typed architecture for a product users can understand,
  value, or trust.

## 9. Strategic synthesis

Vesper's emerging product spine is not a feature, a root, or a universal
object. It is a causal promise:

```text
attention and lived evidence
  -> substantive, permissioned intelligence
  -> native value in the right surface
  -> effortless human shaping where needed
  -> proportional personal, social, or world consequence
  -> only useful continuity carries forward
```

The Interaction Kernel is the middle of that chain. It is essential because it
prevents an ambitious AI product from becoming either passive content or
opaque automation. It is not sufficient because the product differentiates
itself by what it notices, connects, explains, opens, and makes livable before
the person has to operate it.

That distinction resolves the apparent conflict:

- The lab is not a detour.
- The lab is not the whole product.
- Its strongest principles should become shared infrastructure.
- Its fixtures should remain bounded research tools.
- The next design step should join **substantive value return** to
  **effortless collaboration**, not deepen interaction mechanics in isolation.

## Closing ruling

> **Philosophically: yes, the lab is on the right track—but one level down from
> where it currently appears to sit.**

The product north star remains: make sense, open possibility, help it work, and
carry forward. The Interaction Kernel should make those contributions
addressable, shapeable, actionable, and repairable without turning life into a
form, a prompt, or a project. It should not become the standard by which every
Vesper experience is forced to ask for interaction.
