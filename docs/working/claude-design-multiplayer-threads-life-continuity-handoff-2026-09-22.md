## 2. What the founder clarified

- Voting feels awkward and should not become the default grammar for gathering.
- Board 15 was ported from the Life design project intentionally. It establishes
  continuity with Life, not a new multiplayer destination to redesign separately.
- People should be able to start something under a broad theme and build on it
  with one friend or several friends over time.
- The same capability should work entirely privately. Multiplayer is optional.
- A restaurant list can be a simple expression; it need not become a rich project.
- Some collections accept miscellaneous interesting material. Some hold future
  possibilities. Other views show places actually visited. These are not the
  same intent or truth state.
- Life primarily makes lived experience understandable, but should remain
  flexible enough to hold interests and possibilities people carry forward.

Working principle:

> People decide what they want to keep together. Life offers useful ways to see
> what is there and what has happened. Social interaction can remain informal
> while useful continuity becomes organized.

The detailed treatments below are recommendations to explore, not a claim that
the founder selected every control, label, notification rule, or ownership policy.

## 3. Distinguish useful intentions, not four mandatory object types

| Example | What the person means | Suitable presentation | Do not assume |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Interesting stuff | Keep these things together | Mixed collection | A coherent interest, plan, or obligation to return |
| Restaurants to try | Keep these possibilities available | Place list and map | Attendance, liking, or a commitment to try all of them |
| Restaurants I've been to | Show supported personal history | List, map, or time view | A reservation, saved place, or photo alone proves a visit |
| Getting pasta right | Continue exploring something | Attempts, questions, photographs, sources and replies | A deadline, course, task board, or required progress |

“Thread” is useful working language for continuity around a subject. It should
not become a mandatory label for every saved list, album, historical view, chat,
Plan, or Occasion. Open “Restaurants to try” and show restaurants, not an empty
chat transcript or a generic thread-management screen.

A list may stay simple forever. A thread may remain dormant for months. A
mixed collection does not need AI to invent an organizing thesis. These examples
are compatible experiences, not a requirement for a type-selection wizard.

### 3.1 Three interaction forms, not a feature for every social intention

The founder consolidated the social foundation into:

| Form | Human purpose | Important distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sharing / broadcasting | Put something into another person's world | Address a person, several people, or an audience; receiving need not require a response |
| Kept threads / collections | Keep something together and optionally build on it | Organized around a subject; personal or shared; no required endpoint |
| Chats between people / in a group | Talk naturally | Organized around participants; not every exchange belongs in a durable collection |

“This made me think of you,” asking for experience, adding another perspective,
offering help and suggesting company are intentions expressed through these
forms—not separate feature systems. Reactions and replies are actions; photos,
maps and lists are media or presentations; a group is a participant scope.

A chat with Maya can connect to several kept threads. A kept thread may involve
a different subset of people or remain entirely private. “Thread” here means a
kept subject, not every chat transcript or reply chain. Keep audience boundaries
legible when moving material between them.

Examples of possible continuity, not mandatory funnels:

- share a restaurant → talk → optionally add it to Our places;
- share a photograph → a friend enjoys it → stop;
- keep a personal question → explore it later → optionally share selected material.

Design human/group conversation honestly where the experience needs it. Do not
replace conversation with voting solely to avoid messaging scope. Conversely,
this design foundation does not itself authorize shipping a general-purpose
messenger, read receipts, calling, or presence tracking. Record implementation
scope conflicts with the older group-room ruling for explicit reconciliation.

## 4. Automatic Life organization and deliberate keeping coexist

### Automatic organization

Life can organize and connect material already admitted under its actual
retention and use permissions. It can make supported history findable through
time, places, people and themes without requiring manual filing.

**This does not mean “save every question.”** The current
[Contribution and Consequence contract](../systems/contribution-and-consequence.md)
distinguishes Ask from Bring/Keep and says optional conversational continuity
does not itself constitute an effective retention agreement. A design that uses
a prior conversation must identify its authorized continuity basis, or use an
explicitly kept excerpt in the specimen. Do not portray unadopted retention as
shipped behavior.

Automatic grouping also does not authorize identity inference, a factual visit
claim, publishing, enrollment of a friend, or adding to a shared collection.
Corrections should be available where they matter, not through a mandatory
review queue before Life delivers value.

### Deliberate keeping

“Keep these together” creates intentional continuity around selected material.
It may start with one item, a casual title, a natural-language request, or an
existing collection. Personal by default; another person is not a prerequisite.

Being deliberate does not mean being manually maintained. Vesper can help find,
organize and use eligible material. Avoid asking users to approve every ordinary
filing association or classify every message as a reply versus contribution.

### Becoming shared

Invite someone into a clearly bounded collection/thread or create a shared
continuation from selected material. Do not expose the private conversation,
source bundle, inferred themes, or personal history behind those selections.
Adding a member to an existing shared context needs an explicit treatment of
historical access; that decision is still open, not silently “all past items.”

Keep and shared contribution must remain distinguishable. A private Keep action
must not quietly become “add to our shared list.” Likewise, recognizing that a
private item is relevant does not authorize posting it to the shared thread.

## 5. Life integration: reuse board 15, do not build another destination

Life's center of gravity is what someone has lived, with room for what they are
keeping and carrying forward. Do not replace the existing Life composition with
a collection dashboard or split the entire page into “automatic” and “manual.”

- **Time:** visits, episodes, and the course of an ongoing exploration.
- **Places:** spatial views of saved possibilities and supported history, with
  the distinction legible rather than all pins implying visits.
- **People:** shared experiences and deliberately shared things with a person.
- **Threads:** things kept together for continued exploration; exact naming and
  placement of simple lists versus inferred themes remain design questions.

“Shared with Maya” is relationship context. “Our places” is a particular thing
you build within that relationship. Neither needs to stand in for the other.
For a group, make its actual contributors and shared scope understandable
without requiring a new group profile or a fifth root tab.

The same authorized material can be reachable in several views without asking
the user to copy or move it between folders. Presentation may vary; authorship,
audience, source access and correction must remain consistent. This is a user
experience requirement, not a prescription for database deduplication.

Example: a restaurant saved from Priya's recommendation later appears in actual
visit history. Keep the recommendation and the person's own experience. Do not
automatically remove it from the original collection, mark an intention complete,
infer liking, or infer that an accompanying friend also visited.

Board 15's administrative copy can be refined in coordination with Life:
lead with human words, useful material and experiences; make detailed custody
and provenance inspectable rather than the emotional foreground. Preserve the
Life layout and source lineage. Do not independently replace its canonical design.

## 6. Social participation should feel additive, not managerial

The invitation is “add something if it speaks to you,” not “submit your part.”
People can contribute a place, photo, link, observation, question or attempt;
others can read, enjoy, react, reply or return later. Unequal effort is normal.

Recommended treatments:

- Start from something concrete, not a setup form with required purpose and type.
- Preserve each person's voice and attribution without contribution rankings.
- Let replies stay conversational; useful material remains easy to revisit.
- Support a thread-level conversation and replies around particular material
  without asserting that general-purpose group messaging is already approved.
- Avoid default equal-contribution prompts, streaks, progress meters, votes,
  pending-response debt or pressure to keep a thread active.
- Clarify additive participation versus editing/removing someone else's material.
  Do not assume that “shared” grants universal edit or delete authority.
- Show what happens when the thread is quiet and later becomes useful again.
  Quiet does not mean abandoned, failed, or in need of an AI revival prompt.

## 7. AI value and four-root continuity

Human contribution need not have an AI response beneath it. AI earns its place
by helping use what is already there: recover an earlier recipe, compare attempts,
show a place collection on a map, or answer which saved places suit a stated need.
Ground any current practical claim in actual or explicitly simulated evidence.

| Root | Role in this experience |
| --- | --- |
| Home | Worthwhile new contributions or timely value from existing material, not a filing or participation queue |
| Chat | Ask, explore, bring something, and request keeping; human conversation only within the separately selected design scope |
| Places | Use place-related contributions in their geographical and practical context, preserving who said what |
| Life | Find the durable collection/thread, related history and shared context; inspect and correct when needed |

One item may matter in several roots without becoming four independent objects.
Do not turn a friend's contribution into everyone's preference. “Added this for
Maya” and “I like this” are different. A joke is not a declared intention.

### 7.1 Additional capabilities: awareness, doing together, and reliable arrangements

Research-informed recommendations, not three new founder-selected features:

| Capability | What is different from exchanging material? | Vesper expression to explore | Existing home |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Temporary presence / context | Information is useful while it describes a current situation, then stops being actionable | Around Brooklyn this weekend; at the market until noon, happy for company | Deliberate audience-bounded sharing; relevant Home/Places views |
| Shared activity | People participate in an unfolding experience, not merely discuss or collect it | Use a shared walking route, cook from an evolving recipe, or look through trip photographs together | Existing shared object or Occasion; no new session root |
| Shared arrangement | People can rely on current agreed details without reconstructing a conversation | Meeting point, settled time, actual participation, an accepted offer to bring something | Existing Plan/Occasion and relevant Home/Chat views |

These categories are an analytical coverage check, not a six-form ontology.
Presence may simply extend sharing; a live activity may be an Occasion; an
arrangement may be an existing Plan. Do not build competing social containers.

**Presence:** explore deliberate, bounded expressions first. Location, being
online, or doing something does not imply openness to company or permission to
track. An old expression must not remain a current invitation. No always-on
location, last-seen indicator or inferred availability is requested by this pass.

**Shared activity:** distinguish an asynchronous collection from using something
together now. Live-engine help could keep an authorized route change or meeting
point clear, recover relevant material, or answer a practical question. People
may already be physically together, so the best interface may involve less
screen time. Do not default to synchronized playback, calls, multiplayer games,
live cursors or a general collaborative editor to demonstrate this capability.

**Arrangements:** conversation can produce reliable details without a poll.
Keep confirmed details findable, preserve individual participation state and
handle changes through existing owners. A thread is not replaced or completed
because one of its ideas becomes an arrangement. This is an existing Vesper
product responsibility to connect better, not a newly discovered subsystem.

The practical research question is: **when people move from sharing or collecting
into doing something together, can Vesper support them without making them start
over or making the experience feel administered?**

## 8. Lighter gathering and board 16

### 8.1 Why voting is not the default

The founder questioned both the amount of voting UI and the underlying behavior:
it feels awkward to commit heavily to voting. This is not merely a request to
restyle checkboxes. A casual “brunch this weekend?” may be an expression of
interest, a bid to reconnect, an opening for company, or an actual invitation.
Immediately presenting choices can turn that social overture into a decision
procedure before people are ready to declare a position.

Working design hypothesis: reducing scheduling effort can still increase social
effort if people must submit availability, choose sides, or account for silence.
Do not claim research proves all polls are awkward; the direction is to preserve
tentativeness and natural negotiation instead of imposing a procedure.

**Polling is an optional instrument, not the grammar of gathering.** Demote
“Deciding together” as a voting mode from the foundational board 16 sequence.
Do not replace it with another mandatory consensus mechanism or an AI tally of
casual replies. Removing polls must not simply transfer all coordination work
back to the organizer.

### 8.2 Three situations, not a three-mode setup form

| Situation | Example | Appropriate default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An opening | Might go to the greenmarket tomorrow | Enjoy, reply, or leave it alone; no attendance machinery |
| An invitation | Greenmarket at ten—join me? | Natural reply or a simple attendance action; truthful response state |
| Unresolved coordination | We want to go, but haven't found a time | Conversation and practical help; optionally request an availability tool |

These are distinctions the product must respect, not categories everyone must
select before posting. Attaching a place, date, or ticket does not by itself turn
an update into an invitation. Explicit invitations and genuine commitments can
still have useful structured controls; “less voting” does not mean “all prose.”

### 8.3 Replacement sequence for board 16

Draw a person proposing something, friends responding naturally, and the author
settling the details. For example, using proposed fixture dialogue:

> Nora: thinking hato saturday, late brunch. anyone around?
>
> Maya: yes, after noon
>
> Priya: could do 12:30
>
> Nora: perfect, 12:30 then

The resulting plan is easy to find beside the exchange. Distinguish settled
details from each person's actual attendance response. This dialogue establishes
a proposed/settled time, not automatic attendance for everyone who participated.
An explicit “I'm in for 12:30” can resolve participation without a redundant
confirmation; ambiguous availability cannot.

Show room for “after twelve,” “can Jo come?” or another place suggestion without
starting a new ballot. An attached conversation may be enough; this assignment
does not authorize a separate general-purpose group-chat product. Show a quiet
recipient without a holdout badge, public rejection story or reminder campaign.

An invitation can emerge from a thread without converting or replacing it.
The shared collection and conversation can remain useful beyond that one plan.

### 8.4 Keep interest, availability and commitment distinct

Availability is not attendance. Remove the default transition from “Saturday
works” to “you're in.” Neither a reaction, a suggested restaurant, a saved place,
nor silence authorizes enrollment.

Two legitimate treatments may coexist where useful:

- **Availability only:** people indicate workable options; the chosen proposal
  becomes an invitation they may accept.
- **Explicit conditional commitment:** “Count me in for either of these.” If the
  selected option remains within those stated conditions, avoid asking the same
  question again. A material change needs a fresh participation treatment.

Do not add confirmation to every step as a substitute for clear initial meaning.

### 8.5 Where an optional poll earns its place

A host coordinating several people across several dates may genuinely ask,
“Which evenings work?” Make that tool deliberately available or offer it when
requested help reveals a real coordination need. Do not automatically insert it
whenever a conversation contains alternatives.

Keep the question's purpose clear: availability, preference and commitment are
not interchangeable. No automatic winner, mandatory voting, public holdout
pressure or silence-as-consent follows from introducing the tool. If no response
is needed, do not manufacture a decision object merely to give the interaction
an endpoint.

### 8.6 Vesper's job is practical assistance, not social refereeing

Help recover relevant suggestions, keep current details findable, reduce
repetition, check practical constraints when asked, and prepare a clear update
for the authorized person to settle or send. Preserve private reasons. Do not
convert every reply into a ballot, speak for attendees, invent agreement, or
interrupt an easy human exchange to demonstrate capability.

Design success is that getting together feels easier—not that every gathering
is more formally organized. Review the ordinary conversation first and the
optional poll second; neither needs to dominate the broad social experience.

### 8.7 Separate board 16's “With” issue

For “With Maya,” distinguish confirming co-presence, visible association and
adding to personal/shared history. Explore a legible, minimal action rather than
bundling all three into “I was there” or creating a routine confirmation inbox.
Do not make this follow-up depend on resolving every tagging policy first.

## 9. Bounded next design pass

Revise existing relevant canvases and add one clearly labeled thread/list
continuity canvas only if the current boards cannot show the following. Do not
restart the full direction portfolio or create one feature per example.

1. **Personal collection:** start “Interesting stuff” or “Restaurants to try”
   from one item. Add a different kind of item without setup homework. Show it
   staying useful with no collaborators and no further activity.
2. **History beside possibility:** a saved restaurant later has supported visit
   evidence. Show it in Life history and the original collection; correct a
   mistaken association without losing independently valid material.
3. **A private exploration becomes selectively shared:** keep a pasta question
   and an attempt; invite Maya into selected material. Show exactly what remains
   private and how Maya adds her own contribution without becoming an editor.
4. **A small circle builds something:** several friends contribute unevenly to
   “Our New York.” Show enjoyment, conversation, and useful later retrieval; no
   requirement that every contribution become an outing.
5. **Optional practical continuation:** select one idea, discuss it naturally,
   settle an invitation, and keep the underlying thread available afterward.
   Include a person who doesn't respond or attend without making them a failed
   participant in the wider shared collection.

Reuse the established cast and clearly label any new fixture assumptions. The
examples above are compositions of the same capability, not five new subsystems.
Show a plausible initial state with sparse material, not only years of history.

Within composition 4, distinguish its kept subject from the participants' chat.
Within composition 5, show a temporary expression becoming stale, current agreed
details remaining findable, and one useful moment during the shared activity.
Reuse existing components and examples; these are additional states within the
same pass, not three new canvas families. Also retain a branch that never becomes
an activity or arrangement. Do not imply every experience needs presence sharing.

### Acceptance questions

- Can a person understand what this is without knowing the word “thread”?
- Can a list stay a list, and can a personal thread stay private indefinitely?
- Does Life deliver value without manual curation or blanket question retention?
- Are saved, suggested, planned, visited and liked distinguishable?
- Can friends build on something without voting, matching effort, or managing it?
- Can an opening remain casual, an invitation be answerable, and difficult
  coordination receive optional structure without making those setup modes?
- Does board 16 show conversation-led settling without turning availability,
  reactions or silence into attendance, or giving the host more clerical work?
- Is joining a shared context understandable without exposing private history?
- Does later use provide more than scrolling a transcript or collecting links?
- Can quiet, enjoyment and an unacted-on possibility be satisfying outcomes?
- Does the composition fit board 15's existing Life structure and other roots?
- Are sharing, kept subjects and participant conversations distinguishable but
  connected, without asking users to learn an abstract social taxonomy?
- Can current context expire, agreed details remain reliable, and live help
  support an actual experience without new destinations or default surveillance?

Return one consolidated handback: changed boards, preserved outstanding items,
explicit conflicts with older boards/docs, recommended treatments, and unresolved
decisions. Separate what the drawings demonstrate from usability or implementation
claims. No additional engineering queue follows automatically from this pass.

## 10. Research grounding and limits

- [Lost in Co-curation (CSCW 2021)](https://wordpress.cs.vt.edu/echolab/wp-content/uploads/sites/105/2021/02/CSCW2021_Lost_in_Co_curation_CameraReady.pdf): two questionnaire studies (N=100 and N=67) describe differing ownership expectations and discomfort around modifying others' playlist contributions. Communication was not uniformly helpful. Supports investigating additive participation and legible ownership, not a claim that Vesper improves friendships.
- [TikTok sharing in everyday social life (2024)](https://ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/view/20993): ethnographic work describes sharing as relationship-maintaining communication. Enjoyment and acknowledgment need not produce an action.
- [Don't Mess Up My Algorithm (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22817): interviews with 21 meme-DM users examine perceived personalization consequences, not an audit of Instagram's recommender. A contribution for another person should not automatically become the contributor's durable preference.
- [Are.na channels](https://help.are.na/docs/getting-started/channels), [Spotify collaborative playlists](https://support.spotify.com/cg-en/article/collaborative-playlists/), and [Meta's collaborative-collection announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/12/sharing-features-on-instagram-notes-group-profiles-and-more/): precedents for shared collections, not evidence that Vesper's specific experience is differentiated or wanted.

This is a targeted research synthesis, not a systematic review. Proposed Vesper
benefits remain hypotheses. The distinctive candidate is useful continuity
across lived experience, human contributions and current context—not inventing
the shared collection or maximizing contribution volume.

### Additional-form research — September 22

- [Social Translucence (Erickson and Kellogg, 2000)](https://research.ibm.com/publications/social-translucence-an-approach-to-designing-systems-that-support-social-processes): a design framework for visibility, awareness and accountability. Motivates examining useful social context; it is not proof that more visibility improves consumer relationships.
- [Spotify Listening Activity and Request to Jam (January 2026, subsequently updated)](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-01-07/listening-activity-request-to-jam-messages-updates/): distinguishes activity awareness, messaging and a request to join shared listening. Product precedent, not a mandate to adopt its visibility defaults or combined-taste model.
- [Discord Watch Together](https://support-apps.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/26502500234519-Watch-Together-FAQ): shared media activity illustrates doing together rather than merely messaging about content. Vesper need not build streaming infrastructure to learn from the distinction.
- [Partiful](https://help.partiful.com/en-us/articles/15525594-why-use-partiful): event details, attendance and updates illustrate a reliable shared arrangement beyond conversation. This does not justify voting-first design.
- [Apple Check In](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/guide/iphone/iphc143bb7e9/ios): bounded, initiated assistance can communicate a relevant change without another manually authored post. This is not a request for safety monitoring or permission for ambient tracking.
- [Locket's photo-widget explanation](https://help.locket.com/en/articles/14225418-my-teen-asked-me-to-get-locket-what-is-it): delivery on a home screen can change how sharing is experienced without introducing another underlying social form.

These precedents establish capabilities and design distinctions, not demand,
competitive advantage, psychological outcomes, or implementation readiness for
Vesper. No additional feature count or product scope follows from their existence.

### 12.2 Tighten gathering without turning ambiguity into attendance

Board 06's ordinary brunch path still asks the host to settle in conversation,
confirm extracted details, inspect a settled-details screen, and separately ask
people to come. Removing the ballot must also remove unnecessary administration.

Revise the existing conversation in place. One compact prepared action beside it
can show “Saturday, 12:30 at Hato” and “Send as the plan,” with the intended
audience apparent. This is proposed copy, not a required label. Combine redundant
host steps; do not combine independent participants' decisions. If explicit
language already authorized the same bounded effect, do not ask again merely
because the interface has extracted it into a structured object.

Carry forward “count me in any time after 12” when the details fit that condition.
Keep “could do 12:30” as availability unless the surrounding explicit response
establishes commitment. Nobody is enrolled through silence, a reaction or a save.
Show confirmed participants without publishing a list of people who have not
answered. Remove “Dana hasn't said anything” from the social foreground; do not
confuse avoiding a holdout display with falsely marking everyone confirmed.

Keep “With” effects understandable, but avoid a whole Home state devoted to what
a tagging confirmation did not do. Explore a compact receipt with inspectable
scope and correction. Separate personal organization from publishing association
and adding to a shared record; a permission boundary does not require repeated
manual filing after an independently authorized personal record already exists.

### 12.3 Make active collections inviting to contribute to

Boards 08–09 establish Life continuity well, but “Our New York” reads more like a
finished archive than something friends can casually build. Make a lightweight
Add action apparent in the shared example, not only in the personal pasta example.
Show one actual contribution: add a photo, link, place or sentence; see it in the
collection; let a friend reply to that contribution. Do not require choosing an
artifact type, writing a description, or deciding whether a sentence is a chat
message versus a durable contribution before it can be useful.

Keep the Life layout family. Do not require every collection to display Things,
Drawer, Contact sheet, Conversation, People and Places as separate prominent
sections merely because those data exist. Consolidate repetitive counts and
sections when they obscure the material. An active collection should make the
content and participation easy to find; an older one should make retrieval easy.
Neither requires a new Active/Archived mode or another root.

Keep date meanings intelligible in Over time: adding a restaurant on October 5
and visiting it on October 12 are different events. A chronology of contributions
must not silently read as the person's lived itinerary. Preserve future ideas
alongside supported history without turning a place list into a completion meter.

### 12.4 Resolve Keep once; preserve a simple sharing boundary

Board 02 describes immediate private keeping; board 10 asks “Where does it go?”
first. Recommended default: **Keep privately immediately, then optionally Add to
a collection**, with a compact receipt and Undo. This applies to material eligible
for private retention; receiving/display access alone does not authorize a copy.
Do not place destination selection in front of the basic value of saving.

An explicitly invoked “Add to Our New York” is a different, shared contribution:
make that audience clear. The same control must not sometimes save privately and
sometimes post to friends based only on inferred relevance.
