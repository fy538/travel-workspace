# Brand naming: recognizable spelling variants and Greek-root plays

Date: 2026-08-10

Status: active exploration. Domain and collision observations are point-in-time
screens, not legal clearance.

## Why this lane exists

The strongest new naming mechanism is not an opaque invented word. It is a
familiar, conceptually useful word with one restrained spelling change:

- **Beckon -> Bekon**
- **Setting -> Settin**

The reader should be able to recover the source word, pronunciation, and core
idea immediately. The alteration exists to create a compact visual identity and
practical naming space; it should not make the name look encrypted, misspelled,
or generically startup-like.

This direction is closer to the desired Aesop / Sonos / Notion / Oura / Figma
standard than multi-syllable synthetic names. Meaning comes first, but current
availability is an entrance criterion rather than a cleanup step.

## Transformation rules

A variant should:

1. preserve the source word's dominant sound;
2. change only one obvious feature: a final letter, a familiar phonetic swap,
   or one nonessential vowel;
3. remain easy to say after seeing it once;
4. remain easy to spell after hearing it once;
5. avoid a visually broken consonant pile;
6. survive exact-name searches in consumer software, AI, maps, local discovery,
   planning, travel, social coordination, and proactive assistants;
7. preferably have the exact `.ai` available. An open exact `.com` is valuable,
   but requiring both domains should not force a bad name.

Rejected transformation examples include `Setng`, `Amydst`, and `Situatn`.
They create availability by destroying fluency.

## Greek-root plays

### Method

Greek should be treated as a source of compact concepts, not as an aesthetic
costume. Raw transliterations such as `Metaxy` or `Aletheia` often fail the
speech test. The useful pattern is:

1. start with a Greek idea that genuinely describes the product;
2. create at most one phonetic or orthographic adjustment;
3. make the English pronunciation recoverable;
4. screen the adjusted spelling before becoming attached to the etymology.

### Greek-root rejections discovered in this pass

| Name | Reason to stop |
|---|---|
| **Dipla** | Although δίπλα means “next to” or “beside,” an active local-community app already uses Dipla for nearby people, places, activities, and real-time local posts. This is a direct product collision. |
| **Nostia** | Direct collision with an active social travel and group-planning app, plus an AI journal and current software/location trademark activity. |
| **Steki** | The meaning is nearly perfect—a habitual place or hangout—but the raw word is crowded by hospitality uses and adjacent software called Stekio. |
| **Faros / Faroz** | The Pharos-derived spelling is easy to understand, but `Faros` is taken and `Faroz` loses the calm, premium quality of the source. |
| **Astyr** | Attractive Aster respelling, but already registered as an active US word mark. |
| **Metaxa** | Familiar Greek-derived form, but strongly occupied by the spirits brand and not semantically faithful enough to justify the noise. |

## Ranked slate after this pass

These are exploration leaders, not adoption recommendations:

1. **Settin** — best contextual/world-model expression.
2. **Stirin** — best proactive and ambient-intelligence expression.
3. **Pareya** — best Greek-derived and multiplayer expression.
4. **Elswher** — cleanest exact-domain availability; discovery-led.
5. **Amungst** — strongest exact-domain multiplayer control.
6. **Tydin** — promising push-intelligence wildcard.

The Greek search did not outperform **Settin** or **Stirin** overall. Its main
contribution is **Pareya**, plus the useful semantic territory around `steki`:
people developing a recurring relationship with a place they return to. That
territory may inspire positioning even if the literal word is not usable.

## Next validation gate

Run one blind language test on `Settin`, `Stirin`, `Pareya`, `Elswher`,
`Amungst`, and `Tydin`:

1. show the name without its source word or etymology;
2. ask for immediate pronunciation;
3. ask the participant to spell it after hearing it aloud;
4. test ten-minute unaided recall;
5. ask what sort of company it sounds like;
6. show it as an app icon, notification sender, spoken referral, and investor
   sentence;
7. only afterward reveal the source idea and record whether that meaning makes
   the name better or merely rationalizes it.

Advance no more than three names to professional US/EU/UK/China knockout
searches and native-speaker review.
