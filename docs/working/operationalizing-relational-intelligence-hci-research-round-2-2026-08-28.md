# Operationalizing Relational Intelligence — HCI Architecture Research Round 2

## Question and status

Round 1 established the strongest current HCI constraints on Vesper's desired
relationship with a person. Round 2 asks a harder applied question:

> **What must the product, runtime contracts, interaction treatments, and
> evaluation program do so that relational intelligence remains coherent in
> actual use rather than only in philosophy?**

This document investigates seven operational topics:

1. memory experience and causal forgetting;
2. receptivity without surveillance;
3. situated affordance and experienced reachability;
4. temporary social constitutions and social authorship;
5. contribution admission and substantive value;
6. adaptive projection with a stable mental model; and
7. longitudinal value, dependency, and commercial boundaries.

It is applied research, not accepted canon. The decision docket below records
recommended postures so the research can be internally coherent; only an
explicit founder decision may promote them into product or architecture
authority.

## Executive verdict

Round 2 reinforces the Round 1 conclusion while making its operational meaning
more precise:

> **Vesper should be architected as a governed transformation, not as a set of
> intelligent surfaces. It compiles authorized lived evidence into a bounded
> contribution, affordance, or consequence; projects it through the quietest
> sufficient native treatment; observes what actually happened; and carries
> forward only what remains valid, useful, and permitted.**

The product can feel broad because the evidence and consequence domains are
broad: a conversation, movie ticket, route, restaurant, friend, weather change,
photograph, booking, or prior trip may all participate. The scope becomes
unfocused only when these appear as independent capabilities rather than inputs
or outputs of the same four moves:

```text
make sense → open possibility → help it work → carry forward
```

The main applied findings are:

- memory needs **use-time steering and causal lineage**, not only a memory
  center;
- receptivity is best inferred from **interaction boundaries and explicit
  deferral**, not engagement intensity or intimate sensing;
- experienced reachability should return **a feasible set with visible
  tradeoffs**, not one personalized score;
- an Occasion needs a **temporary decision constitution and minimum grounding
  receipt**, not a group profile;
- contribution admission needs a **novelty-to-person posture and semantic-delta
  receipt**, while admitting honest uncertainty about what the person knows;
- adaptive projection should select among **sanctioned native expressions
  after semantic admission**, never generate product structure as truth; and
- evaluation should measure **capability, outward consequence, second-occasion
  improvement, and withdrawal health**, not only satisfaction or engagement.

## 2. Round 1 decision docket

### 2.1 Recommended for acceptance

| Decision | Recommended posture | Why it is architecture-bearing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Relational doctrine** | Contextual intimacy without synthetic intimacy | Governs voice, memory, initiative, vulnerability, notification, and evaluation. |
| **North-star consequence** | Relational value points outward to people, Places, understanding, capability, and action | Prevents engagement or attachment from becoming the product goal. |
| **First-person authority** | Vesper interprets evidence and bounded situations; the person authors what those imply about them | Governs memory claims, editorial copy, social expression, and correction. |
| **Evidence authority** | Authority depends on the question; behavior confirms occurrence/outcome but does not automatically override stated intent, preference, or meaning | Corrects the current universal behavior-over-claims invariant. |

| **Initiative law** | Relevance earns eligibility; receptivity earns treatment; authority earns action | Prevents confidence, familiarity, or predicted acceptance from granting consequences. |
| **Social ontology** | One occurrence, plural Outcomes, minimum common ground, temporary constitution | Prevents group profiles, inferred consensus, and private-to-shared leakage. |
| **Interface doctrine** | Stable roots and controls; adaptive contributions, media, density, and continuations | Preserves learned structure while allowing contextual expression. |

### 2.2 Recommended to keep unresolved

- Whether “companion” remains the public category word. The relationship
  doctrine is clearer than the market language.
- Whether every governed-claim concept deserves a new persisted table. The
  semantic distinction is required; storage topology is not yet decided.
- Whether Vesper should infer receptivity beyond low-risk interaction-boundary
  signals. Evidence does not justify broad sensing.
- Whether an Open intro or public Status belongs in the first release. Current
  work supports the mechanics, not release priority.
- Whether a production adaptive resolver needs model judgment. The authored
  fixture portfolio should fail before model selection is authorized.

### 2.3 Current doctrine correction to queue

`docs/systems/memory-preference.md` currently states that a dwell or visit
outweighs a stated interest. The Unified Context and Memory Plan already uses a
more defensible precedence model. If the decision docket is accepted, the
system charter should be changed to:

> **Evidence authority depends on the question. Behavioral evidence may confirm
> exposure, occurrence, selection, or outcome; explicit current self-statement
> remains authoritative for current intent, preference, and personal meaning.
> Behavioral inference stays scoped, contestable, and lower-authority unless
> corroborated.**

Round 2 proceeds under this recommended rule without silently editing canon.

## 3. Shared applied model

### 3.1 The same four situations

Every topic is tested against a common fixture portfolio:

| Fixture | Situation | What it stresses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **S1 — Ordinary New York** | Nothing urgent; a normal week and an open weekend | value without urgency, silence, broad life context, no empty-state homework |
| **S2 — Returned home** | Nice → Sorrento → Amalfi → Rome evidence enters a life already moving forward | reconstruction, memory reuse, source attribution, past-to-future continuity |
| **S3 — Live Rome** | 91°F, walking, dense cultural environment, uncertain energy and time | situated reachability, interruption, operational-first treatment, eyes-to-world |
| **S4 — Brooklyn dinner** | Host, partner, close friend, and weaker tie; private constraints and one shared choice | authorship, audience, common ground, mandate, nonresponse, plural Outcomes |

These are not one privileged behavior loop. They are a minimum compositional
portfolio that prevents one surface or lifecycle from dictating the
architecture.

### 3.2 Comparative treatment pattern

Each topic compares four conditions where applicable:

- **A — infrastructure baseline:** direct map, booking state, factual recap,
  ordinary message, or static canonical object;
- **B — underreach:** Vesper repeats facts, asks the person to do synthesis, or
  exposes only controls;
- **C — overreach:** Vesper diagnoses, merges, acts, publishes, or adapts beyond
  authority;
- **D — recommended:** Vesper supplies completed bounded value with provenance,
  correction, an optional consequence, and a clear stopping condition.

Silence is always a real fifth counterfactual, not an empty failure state.

## 4. Topic 1 — Memory experience and causal forgetting

### 4.1 Operational question

How can Vesper use longitudinal evidence naturally at the moment of value while
keeping retention, interpretation, correction, and forgetting legible—without
turning Life into a database administration surface?

### 4.2 Research synthesis

Current HCI work consistently rejects the all-or-nothing model of agent memory.
People expect controls over kinds or scopes of memory and want those needs to
change across stages of work. A 2026 ACL paper makes **memory reliance** itself
steerable, showing a useful distinction between retrieving everything and
starting without history. Emerging CHI work maps privacy and usability problems
in current conversational-memory controls, while the ChatGPT memory study
shows that continuity gains coexist with opacity, unforgetfulness, and privacy
strain.

Two additional findings matter for Vesper's editorial ambition:

- AI can shape recall and introduce persistent errors when assisting with
  memory reconstruction; “Tell Me What I Missed” and prior eyewitness work
  make factual reconstruction a higher-risk job than ordinary generative prose.
- People misremember which ideas or elaborations came from themselves versus AI,
  especially in mixed workflows. A one-time “AI assisted” label cannot carry
  longitudinal authorship provenance.

The supported direction is **use-time steering plus durable lineage**. Most
people should not maintain every claim. They should be able to understand and
change the consequential use that appears now, while a deeper Life owner
supports inspection and batch governance.

### 4.3 Current Vesper alignment and gap

The Unified Context and Memory Plan is already close to the target: source
evidence, typed claims, audience-specific compilation, decisions/actions, and
outcomes/corrections form one lifecycle. `PersonalClaim` resolution uses scope,
source authority, confidence, and time; corrections can immediately withhold
stale synthesis; context values remain ephemeral while manifests retain safe
lineage.

The remaining asymmetry is experiential and causal:

- Personal Memory narrative still has broad practical authority in legacy read
  paths and user-facing language.
- Current correction suppresses or rewrites memory reads, but not every
  downstream projection, notification, export, social foreground, provider job,
  or learned signal has one universal invalidation lineage.
- Retention, inference, proactive recall, social eligibility, and action use
  are not yet independently steerable at the claim-use boundary.
- Source authorship and AI editorial contribution can become visually detached
  when an artifact resurfaces months later.

### 4.4 Recommended memory interaction

Memory should operate through three layers:

1. **Quiet retention receipt.** First durable retention is briefly legible and
   correctable, then recedes.
2. **Use-time memory aperture.** A material contribution names the small basis
   that affected it and offers local controls.
3. **Life memory owner.** Deeper inspection, contradiction, scope, source,
   correction, export, and dependency-aware forgetting live here for people
   who need them.

Example for S2:

```text
The ferries changed the shape of this trip

Across Nice, Sorrento, Amalfi, and Rome, five of your seven intercity
transitions happened by water or rail. That makes Governors Island this
weekend a continuation in motion, not a substitute for Italy.

Based on: tickets you sent + confirmed route sequence
[See the route] [Don't use trip logistics this way]
```

The contribution does not ask what the trip meant. It attributes the factual
reconstruction, makes one new present-facing relation, and provides a local
stop-use control. “Keep the tickets but stop drawing travel-pattern
connections” should not delete the artifacts.

### 4.5 Memory-use contract

A material memory application needs:

```text
application id and current job
source evidence refs + subject
governed claim refs, if any
use purpose and scope
memory reliance posture: none | bounded | continuity_high
source / person / Vesper authorship roles
audience and egress boundary
novel contribution or consequence affected
expiry / revalidation trigger
local correction and stop-use commands
dependent projections, jobs, and learned signals
```

The reliance posture belongs to one application, not a global personality
setting. “Fresh start for this dinner” may suppress historical taste while
retaining accessibility constraints. “Use what you learned from Italy” may
increase continuity for a Home composition without exposing it socially.

### 4.6 Comparative treatments

| Treatment | S2 expression | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Static trip timeline and ticket list | Useful reconstruction, but no semantic or future delta |
| B | “What from the trip do you not want to lose?” | Makes the person maintain memory before receiving value |
| C | “You experience life through transitions” | Converts evidence into identity and obscures authorship |
| D | Grounded route reconstruction plus a current New York opening, with local memory-use control | Recommended |
| Silence | No memory unit | Correct when reconstruction adds nothing beyond what is already known or timely |

### 4.7 Adversarial cases

- User deletes the ferry ticket after a derived route comparison was featured.
- A friend appears in a photo but never authorized relationship inference.
- The same restaurant visit reflected a partner's choice, not the user's taste.
- A corrected accessibility need remains embedded in a cached group-safe reason.
- Vesper's prior recommendation caused the observed behavior later used as
  preference evidence.
- A generated story resurfaces after the person has forgotten which sentences
  they authored.
- A former partner or dissolved circle remains in continuity projections.

### 4.8 Evaluation protocol

Compare invisible memory, constantly narrated memory, and quietly legible
use-time memory. Measure:

- correct prediction of what was retained and why it appeared;
- source and authorship attribution after one week;
- time to “keep artifact, stop learning” and full-cascade deletion;
- percentage of dependent projections and jobs actually invalidated;
- recognition without misrepresentation or identity foreclosure;
- second-occasion benefit; and
- governance burden per material correction.

The hard oracle is causal: after a source is corrected or forgotten, no active
dependent treatment or action may continue to claim it.

## 5. Topic 2 — Receptivity without surveillance

### 5.1 Operational question

How should Vesper decide between Home, in-context assistance, Push, or silence
without treating engagement as wellbeing, inferring intimate mental states, or
asking people to configure a notification control panel?

### 5.2 Research synthesis

Current proactive-assistant work converges on a boundary-sensitive model.
Controlled programming studies show that conservative initiative can improve
outcomes, while persistent suggestions attract interaction but are less
preferred. A five-day field study found materially higher engagement at natural
workflow boundaries and high dismissal mid-task. Sensor-informed timing can
improve assistance accuracy, but it also increases surveillance and deference
risks. The 2026 CHIIR proactive-agents report therefore treats timing,
transparency, contestability, autonomy, privacy, and welfare as one design
problem.

The important correction for Vesper is that **receptivity is not predicted
acceptance alone**. It includes whether the treatment respects attention,
competence, relationship context, and the person's current physical world.

### 5.3 Current Vesper alignment and gap

Vesper already separates semantic worth from deterministic `p_accept`, has
push→ambient→drop treatment, dismissal ratchets, content-free semantic
admission, treatment ceilings, and explicit silence receipts. `SituationEnvelope`
supports expiring attention references and current world facts.

The gaps are consequential:

- current `p_accept` is largely a response-history model: engagement pattern,
  velocity, volume, and dismissals;
- it does not represent operational, cognitive, and relational receptivity
  separately;
- engagement can reflect urgency, anxiety, novelty, or manipulation rather than
  welcome;
- the legacy design exempts all time-sensitive events from receptivity, but
  “time-sensitive” is too broad—some facts justify Push, others justify an
  updated owner surface;
- the notification model does not yet carry competence/identity sensitivity or
  the reason silence loses.

### 5.4 Recommended receptivity model

Use a sparse evidence hierarchy:

1. **Explicit present state:** asked, opened, deferred, snoozed, quieted,
   “not tonight,” “too hot,” “leave me here.”
2. **Interaction boundary:** app open, route transition, arrival, schedule
   change, post-commit, provider deadline, Occasion decision window.
3. **Operational materiality:** exact consequence of waiting or missing.
4. **Coarse burden:** recent Vesper volume and explicit dismissals.
5. **Weak inference:** engagement history, only as a conservative modifier.
6. **Prohibited default:** gaze, physiology, emotion, voice affect, or inferred
   fatigue as durable or high-authority state.

Receptivity should be represented as a treatment constraint, not a welfare
diagnosis:

```text
operational: open | constrained | urgent
cognitive: available | boundary_only | unknown
relational: appropriate | sensitive | unauthorized | unknown
basis refs + explicitness
valid until
demand cost
competence / identity sensitivity
reason silence loses
```

### 5.5 Comparative treatments

For S3, the Colosseum closes soon, it is 91°F, and the person is walking:

| Treatment | Expression | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Google Maps route and closing time | Strong infrastructure baseline |
| B | Home card waiting for the next app open | Too quiet if a real deadline will be lost |
| C | “You seem tired; take the shaded route” | Surveillance-shaped person diagnosis |
| D | “The shaded route adds 7 minutes and still arrives before last entry” in the active route; Push only if the margin is becoming unsafe | Recommended |
| Silence | No cultural interpretation while the route problem owns attention | Correct |

For S1, a rich curiosity contribution can sit complete-on-view in Home. It does
not earn a Push merely because past cultural cards received long dwell.

### 5.6 Contract delta

Extend judgment admission or its input adapter with:

- `initiative_origin`: asked, detected, anticipated;
- operational/cognitive/relational receptivity states;
- explicit versus inferred basis;
- `demand_cost` and `competence_identity_sensitivity`;
- boundary type;
- reason silence loses;
- treatment-specific expiry; and
- downgrade path.

Do not replace the current deterministic estimator. Narrow its meaning to one
behavioral prior and combine it with explicit/boundary evidence under policy.
Do not let predicted acceptance authorize the action itself.

### 5.7 Adversarial cases

- High engagement caused by a disrupted flight is interpreted as general
  receptivity to cultural Push.
- A person dismisses because they already knew the content; the system infers
  disinterest in the subject.
- A lock-screen notification exposes an intimate Place or relationship claim.
- A deadline is real but has already been delegated to someone else.
- “Undo” removes a notification after a recipient already saw it.
- Frequent app opening reflects anxiety rather than permission for more
  initiative.

### 5.8 Evaluation protocol

Compare reactive-only, boundary-timed, engagement-optimized, and persistent
proactivity. Evaluate identical contributions across silence, Home, active
surface, and Push. Measure task consequence, interruption cost, dismissal,
competence threat, “seen versus watched,” prediction of future system behavior,
and treatment regret after 24 hours. Weight false positives by attention,
privacy, authority, and social exposure rather than counting all sends equally.

## 6. Topic 3 — Situated affordance and experienced reachability

### 6.1 Operational question

How should Vesper combine map/provider infrastructure with current bodies,
conditions, companions, commitments, and experiential intent so that “what can
we do?” becomes practical without becoming a universal Place score or invasive
personal model?

### 6.2 Research synthesis

Affordances are actor–environment relations. Recent map research demonstrates
that even people grouped under one accessibility category can evaluate the same
barrier differently. Bike-routing research shows that purpose and companions
change route preference and that alternative comparison plus segment preview
can matter more than one opaque optimum. Thermal-routing work shows that shade
alone is not a reliable proxy and that shortest routes can misrepresent actual
pedestrian burden under heat.

The operational implication is a **feasible possibility set**, not a prediction
of the one best choice. Vesper's differentiated value is not replacing a map's
coverage or a booking provider's inventory. It is translating those facts into
experienced reachability for the current person or group while retaining
tradeoffs and uncertainty.

### 6.3 Current Vesper alignment and gap

`SituationEnvelope` already binds route, weather, position, attention,
availability, source observations, material unknowns, and a shared validity
horizon. The context compiler declares requirements and records missing or
expired sources. Place canon separates world face, relationship face, and
current situation.

What is missing is the candidate-relative decision object:

- situation is represented once, but not assessed against each candidate or
  route;
- capabilities and constraints lack a shared provenance vocabulary separating
  explicit need, provider observation, sensed signal, and system inference;
- experiential intent—restore, learn, linger, reconnect, get fed, make the
  evening work—is not explicit in feasibility comparison;
- there is no typed preservation relation saying which alternative keeps the
  original intent while reducing burden; and
- “unknown” is present, but the UI consequence of uncertainty is not defined.

### 6.4 Recommended assessment

`SituatedAffordanceAssessment` is a short-lived computation over one candidate
family:

```text
principals + current constitution
immediate job / experiential intent
candidate Place, route, Move, or action
world facts and individual expiries
explicit bounds and durable access needs
inferred situation signals, separately marked
hard feasibility and conditional feasibility
burden vector: time / heat / grade / cost / coordination / attention
opportunity vector: restoration / learning / connection / utility / novelty
unknowns and sensitivity
intent-preserving alternative
why-now evidence
authority, audience, expiry, and fallback
```

The vectors support explanation and Pareto comparison; they should not collapse
into one stored welfare score. Hard constraints eliminate. Remaining candidates
are shown on the two or three axes that actually distinguish them now.

### 6.5 Comparative treatments

For S3:

| Treatment | Expression | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Nearest or highest-rated tour | Ignores heat, effort, timing, and human interest |
| B | Long cultural guide in Chat | Content may be good but mistimed; creates homework |
| C | “Because you love history, walk the full route” | Identity inference overrides physical reality |
| D | Two feasible options: shaded short route with one concrete cultural cue, or indoor museum sequence preserving the same Aeneas/Rome question | Recommended |

The cue should point to something perceptible and then recede. Vesper might say
which relief panel or inscription completes the relation; it should not require
the person to keep reading while standing in the heat.

For S1, “nearby” can include a ferry ride or meaningful transition if it is
experientially reachable this weekend. The route's texture is part of the
possibility, not an inconvenience hidden after selection.

### 6.6 Contract delta

- Add candidate-relative affordance assessment as a pure or read-model
  contract over existing situation/context authorities.
- Preserve per-field source and expiry; do not rely only on one envelope expiry
  in explanation or fallback.
- Standardize provenance of current bounds:
  `explicit_user`, `durable_constraint`, `provider_observed`, `sensed`,
  `system_inferred`.
- Require one intent-preserving fallback when a recommended candidate has
  material situational burden.
- Keep precise position and body-state inference out of durable receipts;
  persist only safe source classes, decisions, and outcomes.
- Make offline and degraded behavior part of assessment, not a renderer-only
  afterthought.

### 6.7 Adversarial cases

- Weather is fresh but transit outage data is stale.
- One participant has a private mobility constraint that shaped the choice.
- A low-energy evening is incorrectly promoted to a stable identity.
- A scenic alternative adds stairs the system cannot verify.
- Companions split and rejoin, changing the assessment mid-route.
- Provider availability is only inferred from search inventory.
- The “meaningful” route is unsafe, closed, or no longer feasible offline.

### 6.8 Evaluation protocol

In field or intercept studies, compare distance-first, context-aware single
ranking, and context-aware feasible set with tradeoff preview. Measure actual
completion, abandonment, route burden, choice fit, correction, confidence
calibration, eyes-to-world time, and whether the alternative preserved the
person's purpose. Recruit across mobility and sensory access needs; a founder
plus able-bodied partner is not a valid reachability sample.

## 7. Topic 4 — Temporary social constitutions and social authorship

### 7.1 Operational question

How can Vesper use private understanding to reduce coordination and open human
connection while keeping audience, authorship, common ground, decision power,
and personal Outcomes distinct?

### 7.2 Research synthesis

Common ground is the mutually warranted knowledge sufficient for a current
joint purpose, not the union of everyone's information. Current studies of
shared LLM accounts show that cognitive traces create observer effects,
self-censorship, peeking, and mismatched informal norms even among known people.
This strengthens Vesper's private-relationship model: social collaboration
cannot be built on a shared account or merged transcript.

Current AI-mediated communication studies also show that authorship labels do
not solve the whole problem. AI mediation changes how messages diagnose warmth
or moral character; mixed human/AI workflows create source-memory confusion;
and authenticity depends on whether the person constructed the meaning, not
only whether they approved the output.

Finally, human–AI team research warns against treating task reward as
cooperation. An AI can improve completion while reducing human-human
responsiveness, mutual status, information diversity, or actual interdependence.

### 7.3 Current Vesper alignment and gap

Vesper already has unusually strong pieces:

- exact pair/circle scopes and addressed handoffs;
- explicit delivery modes, recipient pull consent, expiry, revoke, keep,
  dismiss, and `open_together`;
- invitation intent that requires authorship source when meaning is claimed;
- separate recipient-private participation context;
- one Occasion with personal and shared Outcomes; and
- participant-scoped Commitment commands and optimistic revisions.

The principal gaps are:

- `DecisionVisibility` remains `private/group/system`, too coarse for named
  principals, roster revision, circle, public Place, provider, and inference or
  copying limits;
- the system can validate who is a participant but does not represent a
  complete temporary decision constitution;
- shared consequences do not preserve the minimum facts and authority that
  participants mutually grounded;
- authorship is strong in invitation intent but not generalized across human
  meaning, AI editing, decision, sending, execution, and visible speaker; and
- no runtime participation budget limits Vesper's conversational role.

### 7.4 Recommended temporary constitution

An Occasion should compile—not necessarily persist as one object—the following
constitution revision:

```text
active principals and roster revision
participation state for each principal
who may propose / decide / prepare / execute
decision rule and affected-principal requirements
private hard bounds, never copied into shared state
minimum group-safe reason vocabulary
communication and authorship policy by speech act
AI participation job, budget, and exit condition
expiry and reconfirmation triggers
```

A `GroundingReceipt` for one consequential choice should retain:

```text
facts mutually established
choice and rejected alternatives necessary to understand it
decision rule and constitution revision
decision principal, executor, and affected principals
unresolved or contested items
action and recovery state
```

It must not retain hidden rationales merely because they influenced the result.

### 7.5 Comparative treatments

For S4, one guest privately needs an easy exit and another is bringing a dish:

| Treatment | Expression | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Host manages everything in ordinary group chat | Familiar but high coordination burden and weak durable consequence state |
| B | Vesper polls everyone for preferences | Converts invitation into group homework |
| C | “Everyone prefers the nearby option” based on private evidence | False consensus and privacy violation |
| D | Vesper privately narrows to two workable choices; host sends human-authored intent; shared reason says “nearby, easy to join late, kitchen available”; constitution routes the final decision and action | Recommended |

For an expressive message, Vesper may surface a concrete shared artifact or
fragment—“the photo of the dish he brought last time”—but the host authors why
that person is wanted. For logistics, Vesper may draft and act more fully under
mandate.

Afterward, the shared occurrence may be “dinner happened.” One person keeps the
dish, one the conversation, one nothing, and the host a future opening. No one
must complete a recap.

### 7.6 Contract delta

- Replace coarse visibility at social egress with an exact
  `AudienceCustodyEnvelope`: principals, roster revision, purpose, retention,
  inference, copy/reshare, egress, expiry, and revocation.
- Generalize `AuthorshipEnvelope`: material source, human meaning author, AI
  editor/composer, initiator/sender, decision principal, executor, audience,
  and visible speaker.
- Add constitution and GroundingReceipt projections at consequential decision
  boundaries.
- Add `AgentParticipationPolicy`: job, novelty/urgency threshold, maximum
  turns/words, human-ratio guard, exit, and repair.
- Do not create a group profile as the shortcut connecting these contracts.

### 7.7 Adversarial cases

- Roster changes after a private constraint influenced a proposal.
- Host revokes a handoff after the recipient has kept or acted on it.
- A guest declines silently and the system infers relationship damage.
- A human-approved apology was semantically invented by Vesper.
- Recipient-controlled place pull leaks recipient location to the sender.
- A fairness heuristic exposes who compromised previously.
- A simulated second AI voice appears to provide independent social proof.
- A person pictured or discussed in an artifact did not consent to social use.

### 7.8 Evaluation protocol

Compare ordinary group chat, Vesper as silent durable tool, Vesper as bounded
facilitator, and Vesper as active teammate. Measure organizer burden, decision
time and quality, human-human responsiveness, speaking and information share,
belonging/status, audience comprehension, privacy leakage, authorship accuracy,
nonresponse pressure, and separate participant Outcomes. Interview participants
individually so a smooth shared presentation does not erase disagreement.

## 8. Topic 5 — Contribution admission and substantive value

### 8.1 Operational question

How does Vesper decide that it has actually added something—rather than
repeating the person's attention, decorating a generic recommendation, or
manufacturing a surprising but weak connection?

### 8.2 Research synthesis

Current work on knowledge-adaptive advisors reinforces recipient design: people
notice when explanations misjudge their knowledge level. CHIIR's proactive
agents agenda identifies knowledge-gap navigation as an open problem rather
than a solved personalization primitive. AiGet found an early signal that
contextual assistance can increase attention and curiosity toward immediate
surroundings, but its studies were small, short, low-demand, and sensor-heavy.

Tools-for-thought and epistemic-agency research adds a crucial distinction:
immediate performance with AI does not show what the person understands or can
do later. Emerging epistemic-transfer work proposes testing unassisted ability
on new cases after the tool is removed. Research on cognitive offloading
similarly distinguishes scaffolding that transfers agency from substitution
that creates dependence.

For Vesper, contribution quality has three independent questions:

1. **Grounding:** is the new claim supported?
2. **Novelty-to-person:** is it reasonably likely to add something?
3. **Capability or consequence:** what becomes perceptible or possible?

No model can know the second perfectly. The architecture needs an honest
posture, not a false binary “user knows this.”

### 8.3 Current Vesper alignment and gap

The Editorial Canon's restructure/explain/differentiate/transfer operations are
a strong semantic vocabulary. `JudgmentAdmissionInput` compares candidates
against silence and checks source binding, evidence state, current job,
materiality, practical satisfaction, receptivity, and treatment. Content
receipts distinguish candidate, selected, and rejected claim IDs.

The gap is that `materiality` remains a scalar supplied before the actual
semantic delta is structurally represented. Current contracts do not require:

- premises already supplied or exposed;
- the exact new relation/mechanism/distinction;
- known-to-person posture and basis;
- analogy depth and limitation;
- capability opened; or
- the minimum medium capable of expressing that delta.

The engine can prove that a candidate was selected against silence without
proving that the candidate contributed more than a paraphrase.

### 8.4 Recommended ContributionBrief

```text
premise and evidence refs
prior exposure / semantic receipt refs
known-to-person posture:
  likely_known | uncertain | likely_new | explicitly_known | corrected
basis for that posture
new claim / relation / mechanism / reconstruction
editorial operation
uncertainty and counterevidence
analogy mapping and limit, when applicable
capability / perception / decision opened
why now and current job
smallest adequate lead medium
optional consequence
expiry and invalidation
```

The known-to-person posture should use explicit prior statements and semantic
receipts first. Dwell, taps, or exposure cannot prove comprehension. “I already
knew this” repairs the exposure model and candidate family; it does not
automatically mean “less interested.”

### 8.5 Comparative treatments

Using the Colosseum/Aeneas/Odyssey evidence from S2 or S3:

| Treatment | Expression | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | “Rome adopted the survivor's side of the Trojan War” | Restates what the person already encountered and may overstate the history |
| B | “The story at the Colosseum connects to the film you watched” | Repeats the person's own connection |
| C | Poetic identity claim about why mythology defines the traveler | Unsupported and invasive |
| D | A grounded structural transfer the person did not supply—for example, how Augustan political poetry used competing Trojan inheritances, what changed from Homeric defeat to Roman legitimacy, and what specific object or site makes the mechanism visible—with limits and sources | Recommended when supported |

For the Sorrento cliffs example, useful comparison needs shared geological or
urban mechanism, important difference, evidence on both sides, analogy limit,
and a new way to perceive or choose. “These cliffs resemble Big Sur” is not a
contribution.

Social evidence can also supply delta: a friend's simultaneous Paris experience
may juxtapose how two cities stage antiquity, heat, public life, or movement.
The friend activity is not the value; the grounded contrast is.

### 8.6 Contract delta

- Add ContributionBrief upstream of treatment and projection.
- Derive or justify `materiality` from typed semantic fields rather than using
  a free scalar as the complete admission argument.
- Join semantic receipts to exposure without treating exposure as knowledge.
- Require an analogy/transfer structure when the operation is `transfer`.
- Select medium after the contribution is admitted; artifacts cannot rescue a
  weak claim.
- Treat direct canonical state and silence as candidates in the same portfolio.
- Record “already knew,” “wrong relation,” “weak evidence,” and “not now” as
  distinct repair events.

### 8.7 Adversarial cases

- A fact is new to the model's exposure ledger but obvious to the person.
- The most surprising cross-domain connection is false or only metaphorical.
- A friend comparison reveals unshared activity or location.
- A beautiful map hides that the underlying semantic claim is generic.
- Repeated factual content is shown because earlier exposure was not recorded.
- Dismissal is interpreted as disagreement or disinterest.
- The contribution gives a final personal interpretation rather than opening
  the person's perception or judgment.

### 8.8 Evaluation protocol

Blindly compare factual report, personalized paraphrase, poetic interpretation,
evidence-led synthesis, structural transfer, prepared consequence, and silence.
Measure five-second proposition comprehension, novelty-to-person, grounding,
representational gain, correction, value without CTA, and delayed unassisted
transfer to a new Place or example. Track parrot rate, unsupported-aha rate,
identity overreach, and the percentage of units judged better than silence.

## 9. Topic 6 — Adaptive projection with a stable mental model

### 9.1 Operational question

How can Home, Chat, Places, and Life express the same product intelligence in
different media without becoming a generated interface that moves truth,
controls, or navigation under the person?

### 9.2 Research synthesis

Recent generative-interface systems support dynamic interfaces for structured,
interactive, and information-dense work, especially when generation operates
through semantic intermediate representations. Just-In-Time Objectives argues
for visible, specialized objective selection rather than one generic chat
objective. Semantic guidance research similarly tries to bridge the gulf
between natural-language intent and UI structure.

The evidence also preserves classic adaptive-UI cautions: unpredictability,
loss of control, and learned-location breakage. Current generative-UI work is
still early, and strong usability scores in one task do not justify continuous
restructuring of a life product. Accessibility work suggests a more defensible
role: generation can adapt language, modality, or representation where static
content fails, but must remain policy-bound and traceable to equivalent meaning.

### 9.3 Current Vesper alignment and gap

The adaptive-composition lab already reaches the right architectural shape:

- stable semantic spine and canonical revision;
- Home/Chat/Places/Life as surface context;
- orthogonal presence, lead medium, modifier, semantic role, density, and root;
- native client ownership of geometry, focus, motion, accessibility, and
  degradation;
- authored fixtures across four situations; and
- no server component tree.

The lab has not migrated its older renderer-key union to the corrected
dimensions, and production selection remains deliberately unauthorized. The
main unresolved issue is not visual variety. It is whether the resolver can
select a treatment people understand and whether one object remains
recognizable across roots.

### 9.4 Recommended resolver boundary

The resolver should receive only admitted semantics:

```text
canonical owner / object / revision
lifecycle and truth state
viewer, audience, custody, and action authority
current job and product move
ContributionBrief or direct canonical-state brief
environment / attention / accessibility constraints
prior projection exposure
```

It may select:

- silence or presence;
- one lead medium: evidence, sequence, comparison, spatial, prose, or
  instrument;
- optional social, relational, or quantitative modifier;
- compact, standard, or immersive density;
- one root-native component family;
- one complete accessible alternative; and
- an optional continuation or owner handoff.

It may not invent facts, choose a new canonical owner, widen audience, grant an
action, send arbitrary code, move stable roots, or create a new component kind.

### 9.5 Cross-root identity

One object needs a stable recognition bundle across projections:

- canonical identity and owner;
- title or subject lineage;
- truth state and revision;
- source/authorship footprint;
- audience and consequence state; and
- consistent route to the durable owner.

Home may lead with a comparison, Chat may open the reasoning, Places may render
the spatial mechanism, and Life may show its lineage. These are four
projections of one contribution or object, not four independent cards.

### 9.6 Comparative treatments

For the S2 ferry/transition contribution:

| Treatment | Expression | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Same prose card copied into every root | Stable but ignores root posture and medium |
| B | New generated layout and controls per root | Expressive but unpredictable and untestable |
| C | Home comparison, Chat explanation, Places route, and Life provenance as unrelated objects | Creates conflicting truth and repetition |
| D | One canonical contribution with root-native projections, stable identity, accessible equivalence, and owner handoff | Recommended |

### 9.7 Contract delta

- Migrate fixture vocabulary from peer renderer keys to orthogonal canonical
  dimensions before designing production selection.
- Define deterministic must-use and must-not-use rules before adding model
  ranking.
- Require complete nonvisual semantic order and alternative at resolver time,
  not after rendering.
- Record projection exposure separately from semantic exposure.
- Add a stable fallback for every adaptive family and an easy “show simply” or
  owner transition.
- Evaluate silent adaptations, suggested adaptations, and user-selected format
  separately.

### 9.8 Adversarial cases

- Same action appears in different positions or with different consequence
  language across roots.
- Map becomes unusable under denied location or VoiceOver, with no equivalent
  ordered route.
- Large text changes information hierarchy rather than layout only.
- Offline projection preserves stale persuasive copy while hiding freshness.
- Resolver personalizes from inferred identity when current job is sufficient.
- Repeated view of one projection is interpreted as preference for its medium.
- A generated component introduces an action not authorized by the owner.

### 9.9 Evaluation protocol

Test root prediction, object matching across roots, consequence prediction,
five-second comprehension, correction and fallback success, and complete task
performance under 320-point width, 150% type, VoiceOver order, Reduce Motion,
offline/stale truth, denied location, unavailable actions, and corrected
audience. Compare fixed prose, authored native portfolio, deterministic resolver,
and model-ranked resolver. Model selection must beat the authored policy, not
only universal chat.

## 10. Topic 7 — Longitudinal value, dependency, and commercial boundaries

### 10.1 Operational question

How can Vesper learn and become more useful over time without defining success
as dependence, screen time, disclosure, or commercially convenient persuasion?

### 10.2 Research synthesis

Repeated interaction changes mental models, trust, disclosure, memory reliance,
and user strategy. Companion research shows heterogeneous outcomes associated
with anthropomorphism, intensive use, deep disclosure, and offline social
context. Interaction-harm research argues that output-only evaluation misses
effects emerging over time. AI-Wrapped proposes privacy-preserving measurement
of in-the-wild longitudinal use, while recent work on cooperation shows task
reward alone cannot establish healthy human–AI interdependence.

The commercial boundary follows contextual integrity. Intimate context supplied
to help a dinner, route, or relationship does not become legitimate targeting
data merely because a relevant booking or product exists. A correct inference
can be unacceptable when its purpose or audience changes.

### 10.3 Current Vesper alignment and gap

The runtime has unusually strong causal identifiers across opening, judgment,
treatment, action receipt, readback, occurrence, Outcome, and later learning
application. Presentation proofs can bind exact rendered bytes to recipient,
surface, treatment, revision, and expiry. Notification holdouts and treatment
receipts provide useful local counterfactuals.

The system is weaker at the relationship level:

- outcome instrumentation favors engagement and immediate action;
- there is no canonical measure of representational gain or capability
  transfer;
- repeated-explanation reduction can be measured, but identity foreclosure or
  memory anchoring cannot;
- human-human responsiveness and social displacement are not first-class;
- “user returns often” does not distinguish compounding value from dependence;
  and
- commercial suggestion, provider execution, and editorial opening do not yet
  have a constitutional separation in telemetry and optimization.

### 10.4 Longitudinal evaluation contract

Every material study or dogfood program should join seven layers:

| Layer | Minimum question |
| --- | --- |
| Truth/policy | Was the evidence current, permitted, and honestly attributed? |
| Contribution | What semantic or capability delta was claimed? |
| Projection | Did the medium make it perceptible with low burden and equivalent access? |
| Interaction | Could the person steer, refuse, correct, predict, and recover? |
| Encounter | What changed in perception, action, coordination, or real-world burden? |
| Occasion/relationship | Did the second situation improve without privacy loss, identity freezing, or human displacement? |
| Portfolio | Did the four roots and cumulative interventions remain coherent and worth their total demand? |

### 10.5 Core measures

Develop Vesper-specific measures:

- **attention dividend:** value or consequence divided by attention and input;
- **representational gain:** new supported relation, mechanism, distinction, or
  reconstruction;
- **epistemic transfer:** later unassisted application to a new case;
- **tool-removal cost:** performance or confidence loss when Vesper is absent;
- **calibrated initiative:** correct intervention and correct silence;
- **governance efficacy:** correction/forgetting success and downstream
  invalidation;
- **plural-agency integrity:** participant understanding without inferred
  consensus or private leakage;
- **earned continuity:** second-occasion benefit attributable to permitted
  prior evidence;
- **relational outwardness:** more or better engagement with people, Places,
  and activity outside Vesper; and
- **withdrawal health:** ability to pause or leave without guilt, loss of basic
  function, or hidden retaliation.

### 10.6 Commercial constitution

Separate three jobs:

1. **Editorial relevance:** this provider or product is a grounded consequence
   of the person's current job.
2. **Fulfillment:** Vesper connects to or acts through infrastructure under
   authority.
3. **Persuasion/monetization:** a commercial party benefits from exposure or
   conversion.

The first two can use purpose-limited context. The third must not silently use
intimate memory, relationship evidence, vulnerability, inferred receptivity, or
hidden welfare weights. Sponsorship or commission must be legible at candidate
admission and evaluation. Commercial candidates must beat a non-commercial
alternative on user value without receiving a ranking bonus from revenue.

### 10.7 Adversarial cases

- More sessions are interpreted as stronger relationship quality.
- High disclosure drives personalization and commercial targeting.
- A partner offer exploits grief, loneliness, fatigue, or social uncertainty.
- The app becomes necessary to notice or decide things the person previously
  could do independently.
- Repeated successful mediation displaces direct contact between friends.
- A second Occasion is faster only because one person's preferences dominate.
- Silence cannot be measured, so rich content appears superior by default.
- Provider commission changes which possibility Vesper presents as meaningful.

### 10.8 Evaluation program

Run a six-to-eight-week study across at least three occasions: ordinary local
life, travel or live movement, and a shared social Occasion. Include new and
established users, access variation, relationship change, and participants with
different baseline social support.

Use event-contingent sampling rather than daily reflection homework. Compare
memory versus no-memory, bounded versus persistent initiative, authored native
portfolio versus simpler infrastructure, and assistance followed by delayed
unassisted transfer. Include an explicit one-week Vesper pause to measure tool-
removal cost and withdrawal health. Interview social participants separately.

Commercial evaluation must compare sponsored and non-sponsored candidate
admission blind to the editorial judge and audit whether intimate context
changed commercial exposure.

## 11. Cross-topic operational architecture

### 11.1 Do not build seven new services

Round 2 refines the Round 1 seams into seven compositional contracts. They may
begin as models, adapters, fixture schemas, or evaluation records inside
existing owners.

| Contract | Extends current foundation | Main missing semantics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **GovernedClaim + MemoryApplication** | `PersonalClaim`, corrections, context compiler, learning applications | permitted use, reliance posture, source/AI/person authorship, dependency cascade |
| **ContributionBrief** | editorial canon, judgment admission, content decision receipts | exact semantic delta, known-to-person posture, capability opened, analogy limit |
| **SituatedAffordanceAssessment** | situation envelope, context dependencies, availability, Place relationship | candidate-relative feasibility, burden/opportunity axes, intent-preserving fallback |
| **ReceptivityEnvelope** | `p_accept`, dismissal ratchet, treatment ceiling, attention refs | explicit/boundary/inferred basis, three receptivity forms, demand and identity sensitivity |
| **Audience/Authorship/Constitution/Grounding** | relationship scopes, handoff permissions, Occasion projections, Commitments and Decisions | exact custody, roster revision, semantic authorship, decision rule, minimum common ground |
| **ProjectionDecision** | treatment, presentation proof, adaptive fixture lab | canonical dimensions, accessibility alternative, cross-root recognition, stable fallback |
| **LongitudinalOutcomeBundle** | causal lineage, action/readback/Occurrence/Outcome, exposure, holdouts | representational gain, capability transfer, outwardness, withdrawal, commercial purpose |

### 11.2 Recommended evaluation lineage

```text
source evidence
  → governed claim / current fact
  → MemoryApplication and purpose-limited context
  → ContributionBrief or SituatedAffordanceAssessment
  → JudgmentAdmissionReceipt against silence
  → ReceptivityEnvelope and TreatmentDecision
  → ProjectionDecision + presentation proof
  → mandate / constitution / GroundingReceipt when consequential
  → action readback / Occurrence / plural Outcomes
  → LongitudinalOutcomeBundle
  → corrected claim, earned continuity, or forgetting cascade
```

Not every path uses every node. Direct provider facts can produce an operational
consequence without a ContributionBrief. A factual Home contribution may end at
projection. Silence still records the content-free decision needed for
counterfactual evaluation.

### 11.3 Deterministic versus model authority

| Decision | Default owner |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence/source validity, roster, audience grant, mandate fit, expiry, hard constraints | Deterministic domain/policy code |
| Semantic delta, analogy quality, likely novelty, experiential relation | Model judgment under structured receipt and evaluator |
| Receptivity from explicit state and boundaries | Deterministic policy |
| Weak receptivity prior from behavior | Calibrated statistical component, conservative and non-authorizing |
| Candidate feasibility and numerical route/provider facts | Deterministic tools and domain adapters |
| Experiential tradeoff interpretation | Model-assisted, with visible evidence and alternatives |
| Root, mandatory medium, accessibility fallback, consequential control placement | Deterministic native policy |
| Ranking among sanctioned equivalent projections | Model only after authored policy baseline is beaten |
| Personal meaning and socially diagnostic expression | Human |
| Provider/social/public action | Human or exact active mandate |

### 11.4 What not to build

- A universal relationship score, familiarity score, welfare score, Place
  score, or autonomy score.
- A second global user-profile narrative to compensate for Personal Memory.
- A generated component-tree protocol.
- A shared group transcript or group mind.
- A settings dashboard for every contextual permission before value exists.
- One generalized “card” schema that owns semantics, presentation, and action.
- A separate mode for travel, post-trip, or quiet life that suppresses other
  horizons.
- A learning model optimized primarily on clicks, acceptance, disclosure, or
  conversion.

## 12. Comparable end-to-end portfolio

### 12.1 S1 — Ordinary New York

**Potential output:** one complete-on-view opening grounded in current city,
calendar boundary, prior evidence, and a real possibility this weekend. It may
be cultural, practical, social, spatial, or a deliberate direct-state unit.

**Correct behavior:** no empty-state prompt, no Push, no Italy-dominated recap,
no generic “because you like” recommendation. Home can be visually quiet while
intellectually alive. Chat deepens or redirects. Places shows the relevant
world field. Life owns why the prior evidence was eligible.

**Architecture exercised:** ContributionBrief, weak/boundary receptivity,
portfolio treatment, projection resolver, memory application, silence.

### 12.2 S2 — Returned home

**Potential output:** factual reconstruction from tickets/photos/places plus one
new relation that re-enters present New York or an open question. Social
juxtaposition appears only when permissioned and when it changes understanding.

**Correct behavior:** no forced debrief, no global personality interpretation,
no nostalgic sealed mode. The past is one current-life resource among several.

**Architecture exercised:** source attribution, governed claims, authorship,
structural transfer, multi-medium projection, causal forgetting.

### 12.3 S3 — Live Rome

**Potential output:** operationally safe route or alternative first, with one
compact world-anchored cultural contribution only when attention permits.

**Correct behavior:** use provider infrastructure; compare experienced
reachability; interpret heat/route/closing facts rather than diagnosing energy;
return eyes to the city; interrupt only for material expiring consequence.

**Architecture exercised:** situation envelope, affordance assessment,
receptivity, treatment ceiling, degraded connectivity, provider readback.

### 12.4 S4 — Brooklyn dinner

**Potential output:** host creates through Chat; Vesper privately prepares
people and options; invitation meaning is host-authored; one shared choice is
grounded under a temporary constitution; action proceeds under mandate;
Occurrence and Outcomes remain plural; future opening is optional.

**Correct behavior:** no Occasion profile, no merged preferences, no forced
poll, no Vesper-authored care, no activity feed, and no reply debt.

**Architecture exercised:** exact audience/custody, authorship roles,
constitution, GroundingReceipt, action authority, plural outcome, addressed
continuity.

### 12.5 Portfolio acceptance criteria

The same architecture passes only if:

- every situation can produce value without first requesting new user labor;
- direct state, rich contribution, prepared consequence, and silence all remain
  available treatments;
- one object or contribution stays recognizable across roots;
- current conditions can override memory without rewriting it;
- private context improves but never leaks into shared explanation;
- socially diagnostic meaning remains human-authored;
- provider and social consequences expose exact authority and recovery;
- corrections invalidate every dependent projection and learning application;
  and
- the cumulative Home composition still reads as one life, not four product
  demos.

## 13. Research execution program

### Phase A — Founder decision pass

Ratify, reject, or modify the eight recommended Round 1 decisions in §2. Record
accepted choices as small amendments or decision records. Do not promote this
entire research document into canon.

### Phase B — Contract fixtures before schema work

Create serializable fixture-only versions of the seven contracts in §11 across
S1–S4. Reuse existing identifiers and authorities. The goal is to expose
missing semantics and contradictions, not to select persistence technology.

### Phase C — Comparative experience prototypes

For each situation, author baseline, underreach, overreach, recommended, and
silence conditions. Prototype the complete root transitions and one consequence,
not isolated cards. Use a dedicated visual design tool after the semantic and
interaction treatments are stable.

### Phase D — Adversarial and accessibility pass

Test source deletion, stale world data, wrong novelty estimate, location denied,
offline, large type, nonvisual order, former relationship, roster change,
private constraint, AI-authored care, irreversible exposure, provider failure,
and sponsored candidate conflict.

### Phase E — Shadow instrumentation

Emit content-free receipts for contribution, affordance, receptivity, treatment,
projection, authority, and outcome without changing product behavior. Validate
join completeness, expiry, invalidation, and privacy before turning on selection.

### Phase F — Longitudinal field study

Study the whole system across ordinary, live/travel, and social occasions with
a Vesper-pause interval. The key release evidence is not that one content unit
was liked. It is that the second occasion improves, capability or attention
transfers, people retain authority, social interaction remains human, and the
app can recede.
