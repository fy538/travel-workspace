## 2. What needs to change

**Different values have similar presentations.** Much of the current field follows
title → facts → paragraph → continuation. A comparison, event, everyday venue and
human contribution consequently feel like more items in one recommendation list.

**Sections classify more than they compose.** “This weekend,” “Any day,” “Worth
seeing” and “Worth knowing” are understandable buckets, but arrangement can do more:
show proximity, meaningful contrast, changing conditions or different perspectives.
The before/after architectural plans are a useful existing example to build from.

**The example world feels narrow.** Film, listening event, exhibition and church
material is worthwhile but dominates the personality. Give food, outdoor time,
practical uses of a place, informal social opportunities and neighborhood exploration
equally specific treatment. Do not substitute generic lifestyle category tiles.

**Type is doing too much of the differentiation.** Source inspection found italic
mainly in repeated work titles such as Playtime and Music for 18 Musicians, not
every paragraph. Their prominence still contributes to the literary effect. Address
that effect; do not confuse repeated comparison canvases with one consumer scroll.

**Map-first versus content-first is insufficient.** Board 10 usefully compares the
opening, but a map above similar units does not resolve full-scroll composition.
Explore maps within sections where spatial relationships are themselves the value.

Target: **a world opening up, not a well-written list of things in it.** Quiet
aesthetics must not imply low information value or monotonous composition.

## 5. Explore a small reusable composition vocabulary

Separate **what value is delivered** from **how the information is arranged**.
A human moment, a reason to return and a curiosity are not necessarily different
component families. A friend's contribution can sit within a comparison, a spatial
pocket, a lead or a compact row. Avoid rebuilding one content type → one section.

| Value / information relationship | Arrangements worth trying | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A worthwhile possibility and related options | Lead + siblings; compact assortment | One discovery with lower-weight alternatives |
| Proximity, access or complementary uses | Map fragment + entries; optional short sequence | A useful neighborhood pocket, not a committed itinerary |
| Contrast or change | Paired alternatives; before–after; compact comparison | Different waterfront experiences; a changed room or place |
| Original attention or plural perspectives | Photo/note; paired voices; contribution inside another group | One person's experience or two separately attributed observations |
| Understanding or enjoyment complete without action | Diagram; reading cover; compact explanation; supported audio | A spatial mechanism or worthwhile piece to consume |
| Existing context becomes useful now | Small contextual line; lead; comparison; spatial pocket | A saved or visited place becomes relevant to an outing |
| Further exploration | Substantive collection preview; lead + siblings; compact assortment | Another neighborhood or subject with a clear way inward |

These are combinations to explore, not mandatory sections, a complete renderer
inventory or new backend types. Demonstrate at least one arrangement supporting
different kinds of value, and one kind of value working in different arrangements.
Choose based on what becomes easier to understand, not novelty alone.

After selecting a composition, consolidate its reusable families and name minimum
content, compact form, optional depth and fallback without media or personal context.
Do not invent precision to make a diagram attractive. Audio is optional supported
value, not a requirement for new personalized generation.

## 6. Typography and containment

1. Default to upright, scannable titles. Compare selective serif and sans roles in
   actual scrolls; do not remove the brand merely to eliminate italics.
2. Make italic exceptional. Prominent UI titles do not require bibliographic italics.
   Avoid long italic headlines and automatic italic styling for human quotes.
3. Reduce stacked labels. A mono section label, mono item label and title should
   not routinely repeat the same information.
4. Use cards to group related information too. A container need not claim special
   authority or recommendation confidence. Avoid both nested-card clutter and a
   uniformly unbounded wall of editorial text.
5. Let comparisons, maps and timing structure replace prose when they explain
   better. Do not hide material conditions merely to reduce text.
6. Vary density intentionally: a substantial unit, compact set, spatial composition
   and human moment need not share one anatomy or every possible action.

**Cohesion is as important as variety.** Do not turn the scroll into a component
showroom. Across arrangements, keep object identity, attribution, practical facts,
action language and navigation recognizable. The same place remains recognizable
whether it appears in a comparison, on a map or beside a friend's note.

Make the opening behavior legible: tapping a group, an individual place, a reading,
or a person's contribution may lead to different subjects. Show clear affordances
without adding a button wall or making a whole-card target compete with nested
targets. Keep a consistent spatial rhythm and hierarchy while varying arrangements.

Test normal phone width, long names and enlarged text with board annotations hidden.
Do not shrink type or require horizontal scrolling to compare essential information.
Propose shared design-system changes explicitly; do not silently apply them to Home,
Chat or Life.

### 12.3 Priority 1 — Imagery and human-photo treatment

| Material | Desired visual role | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Human contributions | Original photographs with natural color, framing and imperfections; recognizable authorship | Making a friend's image look like Vesper's illustration, or polishing every human moment into promotional content |
