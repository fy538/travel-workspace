# Illustration Brief — The Life Zero-Record Threshold

## 1. Product judgment

Life needs an honest treatment for the rare state in which no governed record
exists yet. Pure whitespace can look broken or make Life feel like an archive
the person is responsible for filling. A warm illustration can establish the
character of the surface and make one expectation legible:

> Ordinary things can become a record the person can later find, understand,
> and use again. The person does not maintain that record as homework.

This does **not** justify illustrated placeholders throughout Life. The
illustration is a one-time threshold treatment for a genuinely zero-record
root, not the product's cold-start strategy and not a system for filling four
separate categories.

The real cold-start strategy lives in the product loop: one ordinary thing
brought to Vesper should produce immediate representational gain. A ticket can
become an accurate, attributed, searchable Artifact; several supported things
can form a Journey; an authorized contribution can appear in the right shared
episode. Real evidence replaces the threshold illustration as soon as it
exists.

## 2. The Life model the artwork must express

Life is **one governed corpus seen through four lenses**:

**Time · Places · Threads · People**

These are not four containers the person completes independently. One dinner
with Maya in Rome may be simultaneously:

- a moment in Time;
- part of a relationship with a Place;
- evidence within a pasta Thread; and
- an episode with Maya.

The illustration should therefore suggest that an ordinary lived moment has
several dimensions already present within it. It must not depict an item being
processed into one bucket, or four buckets waiting to be filled.

The artwork may communicate warmth, continuity, plurality, and being held. It
must not attempt to explain the complete custody, correction, audience,
withdrawal, or deletion contract. Those truths belong to inspectable product
surfaces, not to a decorative illustration.

## 3. State policy (binding)

The illustration renders only when the governed Life corpus is truly empty.
Its presence is state-driven, not a permanent onboarding step.

### 3.1 Zero-record root

- Preserve the normal Life masthead, four labeled lenses, and bottom tabs.
- Render one frameless illustration, followed by at most one short sentence.
- Do not render a button, prompt, checklist, example gallery, progress meter,
  completion score, or suggested contribution.
- Do not imply that the person should manufacture a memory for Vesper.

### 3.2 First real record

The illustration disappears when the first governed Artifact, episode,
Journey, Place relationship, Thread, or attributed shared object exists. The
real object becomes the explanation of Life's value. Do not retain a tutorial
strip beside it.

### 3.3 Empty lens inside a nonempty Life

First project the existing corpus honestly through the selected lens. A lens
is a view over the same corpus, so one held object may support several views.

If the lens is genuinely empty, preserve its ordinary page anatomy and use at
most one plain indicative sentence, such as:

> No one else is part of this record yet.

Do not show a lens-specific illustration, CTA, or visual target that implies
category completion.

### 3.4 Missing conditional organ

A missing Return, reflection, window, shared episode, or other conditional
organ is **air**. It leaves no placeholder or explanation. Yielded Returns also
leave no shell.

### 3.5 Search and operational emptiness

No-result, loading, error, offline, and partial-authority states are functional
states, not uses of this illustration. They preserve page anatomy and receive
their own truthful operational copy and recovery behavior.

## 4. What to make

Explore **three genuinely different whole compositions** for the zero-record
Life root. One composition may eventually ship. Do not produce twelve
per-lens strips.

Individual objects may be generated separately for compositing and visual
consistency, but every candidate must be judged as a complete phone-scale
composition.

### Direction A — One moment, several dimensions

One ordinary episode contains quiet signs of Time, Place, Thread, and People.
The leading candidate is a sparse dinner seen from above:

- two simple place settings or one plate and a second pulled-in place;
- a small ticket edge or date-like notch as temporal residue, with no text or
  numerals;
- a cup, saucer, threshold curve, or other spatial cue;
- steam or a loose line becoming a thread;
- one understated version of the held-cards glyph integrated into the scene,
  not placed as a processing step.

The composition should read first as one warm lived moment. Its several
dimensions should reveal themselves second. Avoid four equally weighted icons
around a center; that would become a taxonomy diagram.

### Direction B — Ordinary things gather

A loose still life of a few culturally unmarked objects—a ticket, cup, small
photograph, and addressed note—settles around or partially overlaps the
held-cards glyph. Proximity, overlap, and shared ink rhythm suggest continuity.

There are no arrows, intake funnel, before/after sequence, or distinct output
symbol. The image says "these belong to one record," not "these were processed
by a system."

### Direction C — The first held thing opens outward

One ordinary object, such as a ticket or photograph, sits in gentle physical
continuity with the held-cards glyph. From the same compact composition emerge
subtle motifs associated with the four lenses: a line of days, an arched edge,
a loose thread, and a paired or stitched mark.

This direction must feel like organic visual continuation, not transformation
machinery. If it reads as `input -> database -> four outputs`, reject it.

### Open exploration

Additional whole-composition directions are welcome when they better express:

- ordinary life rather than aspirational travel;
- one record with overlapping dimensions;
- automatic continuity rather than filing;
- warmth without fabricated personal history; and
- a beginning without incompleteness pressure.

## 5. Visual style (binding)

**Risograph two-ink print, hand-drawn, on warm paper.** Think Japanese riso
zine or a lovingly printed café flyer—not flat-vector corporate illustration,
3D rendering, gradient mesh, clip-art, emoji, or AI-magic decoration.

- **Inks:** exactly teal `#4E7A6F` and coral `#C4604F`. Tints made through
  opacity or halftone are allowed. Teal is structural; coral carries living
  warmth, emphasis, or connection.
- **Paper:** `#EFEAE0`. Deliver on transparency or flat paper color. The paper
  is the background; never add a white or colored panel, border, card, shadow,
  vignette, or postcard frame.
- **Line quality:** confident, slightly imperfect hand-drawn contours, medium
  weight, approximately a 2px line at 330px display width. Open contours are
  welcome.
- **Print character:** restrained grain, a hint of misregistration, and subtle
  overprint darkening may appear. Legibility takes priority over texture.
- **Density:** sparse, airy, and readable at a glance. No room, sky, floor, or
  decorative scenic background; the paper is the world.
- **Tone:** warm, calm, observant, and a little wry. Objects over people. No
  faces. Hands may appear only as minimal gesture lines.
- **Text:** none inside the artwork. Product copy is typeset natively below.

Sibling references are the fine-ink fanned-card-and-clock Life glyph and the
riso-duotone keepsakes. The illustration sits between them: warmer than a UI
glyph, quieter and less evidentiary than a keepsake.

Generated illustration must remain visibly decorative. It must never resemble
a real photograph, ticket, note, or other evidence that the person might
mistake for something Vesper actually holds about them.

## 6. The held-cards glyph

The existing symbol for "held" is three or four slightly rotated
rounded-rectangle cards, fanned like a loose hand of playing cards, drawn in
teal contour. The frontmost card carries a small coral clock face: a circle and
two hands, with no numerals.

The glyph is optional inside the illustration because it already appears in
the Life masthead. When used, integrate it into the whole composition and keep
it subordinate to the lived scene. Always test a version without it. Do not
put it in a mandatory middle stage, stamp it repeatedly, or make it look like
a database or upload destination.

Generate it once as a standalone transparent asset if that is the best way to
preserve shape and line consistency.

## 7. Subject palette

Possible objects include:

- a paper ticket with notched edges and no text;
- one or two plates seen from above;
- a cup and saucer with coral steam;
- a small landscape photograph with no identifiable landmark;
- an open book paired with one loose ticket;
- an addressed-looking envelope with no writing and a coral seal;
- two place settings, or a table with one chair pulled in;
- footsteps turning a corner;
- a simple shop threshold without signage; and
- two teal water lines beneath a coral sun.

Use only what the composition needs. Avoid landmarks, flags, brands,
holidays, religious symbols, luxury cues, and alcohol-forward framing. The
illustration must not imply that Life is primarily a travel diary.

## 8. Copy exploration

The copy is reassurance and orientation, not a prompt. It should be
indicative, concrete, and short enough to disappear beneath the image rather
than becoming onboarding prose.

Leading candidate:

> Things you bring, ask about, and share can settle here as a record you can
> return to.

Alternates to evaluate:

> What becomes part of your record gathers here—through time, places,
> threads, and people.

> The things Vesper is allowed to hold gather here as a record you can return
> to.

The first is the warmest and most behavioral. The third is most explicit about
authority but may be too contractual for the root. Do not write "add your
first," "start capturing," "build your Life," "nothing here yet," or any
question addressed to the person.

Copy and illustration must not jointly over-explain the same mechanism. If the
image makes plurality legible, the sentence should emphasize automatic
continuity. If the image emphasizes continuity, the sentence may name the four
lenses.

## 9. Format and production

- **Placement:** one frameless composition in the zero-record Life root, below
  the masthead/lens controls and above the single reassurance sentence.
- **Aspect:** explore approximately 1.8:1 to 2.4:1 rather than a fixed
  three-panel strip. Compose for roughly 300–340 CSS px width on a phone.
- **Master size:** render at least 1320px wide for downsampling and crop tests.
- **Delivery:** transparent PNG is preferred for bitmap exploration. Separate
  object layers plus one flattened composition are most useful. SVG is
  acceptable only if it preserves the handmade print character.
- **Theme:** light theme only. Dark-product canon remains deferred.
- **Verification:** evaluate on the actual `#EFEAE0` root background at target
  phone size, under Dynamic Type, and within the full zero-record scroll—not
  only on a contact sheet.

## 10. Hard constraints

A candidate fails if it includes any of the following:

- four illustrated empty-lens modules or a separate strip per lens;
- arrows, panels, numbered beats, conveyor/funnel imagery, or another explicit
  process diagram;
- four empty containers that imply category completion;
- a card, border, background panel, drop shadow, vignette, or postcard frame;
- any hue beyond teal, coral, and paper, except natural overprint darkening;
- text, letters, numbers, logos, UI chrome, phone imagery, screenshots, or app
  icons;
- faces, detailed figures, or a specific real person or place;
- cursors, tap indicators, plus-buttons, checkmarks, progress indicators, AI
  sparkles, or instructional gestures;
- fabricated personal evidence or imagery that could be mistaken for it;
- more visual material than can be understood in roughly two seconds; or
- a composition that makes Life look primarily like a travel archive.

## 11. Evaluation checklist

Score each whole composition against these questions:

1. Does it first feel like ordinary life rather than product machinery?
2. Does it suggest one record with overlapping dimensions rather than four
   buckets?
3. Does it communicate warmth without pretending to know the person already?
4. Does it imply that continuity happens naturally, without filing homework?
5. Is the held-cards glyph recognizable but not depicted as a database stage?
6. Does it remain clearly decorative rather than resembling lived evidence?
7. Is it frameless and native to `#EFEAE0`?
8. Does the two-ink riso language survive at phone size?
9. Can the illustration and one sentence be understood without a CTA?
10. Does the root become unnecessary—and disappear gracefully—once one real
    record exists?

The strongest candidate is not the one that explains the most. It is the one
that establishes the correct relationship with Life using the least visual and
verbal machinery.

## 12. Working method for image generation

1. Generate and lock a small set of isolated riso objects plus one consistent
   held-cards glyph.
2. Compose 4–6 rough candidates for each of Directions A, B, and C.
3. Reject any candidate that reads as a flowchart before refining texture.
4. Place surviving candidates in the full Life zero-record root at actual
   phone size.
5. Compare the illustration against a typography-only control. It ships only
   if it materially improves comprehension, warmth, and confidence without
   increasing homework or false richness.
6. Refine no more than three finalists. Select at most one production
   composition.

Do not evaluate the work primarily as standalone illustration. Its job is to
make the zero-record Life root feel intentional for a brief threshold, then
yield completely to the person's real record.

## 13. Round-one image-generation findings — 2026-09-01

Assets:

- `assets/life-zero-record-illustration-round-1/direction-a-one-moment.png`
- `assets/life-zero-record-illustration-round-1/direction-b-ordinary-things.png`
- `assets/life-zero-record-illustration-round-1/direction-c-opens-outward.png`
- `assets/life-zero-record-illustration-round-1/direction-b-refined.png`

The two-ink riso language worked immediately. It feels warm, distinctive, and
compatible with the paper surface without becoming childish or corporate.
The strategic composition question remains more important than texture.

### A — one moment, several dimensions

Semantically closest to the product philosophy, because the image first reads
as one episode rather than a collection. The generated candidate is too dense,
food-specific, and large-objected for a 330px root treatment. It would become
illustrative content rather than a quiet threshold. Preserve the episode idea,
not this execution.

### B — ordinary things gather

The strongest first-round direction. It reads warmly and coherently without
arrows. The original is too full and the held-cards glyph becomes a central
archive symbol. The refined version proves that more air and fewer objects
improve the treatment substantially.

Its remaining weakness is strategic: cup + ticket + landscape photograph
still codes Life as a travel scrapbook. The next version should exchange at
least two of these objects for an ordinary local/social moment and test whether
the held glyph is needed at all.

### C — the first held thing opens outward

Reject. Even without arrows, the outward lines and abstract motifs read as a
system diagram. This confirms that the failure of the earlier story strips was
semantic, not merely stylistic. Do not spend another round polishing this
direction.

### Next visual test

Combine A's meaning with B's restraint: one very small ordinary episode, ample
paper, and no illustrated process. Test two cups or two place settings, one
loose note or book edge, and one living coral line. Produce paired candidates
with and without the held-cards glyph. The UI masthead may already carry enough
"held" meaning; the illustration may only need to supply ordinary life and
relational warmth.

None of the round-one assets is production-selected. Their generated canvas
ratios also exceed the intended horizontal treatment, so phone composition and
crop remain unvalidated.

### Paired glyph test

Assets:

- `assets/life-zero-record-illustration-round-1/paired-test-without-held-glyph.png`
- `assets/life-zero-record-illustration-round-1/paired-test-with-held-glyph.png`

The paired ordinary-moment test resolves the glyph question for the next
round. The composition without the held-cards glyph reads as a human scene:
two distinct cups, one shared interval, and a nearby book connected by a living
line. It is calm, non-instructional, and not travel-coded.

The otherwise comparable glyph version makes the book and cards read as a
product mechanism concealed beneath the scene. It divides the image into
"life" and "system" instead of letting one moment remain whole.

**Round-one ruling:** omit the held-cards glyph from the illustration. The
existing Life masthead owns system identity and the caption owns the continuity
promise. The illustration's singular job is ordinary relational warmth.

`paired-test-without-held-glyph.png` is the leading visual direction, not yet a
production asset. It still requires placement in the actual zero-record root,
phone-scale crop/legibility review, and comparison with the typography-only
control before selection.
