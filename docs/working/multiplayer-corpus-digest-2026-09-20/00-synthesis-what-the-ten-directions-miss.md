# What the ten multiplayer directions miss — corpus synthesis, 2026-09-20

## 1. Authority and method

This is a reading aid and an argument, not canon. It adopts nothing and authorizes nothing.

- **Read in full by the session author:** `travel-agent/docs/product/Multiplayer Product Strategy.md` (canon, 871 lines).
- **Read in full by five parallel reader subagents, ~14,000 lines:** the founder-thread strategy docs (08-07 → 08-23), seven research memos, the design lineage (05-20 → 09-07), the contracts and system charter, and the philosophical foundations. Their digests are files `01`–`05` in this folder. They are extractions with section citations; **verify any claim against its source before relying on it.** Where a digest and a source disagree, the source wins.
- **Compared against:** the ten directions D1–D10 of the [Multiplayer product-shapes brief](../claude-design-multiplayer-product-shapes-exploration-2026-09-20.md), drawn as Pass A in Claude Design project `caf916f9-c0ce-466b-92ee-49ebefe1ca38`.

## 2. Finding: the exploration is smaller than the canon

D1–D10 descend from one subsection of the canon — §1 "Social benefit before social structure". Measured against the rest:

| The canon names | Covered by D1–D10 | Absent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8 modes (§4): Handoff, Caucus, Mandate, Our Place, Repair, Convergence, Ritual, Relay | Handoff (D3), featured sharing (D1), Repair (D8 frame 2), a sliver of Our Place (D2, D10) | Caucus, Mandate, Ritual, Relay; and true Convergence — Vesper notices an opening and checks privately with each person, one decline produces silence. D5–D7 are all human-initiated |
| 6 social units (§3) | Pair, Solo, thin Recurring circle | Ephemeral party, Family or household, Host and guest |
| 9 kinds of contribution (§1.3) | Perspective, evidence, some practical labor | Presence, care and boundaries, initiative, intent, meaning |
| §1.1 Social accessibility — "Invitation is not incorporation" | — | The newcomer's route into a social world |
| §7 Resolve, do not poll | — | Protect the worst-served member, track repeated compromise, propose a subgroup split |
| §8 Plural memory and the continuity gradient | D10, lightly | One evening with different private outcomes; "remember privately" / "make me reachable" / "invite us again" as distinct acts |
| §11 sequencing, 9 steps | 1–4 | 5–9: second-occasion memory, public Place contribution, reactivation, convergence, co-presence |

The brief's reading-order line lists six modes; the canon has eight (Mandate and Repair were dropped in transcription).

## 3. Diagnosis: boundaries written against mechanisms were enforced against values

The design-lineage digest (`04`) traces the narrowing from the May social-loop log to the September 7 brief. One move repeats: a correct boundary is applied to a dangerous mechanism, and the human value that mechanism carried is cut with it.

| Boundary (correct) | Value lost with it |
| --- | --- |
| No friend map, no live presence | Orientation in my people's worlds — "What are my friends noticing in Paris, Brooklyn, Shanghai, and Rome?" (aperture handoff §1.2) |
| No profile ritual, no personas | Being known: the self mirror, "how this changes with people", "how you appear", the pair-level Together view (profile doc §9, §12 — once "highest-leverage" prototype 1) |
| No viewed / saved / used reports | Contributor efficacy — the giver learning what their contribution changed (prominence brief MP1) |
| No stranger matching | The newcomer, the host, the met-once acquaintance, the public Place commons |
| Pair-first, for adoption | Groups of 3–8, which are the launch wedge |

The aperture handoff named the cost in advance (§1.2): "Multiplayer invisibility … perceived as personal AI with occasional collaboration."

## 4. A selection test for Vesper-sized social ideas

The 08-07 strategy identifies the one link competitors cannot copy: governed private context across several people. Pass A's own verdicts repeatedly concluded "a group message does this". Proposed test for any further direction:

> Does it need private context from more than one person, shaping a shared result, without that context being disclosed? If not, ordinary messaging is the better product.

Things a chat structurally cannot do: a protected anchor or surprise, a private caucus, a newcomer's private brief, plural memory of one evening, shared perception across time, the Place commons.

## 5. Territories the corpus has thought through and the ten directions dropped

1. **Getting into the room** — the newcomer and the host. "Many gatherings have stairs into participation but no ramps" (gathering doc §5.1); five levels of feeling wanted (§5.2); "hospitality intelligence, not guest dossiers" (§6.5); the relationship gradient and "keep in orbit" (§10). Every invitation to a non-user is this moment, so it coincides with the growth loop. Digest `01`.
2. **The group decides** — caucus, mandate, resolve-don't-poll, worst-served protection, sequential fairness, split and reconvene "as abundance, not division" (Interaction Design §7), the diplomatic layer (§8). "Every competitor sells speed of decision. Not one sells relief from the role" (08-07 §4.4). Much of it is shipped (group-social charter). Digests `02`, `03`, `05`.
3. **Shared perception** — "from preference coordination to shared perception" (08-18 §8); distributed attention; an absent parent or host changing today's encounter (08-21 §1.3); "route curiosity to the person who can make the place more meaningful" (08-18 §8.2). Digest `02`.
4. **The Place commons** — positioned, disagreeing, attributed answers from residents, workers, historians and recent visitors; hospitality left for a future visitor; contributors seeing the outcome; reciprocity so residents are not "unpaid destination content creators" (08-18 §8.10, 08-21 §2.3). Canon §11 step 6. Digests `01`, `02`, `04`.
5. **A shared life over years** — rituals, return promises, unfinished threads with an owner, anticipation as lived value, the trip as "temporary shared life" (gathering doc §13), and the relationship lifecycle through estrangement, reconciliation and death directives (Life social-lifecycle matrix). Digests `01`, `04`, `05`.

**The settled line on strangers** (canon §12 against §11 step 6): people who do not know each other may reach one another through a Place and a deliberate contribution — never through live presence, proximity, or matching. This resolves the apparent conflict between 08-07 §7.2 ("No strangers. Ever") and the 08-18 public realm.

## 6. What no document in the corpus addresses

- **The regular / third places.** "Fringeships" are defined once (consumer-psychology memo §8) with supporting evidence on acquaintance contact and belonging, then never developed.
- **Someone's city, handed to you.** Moving, visiting, hosting, and long-distance friendship. The founder's own sentence — "Making the world feel like it's mine, even at a foreign place … especially with people" (foundations §12) — has no social design.
- **Keeping a secret together.** A "private protected anchor may legitimately shape a shared day without being disclosed" (foundations §3.14, the Capri proposal). Surprise, gifts, milestones.
- **Looking after someone.** "Care and boundaries" is a named contribution kind with no design; household members without accounts are named and not drawn.
- **Family as a time arc; generosity** (reciprocity is called a "constitutional tension", foundations §26, and dropped); **the socially sparse or overloaded person; loneliness; neighborhoods; intergenerational relationships.**

## 8. Proposed Pass A′ — eight further directions

Each held to the §4 test, each exploratory, none adopted: **E1** Getting into the room · **E2** The group decides · **E3** Seeing more through each other · **E4** The place's people (the commons) · **E5** Ours, over years · **E6** The regular · **E7** Someone's city, handed to you · **E8** Keeping a secret together. Named and not yet drawn: looking after someone, family as a time arc, generosity, the socially sparse person.
