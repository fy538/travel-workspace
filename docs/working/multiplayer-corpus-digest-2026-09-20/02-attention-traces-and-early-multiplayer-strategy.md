## DOCUMENT 1. Attention Traces, Consequential Memory, and Multiplayer Perception (2026-08-18)

`/Users/feihuyan/travel-workspace/travel-agent/docs/archive/2026-08/pivot-research/attention-traces-consequential-memory-and-multiplayer-perception-2026-08-18.md`

### A. Social values / human payoffs

- An interaction gives immediate help and can also improve "what happens now or later for me and the people I am with" (§1 hypothesis).
- Ordinary, imperfect capture lowers the performance burden; it separates private attention from public self-presentation (§2.2, §6).
- A cheap answer gains "appropriate continuity" by belonging to an enduring, governed system (§2.3).
- A casual image carries relational meaning: "this is where I am," "this made me think of you," "help me understand this" (§2.5).
- Forty captures compress into a few threads plus "a beautiful, recognizable account of the occasion" (§5.3).
- Value returns at six timescales: now, later in the occasion, during the journey, afterward, on a later occasion, and back in ordinary life. The last connects a distant curiosity to a nearby continuation (§5.4).
- Memory gives direction to curiosity: deepen, connect, contrast, continue, socially translate, or retire. It should not become "a more flattering filter bubble" (§6.5).
- Distributed attention: several people together form "a richer perception of the Place than any one member could form alone" (§8.1).
- Borrowed lenses: a companion's noticed detail, a local friend's situational judgment, an absent friend's story that becomes relevant on arrival, or one member's question that opens a perspective for the others (§8.2).
- The AI routes curiosity "to the person who can make the place more meaningful" and so activates human relationships (§8.2, Law 10).
- Shared occasions stay plural. There is no manufactured group interpretation, and unresolved disagreement is allowed (§8.3).
- Group decisions need no preference survey. The group makes one legible decision from evidence that normal participation already produced, and private reasons never leak (§8.4).
- Shared open loops enable "meaningful second occasions." These include promised reveals, skipped experiences, a tradition expected to change across regions, and a ritual worth continuing (§8.5).
- Relational proactivity can reopen a thread when the relevant people are together again, find where distinct interests intersect, and invite a member to contribute a lens. It can also protect a private need, recognize when people should separate and reconvene, and stay silent when the moment is sufficient (§8.6).
- A recurring group gets "a portrait of a relationship, offered for recognition rather than asserted as identity" (§8.7).
- Asynchronous shared attention lets one person "see a Place with another person, even across distance and time." The other person can be a distant friend, parent, host, or prior companion (§8.9).
- The sender later reveals what happened, and both people carry differently scoped memories (§8.9).
- Public place commons: a visitor asks a concrete question from inside an actual encounter. Plural attributed perspectives answer, and "disagreement is information" (§8.10).
- Local contributors get reciprocal value. They can correct misconceptions, preserve a practice, encourage respectful behavior, and support a local institution or business. They can also meet others who care about a subject and see what became of a perspective they offered (§8.10).
- Place handoffs: "a question, story, route detail, or act of hospitality intentionally left for a future visitor" (§8.10).
- A private observation can improve world freshness for future users after verification (§7).
- The measured value is "user-reported presence rather than phone distraction" (§12.3).
- Correction and forgetting change downstream use, not just what is visible (§10.4).

### B. Named concepts

- **Granola analogy (§3).** Granola's useful structure is not transcription. Meeting audio supplies broad context and the participant's scattered notes mark what entered human attention. The product compiles something more coherent than either source alone.
  - The Vesper analogue: "world substrate + person and group attention traces → coherent lived memory and authorized consequence."
  - The world substrate is Place identity, time and location, route, Plan or occasion, companions, verified conditions, prior questions, and later outcomes.
  - Attention traces are a question, photo, screenshot, voice fragment, save-with-reason, repeated follow-up, correction, choice or rejection, and a lightweight outcome.
  - "The world model supplies context. Human attention supplies emphasis."
  - Stated limit: life is not a meeting to be continuously recorded. The analogy "does not authorize ambient audio capture, total recall, or one allegedly objective account."
- **Multiplayer perception (§8).** This is "the major multiplayer expansion is from preference coordination to shared perception."
  - Product law: "Shared place, plural experience, governed common action."
  - Components are §8.1–§8.10: distributed attention, borrowed lenses, divergence without consensus, evidence-grounded decisions, shared open loops, relational proactivity, group character, multiplayer as a consequence path, asynchronous shared attention, and three governed spaces.
- **Attention trace (§4.1).** A low-friction, user-originated indication that something became salient, independent of medium. It does not prove presence, enjoyment, preference, completion, meaning, or permission to share.
- **Occasion (§4.2).** The bounded context that makes scattered interactions coherent. No Trip or itinerary is required, and "an ordinary Friday evening in Brooklyn" qualifies.
- **Encounter (§4.3).** The canonical smallest visible artifact joining something in the world to what it meant. A trace can begin one but does not automatically become one.
- **Consequence (§4.4).** Six types: epistemic, operational, relational, longitudinal, reflective, silent. "Silence is one consequence policy."
- **Resolution and recall lifecycle (§4.5).** Question → answer → optional intention → outcome or unresolved → selective memory → relevant later recall.
- **Relational memory (§4.6).** The durable unit is the quadruple `(person, place, companion, occasion)`.
- **Relational encounter object (§4.7).** An image directed to a person that accumulates replies, perspectives, decisions, outcomes, and later continuations. "The media … is not the whole object."
- **Compounding participation loop / curiosity loop (§5).** "Show Vesper what made you wonder."
- **Value ladder (§5.1).** Immediate utility → situated understanding → visible accumulation → consequential memory → proactive expansion.
- **Attention hierarchy (§5.2).** Seven tiers, from "explicitly corrected/adopted" down to "merely nearby." "Active attention should outweigh surveillance exhaust."
- **Compression is the product skill (§5.3).**
- **Canonical movement anchor (§6.2).** A ticket treated as a journey-segment anchor.
- **Cross-system compounding map / strategic flywheel (§7).** Fifteen systems. The world model and personal model improve each other without merging.
- **Multiplayer as a consequence path, not a visibility default (§8.8).** Separate transitions run from private → lens handoff → group proposal → shared outcome. "Capturing in a group does not imply sharing with the group."
- **Asynchronous shared attention (§8.9).** Choosing a recipient is "a bounded attention signal," not a hidden social score.
- **Three governed spaces (§8.10).** Personal, Friends-and-family, and Public place realm. They exchange safe projections and never merge evidence, permissions, or audiences.
- **Public place realm / living place commons (§8.10).** Organized "around the Place and the encounter, not around follower growth."
- **Candidate public interactions (§8.10).** Place questions, local lenses, time-bounded observations, public attention threads, place handoffs, outcome returns, bounded invitations.
- **Group-formation ladder (§8.10).** Public perspective → repeated or reciprocal interaction → bounded invitation → explicit shared occasion → governed group.
- **Encounter projections (§8.10).** Private, relational, governed-group, and public-Place projections.
- **Audience gradient (§10.2).** Ephemeral → privately retained → one named person → bounded group → deliberately contributed to the public. It is "not an automatic promotion ladder."
- **Four independent permissions (§10.2).** Media visibility, retention, inference, recall and reuse.
- **Evidence ladder (§10.1).** Eight layers, from captured evidence to future consequence.
- **Relational recall formula (§9.3).** Five answerable questions: why this, why now, why this person, what next, why permitted.
- **Minimal capture interaction (§9.1).** Consequence chips: `I tried this / Keep quietly / Ask the group / Remind me nearby / Just answer`.
- **Thread as the My World unit (§9.2).**
- **17 Product Laws (§13).**

### C. Big / ambitious ideas (quotes under 25 words)

- Public commons with strangers: "help a Place become legible through the plural people who know, inhabit, and encounter it" (§8.10 guardrails).
- Plural public voices: "Residents, workers, historians, institutions, businesses, recent visitors… can offer attributed and situated perspectives" (§8.10).
- New groups formed from strangers: "New-group formation should follow demonstrated interest and explicit invitation rather than begin as random stranger matching" (§8.10).
- Locals as participants with their own stakes: "'Local' is not one identity." The doc also notes "Deeper hosted access or expertise may eventually deserve payment" (§8.10).
- Hospitality across time: "an act of hospitality intentionally left for a future visitor" (§8.10).
- Collective perception: "Several people can inhabit the same occasion, notice different layers, and help one another experience more" (§1).
- AI steps back for a human: "Sometimes the best AI action is to route curiosity to the person who can make the place more meaningful" (§8.2).
- Group identity over a long arc: "You tend to remember trips through one improvised meal, one long walk, and one place someone teaches the others how to see" (§8.7).
- Distance and time: "a way for one person to see a Place with another person, even across distance and time" (§8.9).
- Parents, hosts, and prior companions are named as participants in shared attention (§8.9).
- Private observation feeding the world: "Corroborated or verified observations after privacy-safe review → fresher place state for future users" (§7).
- "Multiplayer should help people experience the world through one another" (Law 9).

### D. Deferred / gated / open

- Nothing is authorized: "No schema, camera integration, photo-library permission, notification policy, or new root surface is authorized" (§11). The reason given is that it needs product testing, privacy design, architecture review, and surface design.
- Open (§14.2):
  - the retention default (per trace, per occasion mode, or reversible private default);
  - honest copy for keep, learn, and share;
  - when to ask for an outcome;
  - a visual system for raw material;
  - whether My World earns a root;
  - how a borrowed lens appears "without creating social pressure";
  - which group memories are reactivated and who retires them;
  - the minimal non-travel occasion;
  - the smallest relational-image exchange that beats a normal message;
  - who initiates scope expansion;
  - which public interactions give residents enough reciprocity to avoid becoming "unpaid destination content creators";
  - the identity, moderation, expiry, contestation, and contact controls required before anything goes public;
  - which contributions stay civic and which are paid.
- The stranger → group progression is gated behind demonstrated reciprocal interest and explicit invitation (§8.10).
- Promotion into the World Foundry corpus is gated on "authority, provenance, scope, and independent verification" (§7).
- Non-goals (§14.3):
  - a photo manager or AI scrapbook;
  - photographing everything;
  - a profound narrative for every capture;
  - public posting or ratings as the participation gate;
  - passive visit detection;
  - an averaged group profile;
  - inferring another person's meaning;
  - synthetic "local" narration;
  - preference repetition;
  - forcing curiosity into Plans.

### E. Evidence and tests

- There is no formal research. Evidence is the founder's lived behavior (French menus) plus product analogies. Snapchat, BeReal, Beli, and Granola "validate demand for casual, imperfect capture" but "do not by themselves provide Vesper's consequence loop" (§2.5).
- The attention hierarchy is explicitly "a product hypothesis, not a universal scoring formula."
- There are 12 core hypotheses (§12.1). H5 is multiplayer perception beyond planning. H8 is sending to a particular person. H9 is relational recall that is not invasive. H10–H12 cover the public commons, plural local perspective, and reciprocity.
- There are 8 qualitative post-occasion questions (§12.2) and about 14 behavioral measures (§12.3). The measures include recipient-adds-perspective rate, relational recalls judged invasive, voluntary scope expansion to public, and local-contributor reports of control and reciprocity.
- The constraint on all measures: they "must not incentivize notification volume, indiscriminate capture, or forced memory creation."

### F. Boundaries

- Not ambient surveillance. Passive context may disambiguate but not replace participation (§2.4).
- No mandatory daily capture ritual and no social camera feed (§2.5).
- Tickets: redact barcodes and IDs; a ticket is never group-visible by default (§6.2).
- Never reveal who supplied a private reason (§7, §8.4).
- A send is never converted into a hidden social score (§8.9).
- What the public realm is not (§8.10):
  - a global feed;
  - live-location broadcast;
  - an unmoderated "ask locals" inbox;
  - influencer ranking or contribution gamification;
  - one synthetic local voice;
  - permanent facts without observation times;
  - a requirement that residents serve tourists;
  - a channel for strangers to receive inferred personal context;
  - reviews relabeled as interpretation.
- Publishing one projection must not expose the private reason, a companion's response, group constraints, precise live location, or relational inferences.
- Changing visibility never broadens the authority of prior inferences (§10.2).
- "Keep quietly" must not disguise indefinite retention (§9.1).

### G. Not covered by D1–D10

1. **Public place commons.** Strangers, residents, workers, historians, institutions, businesses, and recent visitors contribute plural attributed perspectives. None of D1–D10 leaves the friend graph.
2. **Places and locals as social actors with their own reciprocal stakes.** Examples: correcting misconceptions, preserving a practice, seeing what became of a lens. This includes paid hosted access.
3. **Place handoffs / hospitality left for a future visitor.** An asynchronous gift to an unknown person.
4. **The stranger → governed-group formation ladder,** covering interest-based walks, workshops, and events.
5. **Distributed attention on one co-present occasion.** Several people notice different layers and produce a group-safe synthesis. D3 borrows one friend's view; this is collective perception by the group.
6. **Divergence without consensus.** One occurrence yields many private meanings, and disagreement is preserved.
7. **Group character / relationship portrait** over many occasions. D10 improves "next time" but does not reflect a group's identity back for recognition.
8. **Shared open loops,** such as promised reveals or a tradition expected to change in another region. These are multi-occasion, multi-place arcs.
9. **Relational proactivity that says separate, reconvene, or stay silent.** It also protects a private need behind a group-safe alternative.
10. **The AI routing a curiosity to the right human.** The human can be an absent friend, parent, host, or local expert.
11. **Asynchronous seeing-together across distance and time, with outcome return.** The example is "I finally encountered what you taught me about." D1 is ambient sharing and lacks this consequence loop.
12. **Family, parents, and hosts as relationship types,** not only friends.
13. **Private observation → public world freshness.** This is a civic contribution.
14. **One Encounter with four separately governed projections, plus the audience gradient.** Scope is part of the social design.
15. **The travel ↔ home bridge.** A distant curiosity finds a nearby continuation.

## DOCUMENT 2. Multiplayer under the lived-experiences thesis (2026-08-07, superseded 08-16)

`/Users/feihuyan/travel-workspace/travel-agent/docs/working/multiplayer-strategy-2026-08-07.md`

### A. Social values / human payoffs

- Relief from the planner-friend role: "Every competitor sells speed of decision. Not one sells relief from the role" (§4.4).
- "Unequal labor is eliminated, not reduced… 'Nobody had to organize' is rung 1" (§5.1).
- Ambient coincidence becomes a shared afternoon: "a version of tonight where you and Amy both end up somewhere good" (§5.1, §7).
- Each person keeps private context while the group shares one coherent plan that "remain[s] true as the world changes" (§2.1).
- Private caucus: a person says what they will not say in the room, and the resolution exposes neither the source nor the rationale (§6).
- Group trips actually happen. The baseline is that 76% fail, and survivors average 83 messages, 19 hours, and 11 days (§4.3).
- Thin participants attend without adopting anything and with zero install (§5.2).
- Participant exit artifact: every attendee leaves with a useful memory even if they never organize (§6).
- Multiplayer rescue: the plan reshapes instead of collapsing, and each person gets an explanation at the right disclosure level (§6).
- Travel overlap delight, e.g. "Mira lands in Lisbon Thursday" (§6).
- Near-miss: "You and Dev have both been to Ler Devagar three times, never together" (§6).
- Relationship-triggered push: a place reopens, a ritual becomes timely, or a remembered promise becomes actionable (§6).
- Hosting a visitor; a birthday for eight; a recurring group where the memory advantage is visible to users (§6).
- Shared and personal outcomes "without pretending every participant had the same experience" (§2.1).
- Pair: remembered cognitive and operational complementarity, "without fixing either person into a role" (§6).
- Grounded truth as a social good: no hallucinated venues or flights (§4.5).
- Silence, a calm pass, or a protected plan as the best move (§7.6, §7.8).
- N−1 repair: each invited member has their own solo loop as a reason to stay (§5.1).

### B. Named concepts

**The three modes (§3), derived precisely.**
- Multiplayer decomposes by *who initiated*. It is "a ladder, not a menu," and "an initiation ladder, not a complete model of multiplayer."
- The YC-style shared-session axis (who can observe, redirect, approve, or inherit) is orthogonal and applies to every rung.
- **Rung 1, ambient co-presence:** *nobody organized*. The world produced a coincidence and Vesper noticed. The output is a **Move**, which expires and creates no durable object.
- **Rung 2, proposed convergence:** *Vesper organized*. It gives one composed answer with a meeting point that is not a mathematical midpoint. The output is a Plan if accepted.
- **Rung 3, assisted organizing:** *a person organized*. The output is a Plan or a Trip.

**Why the ambient rung is the defensible cell.**
- "Frequency and defensibility run in opposite directions along the ladder" (§3). The market crowds rung 3 because a person declares intent first.
- The "presence cell is empty, and durably so" (§4.2). Life360, Snap Map, and Find My all "stop at presence." Their monetization (family safety, ads) gives them no reason to build judgment, and planning startups have no presence data.
- "Filling this cell requires two data types nobody currently holds together: where people are, and what they would actually want" (§4.2).
- The underlying moat claim: "Governed private context across multiple people" is the only link in the chain that is difficult for Maps or Google to copy (§2).
- Ambient is also "the converter" (§5.1). The solo loop feeds multiplayer at solo frequency, 1–3 occasions a week.
- The doc hedges this: "opportunity and warning, not proof of defensibility." The 08-08 revision adds that ambient "can be the most defensible destination without being the first product step" (§9).

**Other concepts.**
- **Core claim (§2):** "Multiplayer is not a feature area. Governed multi-party context is the strongest structurally difficult-to-copy element."
- **Shared agency around one living object (§2.1):** the YC translation. "Vesper is a shared agent for real life."
- **Four layers (§2.1):** private inputs, shared object, shared agent work, shared and personal outcomes.
- **Strategic position (§5):** "The organizer is the customer. The group is the distribution. The companions are the asset. And the ambient rung is what makes all three compound."
- **Growth loop (§5.2):** seven steps. Steps 4–6 are "load-bearing, unproven."
- **Where to compete (§5.3):** local+solo for frequency; local+ambient as the empty cell; travel+group for stakes; local+group later.
- **Move vs Plan vs Trip;** **CompanionScope** and **PlaceScope;** the candidate scope triple **(person, place, companion)**, where "Damas" and "Damas, with Amy" are different objects (§11.2).
- **Cross-rung mechanics (§6):** private caucus with public resolution; one shared agent job; relationship-triggered push without live location; double-opt-in ambient opening; multiplayer rescue; participant exit artifact.
- **Eight design principles (§7):** surface the opportunity, not the position; resolve, don't poll; one rich owner, many thin participants; reason with identity, compose without it; silence is first-class; personalize activation, not only fit; real consequence only.
- **Ambient-as-a-feed vs ambient-as-a-person-trigger (§10).** The tell that the killed feed has been rebuilt: "it renders as a scrollable list instead of a single Move."
- **Social units in substrate:** circles of kind `pair / group / household / other`; relationship visibility grants; `AMBIENT_COINCIDENCE`.

### C. Big / ambitious ideas

- "Vesper is a shared agent for real life" (§2.1). The AI is a group participant whose work can be observed, redirected, and handed off.
- "Ambient co-presence is the converter" (§5.1). The solo life continuously produces social openings.
- "Nobody had to organize" (§5.1). This removes the organizer role entirely.
- The near-miss, "Memory, not presence. Uniquely ours" (§6). A shared place history reveals latent connection.
- "The segmentation is the evidence for our thesis" (§4.1). One asset spans a Lisbon trip, a Tuesday return, and a Friday with friends.
- The moat "sits on an open research problem" (§2): multi-principal memory with access control and forgetting.
- Household is a modelled circle kind (§1).
- "That group becomes uncopyable" once two or more members hold state (§5.2, step 7).

### D. Deferred / gated / rejected / blocked

**What "blocked" is blocked on.**
- The `why_new` line says the doc covers "what it blocks on."
- §1: the blocker "is not merely a consent table; it is governed relationship meaning, relevance, delivery, and a trusted end-to-end product loop."
- §9: "The next blocker is relationship meaning, not another entity." The doc must prove how asymmetric follows, trip-derived co-travelers, explicit circles, visibility grants, and companion claims compose "without silently converting one signal into friendship, intent, or sharing authority."
- §11.2: "Scope shape — the question the ledger blocks on." The question is whether the unit is the (person, place, companion) triple, which is "cheap now, unretrofittable later."
- §9.1: there is 0/3 physical-device evidence for the multiplayer journeys, and "no multiplayer loop is certified."

**Other items.**
- Ambient is sequenced last (§9). Shipping it first "combines five unproved variables — density, location access, relationship meaning, attention timing, and creepiness."
  - The order is: (1) human-initiated group travel; (2) active-plan proactivity; (3) relationship-memory reactivation without live location; (4) AI-proposed convergence with private opt-in; (5) ambient co-presence.
- Attention governance is "a gate, not a later capability."
- Rejected: multiplayer as the product (competes with Partiful, or with the coordination graveyard); multiplayer as a feature (yields no companion data and no distribution) (§5).
- Do not build (§8):
  - voting, polls, availability grids;
  - a friend-activity feed, because Snap and Instagram own it;
  - simultaneous shared editing;
  - a "discover things to do with friends" browse surface, because it is demand invention.
  - The header later relaxed the polling rejection: "a bounded vote on one pre-synthesized decision is an allowed commitment mechanism."
- "No group chat in-app" was **corrected on 08-10 as wrong.** The real boundary is no messaging table stakes and no chat-volume metric; the native trip room is canon.
- "Friend graph only. No discovery. No strangers. Ever" (§7.2). The stated reason is Down to Lunch, which was killed by a trust-and-safety rumour campaign. **This directly conflicts with Doc 1 §8.10's public realm, written 11 days later.**
- Local+group rung 3 is "later," because it is the most contested cell.
- Hosting a visitor: "Hypothesis, not a wedge," with no demand evidence either way.
- The pair is structurally modelled but product-unproved.
- N−1 repair is "the #1 unvalidated bet."
- There are nine open decisions (§11), including thin participation without an account, presence-consent comprehension, shared-agent authority, and per-participant outcome truth.

### E. Evidence

- Claims are labelled [verified], [market], or [proposed]. [market] means as-reported and not reproduced.
- Market evidence:
  - six AI occasion-planners, with pricing and user counts;
  - Life360 at about 91M users, Snap Map at 400M+, and about 70% of Gen Z using Find My;
  - 76% of proposed group trips never happen;
  - Partiful: 2M new users in 2025, with 60%+ of actives opening weekly;
  - TeamOut hallucinations;
  - GateMem: no method achieves utility, access control, and forgetting together; "filtering leaks and hard partition holds";
  - the Airbnb 2026 Summer Release, Google Ask Maps, and Alfi;
  - the YC RFS;
  - the PiSAs and ProACT papers.
- Caveat the doc holds: the "negotiation is the problem" finding is size-dependent, and swiping works for pairs.
- Appetite is a natural experiment "with someone else's money."
- Eight falsifiers (§12):
  - participants never start their own occasion;
  - coincidences are dismissed or read as creepy;
  - Appetite breaks out;
  - group-local frequency fails to materialize;
  - participants do not value observing the agent job;
  - relationship pushes fail;
  - opportunity framing raises taps but lowers trust;
  - pair role inference is unrecognized or entrenches unequal labor.

### F. Boundaries

- Surface the opportunity, not the position.
- Double opt-in before revealing a convergence; one decline produces silence.
- Hard partition, not retrieval filtering, for group composition.
- Never borrow scarcity, diagnose FOMO, or treat engagement as the outcome.
- No signal (follow, co-travel, circle, grant) silently implies another, or implies friendship.
- A shared screen without shared control is "collaboration theater," and unconstrained shared control is unsafe.
- Creepiness is "a one-shot failure."

### G. Not covered by D1–D10

1. **Rung 1 ambient coincidence where nobody initiates.** D5 and D7 are user-initiated.
2. **The near-miss,** which is latent shared place history never experienced together.
3. **Travel overlap** across stored trips.
4. **Relief from the organizer role, and invisible labor as the core pain,** including fairness over time.
5. **Private caucus with public resolution.** Unsayable constraints are resolved without attribution. D6 and D5 have no privacy-asymmetry mechanic.
6. **The shared agent job,** where the AI is a group participant and the group can observe, redirect, approve, pause, and hand off.
7. **Groups of 3–8, birthday-for-eight constraint density, and hosting a visitor.** D1–D10 are mostly pairs.
8. **Thin, zero-install participation and the participant exit artifact.** This is value for non-users.
9. **Multiplayer rescue with per-person disclosure levels.** D8 is the nearest, but it is help between friends, not a plan reshaping itself.
10. **Group travel as the high-stakes multiplayer wedge.**
11. **Relationship-memory push without location,** e.g. a ritual becoming timely or a remembered promise.
12. **Per-participant outcome truth,** where each person's experience of the same occasion is recorded separately.
13. **Household as a unit.**

## DOCUMENT 3. Multiplayer activation and social psychology (2026-08-09)

`/Users/feihuyan/travel-workspace/travel-agent/docs/working/multiplayer-activation-and-social-psychology-2026-08-09.md`

### A. Social values / human payoffs

- Latent interest becomes "a small number of grounded, well-timed, socially viable real-world moves" (§1).
- The system decides whether something is better alone, with one particular person, or with a group (§1).
- Curiosity and richer seeing: the same place yields different experiences through different legitimate lenses, as in "notice this detail on your walk" (§5).
- Anticipation: a future place becomes emotionally concrete before it is scheduled. A partner's save becomes "your kind of first morning in Nice" (§5).
- Honest scarcity and avoided regret (§5).
- Serendipity as a "retrospectively coherent intersection of person, place, situation, and timing" (§5).
- Competence and confidence: the move is easy, appropriate, and unlikely to go wrong (§5).
- Identity and self-expansion: try on being a "curious resident, thoughtful host, design obsessive, regular, good partner" (§5).
- Belonging and reciprocity through specific provenance: "Maya saved this for the two of you" (§5).
- Momentum: a reversible hold, one route, or a private double opt-in (§5).
- JOMO and protected attention: "keep the plan you have." A calm pass is success (§5).
- A solo experience that is complete, where social possibility is "optional enrichment, not a tax" (§6).
- Pair: relief from repeated micro-coordination with two independent principals preserved (§6).
- Family and household: representation for members who have no accounts or receive no prompts, with asymmetric authority, care, and accessibility handled (§6).
- Recurring circle: rituals and fairer participation over time. The system notices when one person always organizes, when someone's vetoes disappear, or when the same minority keeps absorbing compromise, and it adapts the process without labeling people (§6).
- The veto holder can stop a choice without explaining publicly. The quiet participant "benefits without performing engagement" (§7).
- Vesper "carr[ies] unclaimed labor" and avoids "converting activity level into legitimacy" (§7).
- Organizer burden falls "without lowering the least-satisfied participant's perceived agency" (§12C).

### B. Named concepts

- **Activation intelligence vs recommendation quality (§1).**
- **Preference vs activation posture (§2).** Posture is contextual, uncertain, and revisable, and is never a persona. It has nine dimensions:
  - planning horizon (spontaneous ↔ anticipatory);
  - certainty (explore ↔ commit);
  - choice breadth (one answer ↔ browse);
  - novelty (familiar ritual ↔ discovery);
  - social exposure (private ↔ shared);
  - companion dependence (optional ↔ essential);
  - intervention tolerance (quiet ↔ directive);
  - support need (narrative ↔ operational);
  - decision mode (self-led ↔ delegated).
- **Four axes (§3):**
  - engagement state (driving, contributing, passive, surging);
  - decision role;
  - activation posture;
  - authority.
  - The illustration: "A quiet partner may hold the only veto."
- **Possibility lifecycle (§4).** Noticed → Understood → Relevant → Feasible → Socially viable → Experienced → Remembered. Most possibilities should never traverse the whole lifecycle.
- **Nine psychological levers (§5),** listed in A.
- **Five social units (§6):** Solo, Pair, Family/household, Recurring circle, Ephemeral party. The ephemeral party expires its inferences quickly, and "A Trip roster is… not durable friendship."
- **Nine roles, supported and not assigned (§7):** Dreamer, Scout, Organizer, Validator, Veto holder, Quiet participant, Local expert, Connector, Memory keeper.
- **Opportunity taxonomy (§8).** An opportunity is "an evidence-backed claim that a possibility became unusually actionable." All ten entries:

| # | Class | What changed | Example | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Deadline | a real window closes | final exhibition weekend | source, deadline, confidence |
| 2 | Conditions | the world improved | saved garden fits today's weather | weather, hours, route |
| 3 | Proximity | action cost fell | saved café nearby and open | fresh foreground location, precise geometry, fresh open state |
| 4 | Convergence | people or plans align | both partners free near the same area | scoped calendars/Plans, consent, safe disclosure |
| 5 | Feasibility | uncertainty resolved | hard-to-plan restaurant now has a table | live availability and action path |
| 6 | Emergence | something new appeared | relevant event announced | identity, freshness, provenance |
| 7 | Change | a known place changed | reopening, new chef, access change | before/after claim and source |
| 8 | Return | prior meaning became timely | anniversary, seasonal ritual | outcome memory and current fit |
| 9 | Narrative | a new lens changes the experience | familiar block through migration history | attributed research and local relevance |
| 10 | Rescue | a live Plan became worse | rain closes the original route | Plan truth, changed fact, coherent revision |

- **Five questions every opportunity must answer.** Why these people, why this place, why now, the smallest next move, and why this surface and interruption level. If any answer is missing, stay silent.
- **Framing policy (§9).** Good and disallowed framing, with an avoid/prefer table.
- **Evaluation hierarchy (§13).** Safety and contextual integrity > experienced value > agency and trust > organizer relief and social coherence > groundedness > attention cost > engagement.
- **Product test sentence (§15).**

### C. Big / ambitious ideas

- Identity: "Places let people try on a way of being" (§5).
- Fairness over long arcs: notice when "the same minority repeatedly absorbs compromise — but… adapt the process, not label the people" (§6).
- Members without accounts: "Some participants may need representation without accounts or direct prompts" (§6).
- JOMO: "Vesper should sometimes improve the user's life by closing a possibility" (§5).
- "Local expert: contributes situated judgment with visible provenance" and "Connector: sees which people or histories make a possibility meaningful" (§7).
- Narrative opportunity: "familiar block through migration history" (§8). This is the place's meaning acting as the social or experiential trigger.
- "Provenance earns attention; judgment still earns action" (§5).
- "avoid converting activity level into legitimacy" (§7).

### D. Deferred / gated / open

- The whole doc is superseded provenance, not build authorization.
- Explicitly not authorized (§14):
  - an activation-profile schema;
  - FOMO or personality scoring;
  - ambient push to users;
  - automatic sharing of save rationale;
  - a social feed or friend activity dashboard;
  - mandatory save notes, polls, or questionnaires;
  - autonomous inference of relationship type from behavior.
- Do not create `activation_persona`, `fomo_score`, a fixed social type, or an opaque shared-intent field (§10).
- Study gates:
  - If Study D fails, "Ambient and relationship-triggered push remain dark."
  - If Study A fails, save origin "remains provenance only."
  - If Study B fails, urgency is removed "even if CTR improves."
  - If Study C fails, the agent is narrowed to logistics or private assistance.
- Still open (§14):
  - which activation observations persist, and for how long;
  - the smallest correction surface;
  - when pair patterns may transfer to a new context;
  - which role or authority changes need durable events;
  - whether solo value suffices for socially private users.
- Missing product evidence (§11): seven items. They include whether a pair recognizes its decision pattern, and whether quiet users benefit without pressure.

### E. Evidence

- The psychology evidence lives in a separate research memo, `docs/research/multiplayer-activation-psychology-2026-08-09.md`. This doc itself states no evidence strength. The levers are for "using carefully," and the posture dimensions are "hypotheses."
- The substrate claims were verified against the repo on 08-09. They are described as "implementation substrate, not a coherent consumer loop."
- Four field studies with pass gates:
  - **Study A, save-to-action diary.** 18–24 people over two weeks. Pass: at least two repeatable activation patterns; rationale "mostly right" in ≥70% of probes and creepy or wrong in under 10%.
  - **Study B, framing and pressure trial.** 30–40 testers, six framing conditions, real opportunities only. Pass: better experienced outcomes with no trust or pressure penalty.
  - **Study C, pair and group role simulation.** 8 pairs, 4 recurring groups, 4 ephemeral parties, and at least 2 household contexts. It simulates a late constraint, a closure, an attractive alternative, and a private veto. Pass: organizer burden falls without lowering the least-satisfied participant's agency or leaking private rationale.
  - **Study D, consent and contextual-integrity comprehension.** 15–20 people, including ex-companions. Pass: ≥90% predict location, constraint, and rationale flows correctly, and ≥80% predict retention and inference correctly.

### F. Boundaries

- Disallowed framing (§9):
  - borrowed scarcity, synthetic countdowns, popularity pressure;
  - "everyone is going";
  - diagnosing FOMO, loneliness, fatigue, relationship dynamics, or identity;
  - exposing overlap before consent;
  - treating a save, silence, membership, or a prior yes as current consent;
  - presenting a decision as unanimous when it was not;
  - obscuring sponsorship or the sharer.
- "We know how you two decide" is valuable, while "we have merged your preferences" is dangerous.
- Never import couple assumptions into family or household units.
- Roles are supported and never assigned.
- No friend-popularity ranking.
- Winning engagement while losing safety, experienced value, or agency and trust is "a product failure."

### G. Not covered by D1–D10

1. **The ten-class opportunity taxonomy as social triggers.** Deadline, Conditions, Emergence, Change, Return, Narrative, and Rescue, all applied to "you or the people you care about." D1–D10 are driven by relationships, not by changes in the world.
2. **Identity and self-expansion:** becoming a thoughtful host, a regular, or a good partner.
3. **Anticipation as a shared payoff,** before anything is planned.
4. **JOMO, protected rituals, and the calm pass.** Social value from *not* doing something.
5. **Family or household with asymmetric authority, care, and accessibility,** including account-less members such as children and elders.
6. **Recurring-circle fairness over time:** who always organizes, whose vetoes vanish, who keeps compromising.
7. **The role ecology** of veto holder, quiet participant, connector, memory keeper, validator, and dreamer, and the separation of engagement from authority.
8. **Solo completeness** as a legitimate endpoint for socially private users.
9. **The ephemeral party:** temporary groups of non-friends on a trip, with fast-expiring inferences.
10. **Provenance as the unit of belonging,** e.g. "Maya saved this for the two of you." This is a gift-like save directed at a relationship. It is near D6, but it is the partner's own act and not something the system finds.
11. **Competence and confidence** as the reason people fail to go out together, which is different from needing more ideas.
12. **Narrative and lens opportunities:** a familiar place re-seen together through a history.
