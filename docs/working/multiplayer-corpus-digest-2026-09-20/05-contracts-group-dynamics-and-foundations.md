## DOC 1 — Interaction Design & Social Dynamics (`travel-agent/docs/product/`)

This doc is travel-era and owns "interaction patterns and social reasoning." Authority, units, and memory belong to Multiplayer Product Strategy and the Contribution Contract.

### A. Social values and capabilities

1. **Good trip, not perfect trip (§1).**
   - The agent carries the burden of quality while working from "incomplete, messy, non-uniform input."
   - The bar is better than "WhatsApp, Google Sheets, the one friend who does all the planning."
   - Human problem: unequal planning labor, and groups that cannot supply uniform data.

2. **Organizer-as-collaborator (§2).**
   - The organizer wants "a thought partner who knows things they don't," not a handoff.
   - The organizer brings group knowledge (who the people are, what failed last year). The agent brings destination judgment and has taste.
   - "The preference collection and the planning are the same activity." The organizer's talk yields group context without surveys ("Priya is the foodie").
   - The channel can be voice note, call, text, or Instagram link, and the agent stitches them together.
   - Human problem: the planner's loneliness and burden, and the group's tacit knowledge being locked in one head.

3. **Non-uniform engagement and the four states (§3).** The states are fluid, not personas.
   - **Driving**: active co-planner. The driver can change.
   - **Contributing**: reacts to concrete options and "validates or redirects, not generates."
   - **Currently quiet** (legacy label "passive"): the reason is unknown. "Silence establishes neither disinterest, satisfaction, agreement, nor a durable preference."
   - **Surging**: suddenly highly engaged. The agent "absorbs this energy without disrupting what's already been planned."
   - Human problem: people want different involvement levels, and the product serves all "without requiring any of them to change."

4. **Fluidity principle (§3).**
   - "Current collaborator" is not fixed to the trip's creator, and the agent recognizes practical handoffs.
   - "That fluidity does not silently transfer authority."
   - If nobody is driving, the agent prepares more, but "its authority does not increase merely because humans are quiet."

5. **Four axes that must not collapse (§3).**
   - Engagement state.
   - Decision role: who "notices, scouts, organizes, validates, decides, vetoes, pays, or handles logistics."
   - Activation posture: needs exploration, confidence, social permission, one executable move, a quieter surface, or no intervention.
   - Authority.
   - "A passive person can still hold the only veto."

6. **Interaction by state (§4).**
   - Drivers get the full concierge conversation.
   - Contributors get "Show, don't ask" with three reactions: "Love this / Sure, why not / Not for me." The middle option captures flexibility, and no response is unknown rather than a fourth preference.
   - Quiet members get graceful defaults. Unknown needs stay unknown, and a provisional option is never "represented as checked for everyone." The agent asks one bounded question only if a missing constraint matters.
   - Surgers are absorbed and integrated: "The late surge is not treated as less legitimate than early input."

7. **SMS-first first contact (§4a, flagged as an OPEN QUESTION).**
   - The first message is grounded in the actual trip, requires no app, and offers progressive depth via deep links.
   - The app download "is pull, not push," with cross-channel continuity.
   - The doc itself flags the tension: outbound SMS to a non-consenting invitee conflicts with "friends join because the trip already matters."

8. **Lead-up window and admin spectrum (§5).**
   - Cadence is driven by "what does the agent have that's interesting enough to share," not by data needs.
   - Four admin styles are read from behavior:
     - *Highly decisive*: the agent sends teasers and hype.
     - *Collaborative*: the agent facilitates reactions.
     - *Vibes-only*: the agent drives from its own taste.
     - *Absent*: the agent bootstraps three trip shapes.
   - **Plan-sharing spectrum**: *Curator mode* (private craft, deliberate reveal), *Wiki mode* (group watches it evolve), *Selective mode* (structure private, specific doors opened).

9. **Agent as social state machine (§6).**
   - "It is managing a living social system."
   - It tracks four things:
     - per-person engagement with recency weighting;
     - the group engagement trend (rising means "the trip is getting real");
     - state changes, including the late joiner;
     - **unresolved tension** between members.
   - Quantitative loop: reply tracking, **personal baselines over universal benchmarks**, differential engagement by message type, and read receipts as a secondary signal. Docs 5 and 7 now restrict these signals to L0–L2 situation/product use.

10. **Photos as explicit encounter participation (§6).**
    - A deliberate in-Vesper photo may produce a private Encounter draft. The person can "keep, correct, share, or discard."
    - No camera monitoring. "A photo is not proof of meaning."

11. **Confidence-graduated behavior (§6).**
    - Low confidence: the agent "shares, it doesn't ask."
    - Medium confidence: personalized formats and topics.
    - High confidence: direct, and the agent may say "trust me."
    - Confidence is per person and per dimension.

12. **Scenario handling (§6).**
    - The organizer defers a dimension to a member (delegated judgment without delegated booking).
    - Dead trip: a draft itinerary makes the trip tangible, followed by one private nudge to the organizer.
    - Absent organizer: "primary planner is earned by engagement."
    - Everyone opinionated: the agent "structures the conflict rather than trying to eliminate it," offering two pre-validated options, tradeoffs, its own recommendation, a deadline, and the Plan's declared decision rule.
    - Group split 3/3: sub-group split, a third option, or the driver's call.

13. **Sub-group splits (§7).**
    - "The most powerful tool… for resolving genuinely irreconcilable preferences."
    - Right for a free block with divergent wishes. Risky if the same person is always in the minority, if it feels like "parallel solo travel," or if the trip is for togetherness.
    - Rules: at most one split per day; together is the default; frame splits "as abundance, not division."
    - The agent tracks split frequency and composition as a fairness signal. Reconvening logistics are "the hidden complexity." Phase 2.

14. **The diplomatic layer (§8).**
    - "Optimal plan, communicated in a way that… doesn't make anyone feel overruled."
    - **Attribution principle**: attribute to quality, location, or appeal, never to constraint satisfaction.
    - "Why this restaurant?" gets a hierarchy of truthful-but-incomplete explanations (quality, context, taste).
    - **Disagreement**: neither capitulate nor dig in. Acknowledge, then offer pre-validated alternatives so that "the dissenting person feels heard. The private constraints remain private."
    - **Decision forcing**: healthy urgency, calibrated per group.

15. **Trust and autonomy escalation (§9).**
    - Five rungs: suggest, suggest and hold, book low-stakes, book most, full autonomy with caps.
    - **The group's trust is "governed by its least-trusting member."**
    - Trust recovery: acknowledge, drop one notch, learn.
    - Proactive escalation is framed as convenience.
    - Urgency is not authority. Under time pressure the organizer gets the escalation "rather than the agent assuming that authority itself."

16. **Private side quests (§10).**
    - A personalized tip for one member while the group is occupied: "a whispered tip from a knowledgeable friend."
    - "The moment when the product shifts from 'useful tool' to 'something that actually knows me.'"
    - It proves that private inputs create moments *for them*, not only smooth group dynamics. Premium tier.

17. **Real-time energy adaptation (§11).**
    - The agent detects divergence between group energy and the plan and proposes changes as positives.
    - "Nobody wants to be told they are flagging. They want the plan to quietly become what they need."
    - The agent proposes and never unilaterally cancels.

18. **During-trip adaptation (§12).**
    - A momentary driver appears ("we finished lunch early — what should we do?").
    - Change proposals are tiered by stakes.
    - Structured data says a booking *can* be cancelled; social judgment says "three people were excited about this."
    - Update cadence: morning, midday, evening.

19. **Privacy architecture (§13).**
    - Always private: budget, energy, medical constraints, and **social-role preferences ("I'd rather someone else decide")**.
    - Shareable by default: dietary restrictions, interests, scheduling preferences.
    - Always shared: hard allergies and logistics-affecting accessibility needs.
    - **Pattern-detection defense**: "No single person's constraint should consistently be the visible binding constraint." The agent has a private conversation with the constraining person, giving "agency without exposing them."

### B. Named concepts
Good Trip not Perfect Trip; Organizer-as-Collaborator; Driving / Contributing / Currently quiet / Surging; Fluidity Principle; Four Axes; Decision role; Activation posture; Show-Don't-Ask; three-state reaction model; Admin Spectrum; Curator / Wiki / Selective modes; Social State Machine; personal baselines; Confidence-Graduated Behavior; Sub-Group Split; Diplomatic Layer; Attribution Principle; Decision Forcing; Escalation Sequence; Trust Recovery; Private Side Quest; Real-Time Energy Adaptation; Pattern Detection Defense.

### C. Permitted but under-used
- Decision roles as a first-class axis (scout, validator, vetoer, payer, logistics).
- Delegating judgment on one dimension to a named member.
- Curator-mode "deliberate reveal."
- Sub-group split with managed reconvening.
- Minority-frequency fairness tracking.
- Private negotiation with the constraining person.
- Least-trusting-member rule.
- Organizer time-pressure escalation.
- Side quests.
- Hype and teaser messages for decisive admins.

### D. Hard boundaries
- Engagement "never silently grants disclosure, approval, spending, retention, inference, or action authority."
- Silence is not consent, and the agent does not nudge repeatedly.
- The agent never reveals whose constraint shaped a choice.
- No camera monitoring.
- Pace, linger time, and skips do not become person claims.
- No "diagnosing a person's mood or hardening a repeated afternoon slowdown into a group identity."
- The agent never unilaterally cancels.
- Closing Note: "Social intelligence belongs in the reasoning layer; social rights do not."

### F. Group-scale and long-arc ideas
- 6–8-person opinionated groups.
- Couples trips versus friends trips (different split tolerance).
- 6–8-week lead-ups.
- High-confidence relationships built over "multiple trips of data."
- Fatigue patterns "learned from previous trips" (now constrained by docs 5 and 7).
- A new person joining late.

### G. Not in the ten directions
- The organizer's burden and the organizer as a user type.
- Engagement inequality and fair treatment of quiet people.
- Surges.
- Conflict structuring.
- Splits and reconvening.
- Diplomacy and face-saving attribution.
- Hidden constraints (budget, medical) shaping group outcomes.
- The per-group trust ladder.
- Side quests *within* a group.
- Energy-aware replanning.
- The reveal moment.
- Decision roles.
- Anticipation and hype in the lead-up.

## DOC 2 — Group / Social System Charter (`docs/systems/group-social.md`)

### A. Values and capabilities

- **Relational personalization (Purpose).** "How this person travels with these people, in this destination, for this occasion." Recurring-group memory is "earned only after repeated-trip evidence" and "never overrides what members say on the current trip."
- **Trip as shared social object (Coordination-channel ruling).**
  - The group's own thread stays the talk channel.
  - Artifacts "must preview beautifully and land deep."
  - The in-app room is "the trip's room," judged "against Partiful's bar, not iMessage's."
  - The metric is links pasted out and taps back in, not chat volume.
- **Social votes (Group-decision ruling 1).** Names go on choices and holdouts are visible. "Visible preference among friends is ordinary social life" and creates "momentum and peer accountability." Sensitive objections go to the private channel. "Constraint privacy ≠ preference privacy."
- **Attribution follows the visibility of the intent (ruling 2).** A public ask earns human credit. A private ask stays "Vesper proposed," which acts as the **diplomacy shield**. Unknown provenance fails private.
- **Visible care (ruling 3).** "Show the work, never the accommodation." The work shown is diligence such as hours checked and walking time. "Works for everyone" is banned because the asymmetry "implies someone needed accommodating."
- **Voting on by default, automation opt-in (ruling 4).** "Membership does not imply a governance preference."
- **Chat-first decision ownership.** One card is one decision, and the settled card becomes "the visible receipt."
- **Room agency.** Organizers set a reactive or proactive posture. Every participant may self-mute. Whole-room mute is an auditable emergency brake.
- **Trip shared memory.** A group-safe document readable by members, with organizer-authorized edits and forgetting, versioned append-only.
- **Social units.** Explicit circle and pair membership plus relationship-memory scopes are "durable substrate," "not inferred friendship."
- **Fairness signals.** Vote-absence friction and quiet-member detection.

### B. Named concepts
Relational personalization; shared-object path; the trip's room; social votes; diplomacy shield; visible care; consensus automation; one decision object; room agency; trip shared memory; social units (circle, pair); quiet propagation; membership dignity; room-mute honesty; wiki-mode V1; and "Memory remains plural," which names five distinct objects (internal group model, editable shared memory, personal outcome, live social state, recurring-group hypothesis).

### C. Permitted but under-used
- Explicit circle and pair units with relationship-memory scopes.
- Recurring-group hypothesis (beta).
- Editable and forgettable shared memory.
- Share-out artifacts that land deep in external threads.
- Human credit for publicly asked proposals.
- Group-visible constraint-neutral diligence.
- Room-level posture governance.
- Curator and selective draft propagation, which is *deferred* and so both a gap and an opportunity.

### D. Hard boundaries
- Non-members never read trip data, and invites never expose members' private data.
- The group model must not be "a flat average" or "a bland consensus no one actually wants."
- "Attendance, silence, or circle membership never auto-confirms shared memory."
- Membership dignity: joining gets a group line; leaving gets a quiet line; removal and role change get no announcement.
- A personal mute is never exposed.
- No messaging table stakes until organic banter is observed.
- Human delivery is independent of AI participation.

### F. Group-scale and long-arc ideas
Recurring groups across repeated trips; circles and pairs as durable units; "companion-dependent behavior."

### G. Not in the ten directions
- Group decision mechanics (votes, holdouts, receipts).
- Shared editable group memory.
- Governance of the AI's presence in a room.
- Membership lifecycle dignity.
- The share-out artifact as a growth and social object.
- Recurring-group intelligence.

## DOC 3 — Life Social-Lifecycle Fixture Matrix

### A. Values and capabilities

- **Shared episode without shared ownership of consequences (Executive decision).** The structure is "Governed shared core + attributed contribution lanes + private or relationship-scoped Outcomes + viewer-relative audience and safety evaluation + independent return policy."
- **Four planes (Executive decision)**: record authority, audience authority, relationship safety, and return policy.
- **Juxtaposition (L05).** Two people's separate experiences (Rome heat, Paris heat) are composed into a "bounded comparison, not consensus." Each person keeps a private Outcome of what the comparison means.
- **Personal return of a shared event (`CMP-L02-F`).** A private composition using the shared core plus the viewer's private state.
- **Search by contributor (S01).** "What did Maya contribute?" returns Maya's lane, not a group statement.
- **Commitment versus occurrence (S01).** "Maya planned to bring dessert" is distinct from "Maya brought dessert."
- **Transitions S01–S14 (§4 Transition register).** Decline; defer; leave (S04-A frozen historical core by default, S04-B access-terminating only if declared up front); withdraw; delete; narrow audience or Place precision; block; estrangement; unblock; reconciliation; account deletion; verified death; managed export versus screenshot.
- **Person-controlled softening (§5, S08–S11).** "Less like this," "Not now," and a person or episode shield are available without blocking.

### B. Named concepts
Shared core; attributed contribution lane; plural Outcomes; membership and relationship **epoch**; contribution-use grant with its full field list (§3); relationship-safety overlay; return-policy compiler and precedence (§7); managed export with manifest; Continue envelope; posthumous directive; social-periphery shield.

### C. Permitted but under-used
- Bounded cross-person *comparisons* (juxtaposition).
- Reduced Place precision (city rather than exact location).
- Frozen historical access for leavers.
- Ephemeral Occasions by declaration.
- Reconciliation producing a new epoch.
- Posthumous directives (explicit audience, custody, use).
- Managed exports that recompile.
- Person and episode shields.
- "Explicit retrieval after automatic suppression is a successful outcome."

### D. Hard boundaries
- Zero counts are required for: synthetic group consensus; private Outcome leakage; an old grant restored by unblock; lost attribution; "Retrospective push without prospective authority"; unverified death treated as death.
- No "welcome back" memory is generated on unblock.
- "Death never expands audience," and there is no synthetic presence.
- "Inactivity alone does not authorize a grief, breakup, recovery, or personality interpretation."
- No paraphrase leakage of withdrawn content.
- The product never promises screenshot revocation.
- Compact standard (§10): "Together should make shared life richer without making anyone's life less theirs."

### F. Group-scale and long-arc ideas
A three-person dinner; relationship arcs across years (estrangement, reconciliation, death); account end-of-life; legacy.

### G. Not in the ten directions
The whole *relationship lifecycle*: drifting, breakups, blocks, reconciliation, death and legacy. Also leaving a group gracefully, a shared archive with plural meanings, and comparison of parallel lives across cities.

- **Capabilities**:
  - The solo→group→solo→group room lifecycle.

## DOC 5 — Source, Claim, Scoped Learning, and Multiplayer Purpose

### A. Values and capabilities

- **Integrated product law (Executive finding).** "A contribution may power only the smallest named purpose supported by its Source, authorship, scope, and affected-person authority."
- **Effortless default (Executive finding).** Do low-risk work quietly, leave a receipt, and interrupt only when the flow "changes audience, affected-person standing, public consequence, spend…"
- **Five learning targets (§2.1).** World, product, current situation, person, and **relationship claim**. Examples of a relationship claim: "two people tend to enjoy wandering together or one person often carries coordination work."
- **Scoped-learning ladder (§2.3).** L0–L4: "a scope ladder, not a confidence ladder."
- **Differentiator (§2.6).** "Consequence-bearing continuity, not maximal recollection."
- **Purpose modes (§3.3).** Seven modes:
  - Private contribution.
  - Addressed contribution.
  - Occasion contribution.
  - **Ambient friend activity**, which may "open possibility through an explicitly shared status or artifact projection."
  - **Co-owned material**.
  - **Public Place contribution**.
  - Provider/action payload.
- **Three roles (§3.2).** Contributor, subject or affected principal, and custodian. "The contract cannot assume that they are" the same.
- **Relationship and follow boundaries (§3.5).**
  - Following is "directional ranking interest, not permission."
  - A co-presence relation exists only as a candidate under supported Occurrence.
  - "Group agreement may produce a Decision without producing a single group preference."
  - "People you know liked this" appears only from shareable authored Outcomes.
- **Scenario matrix (§5).** It includes:
  - Status: "back from Nice and Sorrento," with expiry.
  - A friend's Paris experience appearing in Home as a "juxtaposition or social opening."
  - A private dietary constraint shaping group options.
  - An addressed artifact sent to a friend with attribution and revoke.
  - A group photo with affected-person handling.

### B. Named concepts
Contribution Use Grant; `information_flow_grant` with fields `forbidden_purposes`, `transform` (for example `faces_visible_to_participants_only`), and `revocation: remove_future_projection_and_recompute`; interdependent privacy; contextual integrity; T0/T1/T2.

### C. Permitted but under-used
- Featured Status as an ambient social opener.
- Transforms (crop, blur, abstraction) that enable sharing of co-owned media.
- Relationship-scoped claims (allowed at L3 when authored).
- Public Place contribution as a separate civic act.
- Viewer-specific projections without copying graphs.
- Narrow **mandates** offered after repetition.

### D. Hard boundaries
- "Visibility, relevance, confidence, repetition, and successful outcomes do not widen that purpose."
- A vote does not become a global preference.
- A view or dwell does not establish interest.
- Following never reveals location or membership.
- "No all-purpose 'friends can see this' toggle."
- No permanent personality profile.
- No model-prompt-only privacy enforcement.
- Open question 4: co-owned artifact conflict is unresolved.

### F. Group-scale and long-arc ideas
Relationship claims over time ("often carries coordination work"); Occasion archives; cross-service adapters.

### G. Not in the ten directions
- Public or civic contribution to Places.
- Status broadcasting to an audience.
- Rights of people depicted in media.
- Recognition of invisible coordination labor.
- Learning about a *relationship* as its own subject.

## DOC 6 — Four-Root Loop, Object, and Surface Contract

### A. Values and capabilities

- **Multiplayer loop (§3.1).** "Attributed contribution → governed shared state → coordination → plural outcomes," returning a "better gathering and richer future context for each authorized participant."
- **§8 Multiplayer is inside the loop.**
  - Multiplayer is "cross-cutting, not a fifth root or engagement feed."
  - Combined context "improves an opening, Place view, or Occasion," and each participant gets "a viewer-relative Life projection."
  - "Another person's trace modifies the object or possibility it changes rather than creating generic activity."
- **Social contribution row (§5.1).**
  - Home: "appears when it changes current value."
  - Places: "when it changes Place understanding or possibility."
  - Life: preserved "with author, scope, precision, epoch, correction, and withdrawal."
- **Places "Human and horizon" face (§6.3).** "Attributed human perspective and a sparse typed path to another Place, person, mechanism, event, or future possibility."
  - **Human-route candidate** and **transfer candidate** are listed as derived assessments (§4.3).
- **Life People lens (§6.4).** "Attributed shared record… (the one lens whose subjects can object)."
  - "Together needs an authorized attributable human contribution."
  - The Vault holds "grants and withdrawal ledgers."
- **Engine scope (§7).** "Ordinary weekends, gatherings, reservations… errands, local plans, and social coordination—not only during infrequent live trips."
- **Chat verbs (§10).** "Ask, Point, Bring, Keep, Share, Invite, Decide, Act, and Correct."
  - Places can "share a bounded Place projection" and "contribute a situated Artifact or human note."
- **Carried-forward receipt (§10).** Proof that prior experience changed a present consequence.
- **Handoff envelope (§9).** Carries "viewer and audience scope," "usable contribution grant," and "companion/Occasion."

### C. Permitted but under-used
- Human notes on Places.
- Bounded Place projections as shareable objects.
- Person-to-Place horizon paths.
- People lens.
- Carried-forward receipts in social contexts.
- Occasion→Home material change.
- The "local multiplayer Occasion" and "social revocation" acceptance fixtures (§13).

### D. Hard boundaries
- "One participant's Outcome never becomes group sentiment."
- "Passive or inferred live friend location is prohibited without explicit, time-bounded sharing."
- No fifth root or feed.
- Home ranks "not engagement probability."
- Life returns are "fixed, not adaptive… never for engagement."
- "Surface location never grants authority."

### F. Group-scale and long-arc ideas
Journeys; Threads (pursuits that are open, resting, or closed); a years band in Time; Occasions in prospective, live, and historical states.

### G. Not in the ten directions
- People as a *lens over one's whole life*.
- Human perspective embedded in Places.
- Friend traces changing objects rather than producing a feed.
- The compounding loop in which each gathering improves the next, for each participant separately.

## DOC 7 — Contribution and Consequence Contract

### A. Social rules and what they enable

- **§10 Multiplayer rules.**
  - An authored payload "may inherit the audience of the channel"; private derivatives do not.
  - Attribution survives every projection.
  - "A friend's content can appear in another person's private mosaic only within its original audience and revocation scope."
  - Minimum-safe private constraints may shape a shared consequence, but "the public reason cannot reveal the constraint."
  - "Being named is not participation."
  - "Shared Occurrence may converge; personal and relational Outcomes remain separately authored."
- **§3.6 Purpose verbs.** Send/Address; Contribute; **Feature as Status** ("human-authored audience projection for a limited time," with a seven-day default in §3.3); **Publish to Place**.
- **§3.6.1 Exact-original Send.**
  - One original goes to one named recipient.
  - Display only: "Display does not authorize AI use, inference, onward distribution."
  - Opening requires "no Keep, reply or sender-visible read receipt."
  - This is adopted as an implementation contract, "not runtime activation or general Friends/guest/AI-use policy."
- **§3.7 Co-owned material.**
  - An affected person can narrow future projections without deleting the contributor's original.
  - "Least permissive applicable rule."
  - "Prefer omission, crop, blur, abstraction… to a negotiation workflow."
- **§3 (Five independent authority axes — owner-controlled collaboration).** Arrangement editing is owner-only by default, with scoped collaborative editing available. "A comment or suggestion is not an arrangement mutation."
- **§6 (Three visible treatments) — lightweight arrangements.** "An optional suggestion is not a pending decision, exploration is not adoption," and an accepted edit "is not everyone's acceptance or attendance."
- **§3.3 Expiry.** Location and availability last for the "current Moment or active Occasion only." A live Occasion gets a +72h reconciliation window.
- **§3.4 Learning target.** "Relationship — a scoped claim about particular people together."
- **§3.9 Connected services.** "Shared Places rather than complete location history, availability rather than all calendar content."
- **§9 Surface responsibilities.** The Occasion or Plan is not "a container that merges private meaning or silently enrolls people."

### B. Named concepts
Gestures; five axes; three-layer retention; T0/T1/T2; M0–M6 modes; ContributionEnvelope, which carries `context_scope.relationship`, `affected_principals`, and initiator kind `companion`; truth ladder ("noticed → … → publicly contributed"); narrow mandate; Featured Status; receipt and causal repair.

### C. Permitted but under-used
- Narrow mandates (standing delegated authority).
- Status.
- Publish to Place.
- The private mosaic of friends' content.
- Scoped collaborative editing grants.
- Relationship-scoped context and learning.
- Availability sharing.
- Invitations with four independent states (pending, accepted, declined, deferred).
- The companion as an initiator kind.
- Plan- or Occasion-bounded monitoring.

### D. Hard boundaries
- "Confidence, relevance, repetition, model judgment, organizer convenience, and silence never expand authority."
- No model-inferred "personality, emotional investment, social role, or identity" (§11).
- A vote is never generalized to taste.
- No routine review questions.
- Life is not a "personality dossier."
- An edit grant does not authorize autonomous AI work.
- "Notification delivery and silence cannot resolve changed participation."
