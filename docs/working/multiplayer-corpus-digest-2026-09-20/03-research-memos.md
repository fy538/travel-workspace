## Doc 1. Consumer Psychology of Occasions, Identity, and Social Memory (2026-08-23)

Path: `/Users/feihuyan/travel-workspace/travel-agent/docs/research/consumer-psychology-of-occasions-identity-and-social-memory-2026-08-23.md`

The doc rates its own strength as "Evidence memo, not product or implementation authority." It calls its findings from workplaces, romantic relationships, online communities and lab conversations "transfer evidence—not proof of Vesper behavior." Every section separates Evidence, Product inference, Boundary and Evaluation implication.

### A. Findings

- **Context collapse (§4).**
  - A longitudinal study of 6,378 Facebook users found that network size and heterogeneity related to how people varied their language and emotional expression. The authors read this as lowest-common-denominator self-presentation (JCMC).
  - A 2024 diary study of 1,270 Instagram disclosures found that people imagine different audiences for persistent posts than for ephemeral Stories. The differences covered size, diversity, specificity and closeness.
- **Privacy as boundary regulation (§5).** The CSCW literature and contextual integrity treat privacy as whether an information flow fits its context, actors, purpose and transmission norms. It is not a binary state (PMC review). Granular controls create "privacy labor" and are often underused (§4 Boundary).
- **Time collapse (§6).** Old traces that persist can disrupt a person's current self-presentation when past and present selves become visible together. Resurfacing is not always harmful, so temporal context is part of whether an identity display is appropriate (Social Media + Society).
- **Liking gap (§7).** People systematically underestimate how much new conversation partners liked them. This held in lab conversations, first-year dorm relationships and a public workshop, and a 2025 compiled sample confirms it. It is an aggregate tendency only.
- **Weak ties and "fringeships" (§8).**
  - Daily interactions with acquaintances were associated with greater happiness and belonging (Sandstrom & Dunn).
  - Newer theory describes "fringeships": mutually recognized, setting-specific relationships near the edge of friendship, warm but without close-tie obligations (2025). This is theoretical work.
- **Belonging and distinctiveness (§9).**
  - Optimal distinctiveness theory treats inclusion and uniqueness as joint needs.
  - A 2026 newcomer study models successful entry as integration plus a distinctive voice.
  - A longitudinal newcomer study found that more peer connections were not steadily better for role clarity. Making sense of things inside a bounded peer context mattered more.
  - Most of this evidence comes from workplaces. The doc says to "transfer only the tension."
- **Shared reality and responsiveness (§10).**
  - Feeling understood relates to positive interactions and relationship outcomes (review).
  - Faster partner response timing predicts felt connection among both strangers and friends (PNAS-class study, PubMed 35042815).
- **Autobiographical and transactive memory (§11).**
  - Autobiographical memory serves self, social and directive functions.
  - Across three studies (n=1,102), social use of relationship memories was positively associated with romantic relationship quality. The effect depended on memory type and who was being addressed.
  - Best friends develop transactive memory systems, meaning each knows what the other knows. Stronger systems were associated with friendship quality.
  - Collaborative recall differs among strangers, friends and siblings. Collaboration changes vividness, emotionality and narrative.
  - Memory is reconstructive: "More capture does not create objective truth."
- **Reciprocity and contribution (§12).**
  - Motivations in online knowledge communities include belonging, affiliation, self-worth, trust, shared identity, reciprocity, feedback and efficacy. Indebtedness and normative pressure also motivate contribution.
  - A survey of 444 OpenStreetMap contributors found personal-but-shared need, geographic knowledge, learning and community participation.
  - Place-attachment research suggests that "communities of attachment can extend beyond residence and may support stewardship."
- **AI-mediated authenticity (§13).** The doc says "The evidence is not one-directional."
  - 45 dating users described an "authenticity paradox." They felt betrayed when AI mediation was discovered, and anxious about moving from a polished persona to an offline meeting (Cyrano Effect, qualitative).
  - A preregistered study (n=547) found AI-mediated messages were perceived as less trustworthy and authentic, and that participants valued disclosure.
  - Two preregistered trust games (n=1,637) found AI assistance improved efficiency without materially reducing behavioral trust in a transactional setting. The language was judged slightly less authentic.
  - Experiments on AI-mediated apologies found that more reliance on AI reduced perceived authenticity and willingness to forgive.
  - Disclosure alone does not fix this. Overly prominent "AI generated" labels also distort how a message is received.

### B. Product implications the doc draws

- **Twelve product laws (§15).**
  - Compile identity for a purpose, and do not expose a profile by default.
  - Current intent outranks historical inference.
  - Give newcomers both belonging and room for distinctiveness.
  - Use shared objects as footholds, and do not claim compatibility.
  - Make social escalation progressive, mutual and easy to end.
  - Treat co-attendance as contextual warrant, not friendship.
  - Keep shared occurrence and plural meaning separate.
  - Let AI retrieve, organize and propose, and keep relational expression human.
  - Show provenance at the level of the individual claim.
  - Reward contribution through consequence, learning and stewardship.
  - Make correction and forgetting normal actions.
  - Treat silence and a clean ending as successes.
- **Four visibility spaces as different acts (§4).** Private, Relational, Occasion and Public Place are different communicative acts. They are not "a universal biography with more fields unlocked." The system should compile the smallest relevant projection.
- **Permission scope and gates (§5).** Permission binds `source + statement + audience + purpose + occasion/time + allowed consequence`. Explicit gates are required at five category changes:
  - private to social;
  - Occasion to persistent relationship;
  - personal observation to Place claim;
  - generated text to human-authored expression;
  - co-attendance to reachability.
- **Facet lifecycle (§6).** Each facet carries when it was observed, when it was last affirmed, whether it is current, historical, tentative or expired, and a "not me anymore" correction.
- **Entry object (§9).** An entry object gives the newcomer a foothold and a possible contribution.
- **Afterglow is not one recap (§11).** It holds four things: My memory, Together, Relational, and a Retrieval map. The Retrieval map records "who captured or may remember which source, without exposing its private content."
- **AI role table by communicative function (§13).**
  - Retrieve a shared Place or source: act deterministically and cite.
  - Summarize logistics: draft or act within Plan authority.
  - Suggest a conversational foothold: propose with source and audience preview.
  - Describe why the host chose someone: never invent, and render the host's own intent.
  - Describe why an Occasion may matter to the recipient: compile separately permitted evidence and label the source.
  - Express affection, care, apology, gratitude or desire: help the person find their words only on request.
  - Declare friendship, compatibility or group meaning: do not author.
- **Continuity primitives (§7–§8).** These are a mutual "keep in orbit" action, an Occasion-bounded reply, and "let this end here" as a first-class choice.

### D. Opportunities named but not pursued

- **Hypotheses with disconfirmers (§16).** Six hypotheses are listed and none has been tested. They cover Occasion-relative identity, the shared-Place foothold, mutual low-commitment continuity, plural Afterglow, visible contribution consequence, and claim-level provenance.
- **Interview and diary study (§17).** The study is suggested and not run. Its recruit list names social situations that mostly go unserved:
  - frequent host;
  - invited newcomer;
  - quiet or peripheral participant;
  - recurring-group member;
  - partner or close friend on a long trip;
  - resident who has helped visitors;
  - highly connected participant with social overload;
  - person who recently lost a promising weak tie after an Occasion.
- **Continuity comparison (§7).** The doc proposes a four-arm comparison: direct contact exchange, keep-in-orbit, Occasion-bounded reply, and no prompt.
- **AI authorship test (§13).** It proposes a four-treatment test: human-authored, AI-organized and human-approved, AI-generated with provenance, and AI-generated without provenance.
- **Later reopening (§14).** "later Place or Occasion may reopen the relationship" is the last arrow of the joined model, and it is untested.

### E. Warnings

- Do not use compatibility scores ("92% Sorrento compatibility" preempts meaning). Do not assign a personality. Do not announce private constraints.
- Never tell a user that another person likes them. Never fabricate mutuality, infer romantic intent, or pressure a reply (§7).
- Do not maximize new connections. The doc warns that "Overexposure, social fatigue, safety, and status dynamics remain real" (§8).
- Do not assign social homework or optimize people for group performance (§9).
- Do not optimize groups toward consensus. Shared reality is not the same as agreement (§10).
- An AI summary can become the authoritative memory, and shared retelling reshapes individual recall (§11).
- Reciprocity can turn into obligation. Avoid rewarding volume or extracting resident knowledge. The doc notes that "'Local' is plural and contested" (§12).
- AI must not covertly speak as a friend, host or partner. When mediation is discovered, people feel betrayed (§13).
- A settings matrix creates privacy labor (§4).
- "The product must not render the sequence as an inevitable relationship funnel." (§14)

## Doc 2. Adjacent Consumer Products and Social Mechanics (2026-08-23)

Path: `/Users/feihuyan/travel-workspace/travel-agent/docs/research/adjacent-consumer-products-and-social-mechanics-2026-08-23.md`

The doc separates three evidence classes: observed product contract, company claim (not independent evidence of retention), and product inference. It adds: "Absence is not evidence of failure."

### A. Findings (§3 Executive synthesis)

- **One verb per product (§3.1).** Each successful product is carried by one legible verb. The rest accumulates around that primitive.
  - Partiful: invite people.
  - Howbout: find when friends are free.
  - Timeleft: show up to meet strangers.
  - Locket: send an ordinary picture.
  - Airbuds: let friends see what you are listening to.
  - Letterboxd: mark or log a film.
  - Beli: rank a restaurant against restaurants you know.
  - Strava: record an activity.
- **The source object (§3.2).** A source object removes the social blank-page problem. "The object supplies a reason to act and a safe thing to discuss."
- **Audience (§3.3).** Audience changes what a trace means. Audience is not "one post plus an audience picker."
- **Event memory (§3.4).** Event products are strongest before and at entry, and their memory is thin. The shared event ends up as a closed chat, a photo album or a past-event row. The underserved transition runs: "we were there together → what each of us noticed… was different → selected meaning remains available to the right relationship → it changes how a later Place or Occasion opens."
- **Automatic traces (§3.5).** Automatic traces lower effort and raise the risk of misinterpretation.
- **Accumulated identity (§3.6).** Accumulated identity works when the atom stays simple. The ladder runs: encountered, privately remembered, interpreted, made legible to a chosen person or Occasion, used later, and deliberately contributed to a Place.

### B. Product implications (§10 Laws, §13 Boundaries)

- **Seven laws.**
  - Borrow an existing intention.
  - Deliver value for one person before network value.
  - Use the object as the social warrant.
  - Escalate progressively.
  - Separate automatic evidence from authored identity.
  - Make memory produce capability and not only a recap.
  - Do not build the whole social stack. Interoperate and export instead.
- **Boundaries.**
  - No generic social feed and no people-discovery root.
  - No universal profile before an Occasion.
  - No compatibility claims from sparse evidence.
  - No permanent reachability for all attendees.
  - No collapse of personal and group memory.
  - No rebuilding of calendar, chat, ticketing or community infrastructure.

### D. Opportunities named but not pursued

- **Open territory (§11).** The open territory is "the joined middle": private attention, then Place- and Occasion-grounded consequence, then authorized social legibility, then plural memory, then a "materially better later encounter." The doc says this is defensible only through governed continuity and not through features.
- **User-research questions (§12).** Ten questions are listed and unasked. Notable ones:
  - Q1: an invitation that made you feel "specifically wanted."
  - Q2: a gathering where you knew few people.
  - Q3: someone you enjoyed meeting and failed to contact afterward.
  - Q8: "Have you ever helped a stranger or visitor understand a Place? What made the contribution feel worthwhile—or extractive?"
  - Q9: a time someone else's retelling felt wrong.
- **Deferred.** Persistent groups as something distinct from Occasions (Geneva) are acknowledged and explicitly deferred until place and Occasion value exists.

### E. Warnings

- Leaderboards, streak pressure and universal metrics (Strava).
- Constant ambient visibility used as engagement, and automatic traces exposed to friends by default (Airbuds).
- Match and compatibility scores (Beli, Timeleft, 222).
- Large data requests before trust is earned (222).
- Attendance treated as permanent reachability, or an organizer's graph turned into a social graph (Luma).
- Guest-list browsing as the main social mechanic (Partiful).
- An all-in-one community OS (Geneva).
- Notifying on every change to a plan (Howbout).

## Doc 3. Multiplayer Activation Psychology (2026-08-09)

Path: `/Users/feihuyan/travel-workspace/travel-agent/docs/research/multiplayer-activation-psychology-2026-08-09.md`

The doc's discipline rule says product inferences are "not a replicated travel-product result." It treats the 2026 agent benchmarks as preprints, calling them "warnings and evaluation tools, not settled evidence."

### A. Findings

- **§1 The pair as a cognitive unit.**
  - Couples' transactive memory works through specialization, credibility and coordination. Couples outperformed stranger pairs on bounded recall (Hewitt & Roberts). These were lab tasks and may not generalize.
  - A 2025 Delphi study produced 219 dyadic behavior-change techniques by specifying "who performs what for whom" (Di Maio et al.). Actions can be joint, parallel or cross-over. The domain was health, not travel.
- **§2 Interest to action.**
  - Implementation intentions (if–then plans) showed a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment across 94 tests (Gollwitzer & Sheeran).
  - Choice overload is conditional (meta-analysis of 99 observations, Chernev et al.). It is moderated by complexity, difficulty, preference uncertainty and goal.
  - Limit: these intentions are written by the user. "An AI inferring 'you meant to go' from a save is materially different."
- **§3 Regret, FOMO, JOMO.**
  - Anticipated regret is associated with intentions and behavior (81 studies, n=45,618, health; Brewer et al.).
  - FOMO correlates with problematic social-media use and is not shown to improve experiences.
  - JOMO is early-stage research.
  - A 2026 meta-analysis found that freedom-threatening language drives anger and reactance, while framing in general does not (Quick et al.).
- **§4 Receptivity.** *My Phone and Me* logged 10,372 notifications from 20 users. Disruption varied with presentation, sender relationship, current task and personal traits, and even useful content disrupted. The study is small and dated. ProACT (3,244 examples) is a 2026 preprint and an offline benchmark.
- **§5 Multi-user privacy.** The sources are contextual integrity (Nissenbaum) and a month-long smart-home study of 7 households. Even non-adversarial multi-user systems produce tensions around access, transparency, privacy and **power** (Zeng & Roesner). GateMem and PiSAs show that state-of-the-art models make unreliable authorization judgments. The doc says these "falsify the idea that a strong model prompt is a sufficient privacy boundary."
- **§6 Persistent and ephemeral groups.** Group-recommender research distinguishes groups with joint history from ad-hoc ones (GroupIM; Ceh-Varela et al.). Aggregation results vary by dataset. Offline metrics "do not measure dignity, organizer relief, decision quality, or whether the experience happened."
- **§7 Place relationship is plural.** Place-attachment reviews show varied and context-dependent links to behavior (Chen et al.). The doc says to use the finding "to reject flattening," not to claim a validated model.

### B. Product implications

- Preserve "who knows, wants, validates, or handles what," and do not average two people into one taste profile (§1). The pair promise is this: "we know how you two get from possibility to a decision, and neither of you has to perform the other's role."
- Possibility lifecycle: noticed, understood, relevant, feasible, socially viable, experienced, remembered (§2).
- Offer one good next move and an honest alternative to doing nothing.
- Use real consequence only (§3). That means a real closing window, a weather edge, an overlap or a capacity limit, with a note on when the next similar window comes. "Keep tonight quiet" counts as a successful outcome. Measure pressure and trust.
- Rank surface and timing separately from recommendation quality (§4). This includes "a private ask to one person before any group disclosure" and "no surface at all." Non-response is ambiguous.
- Privacy rules (§5):
  - Private relevance is not authority to share.
  - Circle membership is not permission for every type of information.
  - Revocation must stop future derivations.
  - Group composition gets the minimum safe representation.
- Five social units with different priors (§6):
  - Pair.
  - Household or family, which is "recurring but authority and safety can be asymmetric."
  - Recurring circle, with "joint rituals and meaningful outcome history."
  - Ephemeral party, where the system should "infer less and expire more."
  - Solo, with "full value without social pressure."
- Place relationship can express "familiarity, curiosity, identity, ritual, competence, care, grief, social memory, or a wish to return" (§7).
- There are eight safe working principles (§8).

### D. Opportunities and studies not yet done (§9)

1. Save-to-action diary.
2. Opportunity-framing trial. The arms are factual window, **social convergence**, **serendipity**, quiet alternative, and control.
3. Dyad and group role simulation: who notices, validates, decides, vetoes and handles logistics.
4. Consent-comprehension test for **circle, place and nearby permissions** "before any ambient delivery."

The doc also notes that household or family as a distinct unit, and the rituals of recurring circles, are named and not developed.

### E. Warnings (§8 "Claims this evidence does not support")

- FOMO increases retention without harming trust.
- Couples want an AI to infer or mediate their roles.
- Silence means permission.
- A shared save means shared intent.
- A group average is fair.
- A useful notification is a welcome one.
- An LLM can safely decide which private context to share.
- Place attachment predicts a specific action.

Two further warnings sit outside that list. Do not infer relationship health, dominance or fixed roles from a small history, because "A repeated behavior can be habit, unequal labor, convenience, or accident." Never "diagnose a user's fear of missing out."

## Doc 4. R7: AI Intervention Timing for Group Planning Conversations

Path: `/Users/feihuyan/travel-workspace/travel-agent/docs/research/R7-ai-facilitation-group-decisions.md`

The doc rates its evidence as mostly short, synchronous lab tasks with students or work teams, with limited ecological validity. Several of its conclusions are its own synthesis.

### A. Findings

- Inactivity alone is a weak trigger. The strongest systems key on process state: stage drift, participation imbalance, unaddressed disagreement and stalled progress.
- Delivery matters as much as timing.
  - Private, targeted, low-embarrassment prompts beat public call-outs.
  - "ask before acting" is preferred, as in the "Observe, Ask, Intervene" approach from a 2025 co-host study.
  - Public prompts to quiet people produce one reply and then withdrawal when the quietness comes from low confidence.
- Process facilitation and content facilitation are different. Content interventions can help group dynamics and can also feel intrusive. Rigid scripted process was ineffective.
- Structure plus facilitation improves even contribution, fairness, and alignment between the group's consensus and members' privately held opinions (DebateBot).
- Task boundary: facilitation helps well-defined, high-coordination tasks. Ill-defined ones, which include travel planning, are less predictable.
- Near-convergence is not the same as stuck. Sometimes silence is right, and a devil's-advocate prompt is only worth it for consequential decisions.
- Timing: a 3–4 minute monitoring interval suited one synchronous task, and the doc says not to transfer that number. In a Wizard-of-Oz study, 44% of cues were not noticed.
- How AI advice is weighed:
  - Algorithm appreciation holds for objective tasks and aversion holds for subjective ones.
  - There is an "authenticity discount."
  - AI exerts limited normative pressure, and hosts rationalized away its critical feedback.
  - AI helps when neutrality is valuable. Bot in the Bunch "reduced the relational burden of calling on quiet participants" because the bot can say the awkward thing.
- "To Err is AI": false-positive prompts raised participation and lowered trust. False negatives felt better and missed people who were actually quiet.
- Social loafing falls when a contribution is visible, consequential and expected. Peer-mediated prompts can "teach group members how to involve each other."
- ReDBot elicited preferences from chat history for restaurant choice and rated well on fairness, consensus and optimality.

### B. Implications

- Treat idle time as an eligibility filter only.
- Use process nudges before content nudges.
- Ask before diagnosing people.
- Prefer private channels.
- Make compromise proposals only when deadlock is clear.
- Use a four-state policy: active exploration (stay silent), balanced convergence, participation imbalance (private or peer-mediated prompts), and deadlock with clear clusters (summarize, then propose).

### D. Open

- No threshold for asynchronous friend-group chat.
- No canonical definition of "stuck."
- The AI's role in adjudicating tradeoffs between people is immature.

### E. Warnings

- Public diagnosis of individuals.
- Content pushed early.
- Rigid structure.
- Tuning loafing detection for sensitivity, which over-detects.

## Doc 5. R8: Behavioral Traces for Modeling Group Dynamics

Path: `/Users/feihuyan/travel-workspace/travel-agent/docs/research/R8-behavioral-group-modeling.md`

### A. Findings

- Roles, dominance, participation inequality, cohesion, hierarchy and brokerage can be inferred from interaction traces without message content. The supporting literatures are sociometric badges and Pentland's work, reality mining, email and IM metadata, and Wikipedia edit logs. This evidence is strong.
- Classifying whole groups as autocratic, consensus-based or emergent has no mature benchmark. This evidence is weak.
- Woolley 2010: collective intelligence tracks social sensitivity and equal turn-taking. It does not track cohesion or the smartest member.
- Communication quality matters more than frequency (Marlow meta-analysis).
- Recurring interaction patterns go with worse information sharing, and participative patterns go with better (Hoogeboom).
- High-performing teams show exploratory discussion, timely summarization and conflict coordination (Zhao 2024).
- Network centralization harms collective problem solving (Bernstein).
- Synchrony predicts cohesion (TeamSense, AUC above 0.8).
- Signals emerge quickly. 15 minutes of conflict interaction predicted team performance months later at up to 91% accuracy (Jung). Judgments from 10-second clips were meaningful. Initial rapport in new triads predicts later bonding. Style labels need more episodes than this.
- Microsoft telemetry on more than 10,000 new employees shows how onboarding communication networks grow and remain incomplete.
- Monitoring damages trust, autonomy and creativity when it feels punitive. Reality mining also captured "nonparticipating alters" (people who were not participants), which raises consent questions. The 2024 EEOC wearables warning is cited: "just metadata" is not ethically trivial.

### B. Implications

- A two-layer, trace-first model: individual positional roles first, then group interaction regime derived cautiously over time.
- Purpose-limited collection, obvious notice, and opt-in.
- "group-level reporting by default."
- No punitive or evaluative framing.

### D. Open

- Archetype labels.
- Calendar-only inference.
- Whether trace models beat text models.

### E. Warnings

"If people believe the system is secretly ranking influence or identifying 'problem users,' they are likely to react very differently."

## Doc 6. R9: Group Recommendation Strategies

Path: `/Users/feihuyan/travel-workspace/travel-agent/docs/research/R9-group-preference-aggregation.md`

### A. Findings

- There is no universally optimal aggregator. The field moved from treating the group as a "virtual user" to a "social, negotiated, fairness-sensitive, and context-dependent" problem (Jameson & Smyth 2007 and later surveys).
- Average hides polarization. Least misery lets one voice dominate. Amer-Yahia adds explicit disagreement terms.
- Most Pleasure scored lowest on perceived fairness, consensus and satisfaction in a 2024 preregistered study (n=399). The tie-strength justification for it was anecdotal.
- Sequential fairness means reweighting by each member's prior satisfaction, so the same person is not disappointed repeatedly (Xiao et al. and related work).
- Group configuration matters. Uniform and coalitional groups tolerate many rules. Minority and divergent groups score lower on all measures. The authors suggest an adaptive strategy.
- In the 2024 study, the amount of explanation made no significant difference. What mattered was framing that communicated fairness and fit.
- UMAP 2018 studied 150 participants in 41 groups choosing a destination. Social relationships and diversity shaped the decision process.
- Organizer-centric, or single-user, group recommendation is "legitimate and understudied" (MyFoodGRS, 2023–26). The tasks are anticipating others' preferences and managing incompatibilities.
- Tourism recommendation deals with bundles, is emotional and contextual, has sparse data, and has thin field validation.

### B. Implications

- Filter by hard constraints first.
- Then use average utility with a disagreement penalty.
- Switch to least misery when the group is divergent or the downside is high.
- Make interaction organizer-centric, and do not make optimization organizer-centric.
- Rebalance over time.
- Write copy such as "fits everyone's must-haves and keeps tradeoffs small," and do not name the algorithm.

### D. Open

- Mapping relationship type to the best aggregator.
- Large-scale field validation.
- Organizer support, which is very recent.

### E. Warnings

- Using Most Pleasure as a default.
- A minority being silently sacrificed in the mean.
- Exposing algorithm mechanics as the explanation.

## Doc 7. Group Chat Facilitator (canonical product doc, updated 2026-08-16)

Path: `/Users/feihuyan/travel-workspace/travel-agent/docs/product/Group Chat Facilitator.md`

### A. Findings

The doc rates its own confidence. HIGH applies to the unified gate, fail-closed privacy, and privacy as contextual-integrity flow-control. The commercial teardown is partial. Agency-mode evidence comes from a single study of 18 people.

- "When to speak" is a separable capability that LLMs do not have zero-shot. Supervised fine-tuning gave up to a 23-percentage-point gain. Silence counts as an action (GroupGPT, ProMediate, Inner Thoughts, turn-taking survey).
- Judge-action gap: LLMs classify information as sensitive about 98% of the time and still leak it about 33% of the time. Chain-of-thought makes this worse. MAGPIE's baseline is 50–60% leakage under explicit privacy instructions. Detecting an implication leak is a Theory-of-Mind inference, and LLM Theory of Mind is brittle.
- Reactive by default. In Koala, 72% preferred reactive behavior. Slack, Teams and Telegram are gated behind @-mentions.
- Transparency dilemma: disclosing AI carries a trust penalty, and concealment followed by exposure is worse.
- ProMediate: proactive mediation wins cleanly only in the hardest, competitive setting (+3.6pp consensus, 77% faster, best case). "facilitation is not a universal good."
- Refuted claims, which the doc says not to cite:
  - GroupGPT's claim that generalization-sanitization solves implication leaks.
  - Two "disclosure builds trust" findings.
  - Telegram per-message isolation mechanics.

### B. Implications

- The principle is a "quiet facilitator" with five behavioral rules:
  - It speaks less than a normal chatbot.
  - It speaks with a job: clarify, decide, summarize, propose, record, or protect privacy.
  - It uses structured objects when the group needs action.
  - It moves sensitive reasoning to private channels.
  - The group can see and control its level of agency.
- The room is "not a messaging-growth bet," and low message volume is compatible with success.
- Do not build:
  - the four named agency modes;
  - a prompt-only should-respond gate;
  - chain-of-thought self-policing as a privacy guarantee;
  - trust in per-user data scoping as absolute (the 2024 Slack prompt-injection breach is cited).
- Shipped:
  - the structural group composer;
  - the fail-closed semantic guard;
  - the agency binary with threshold;
  - the dignified-exception handoff;
  - trip-scoped shared memory;
  - proposals, votes and receipts.

### D. Open

"handoff, caucus, mandate, repair, convergence, ritual, relay, recurring-group learning, and second-occasion value still require mode-specific evidence." Also open:

- Relationship-scoped shared memory, as opposed to trip-scoped.
- Legibility of shared memory when the roster changes.
- Agency control by members versus the organizer. This was resolved by accident in the organizer's favor and should be ratified or reversed.
- Private handoff when the sensitive context belongs to someone other than the person asking.
- Whether the facilitator should be overtly AI or human-passing: "no study tests it."
- The MAGPIE-style eval has not been run.

### E. Warnings

- Dominating the room.
- Treating facilitation as a default.
- Privacy that relies on the model policing itself.
- Hiding AI identity.

## F. Bigger-than-friends ideas (cross-document, with quotes)

1. **Strangers at a designed table (Doc 2 §6.1).** Timeleft is a "strong precedent for AI or service mediation reducing social activation cost."
2. **People and Place composed together for strangers (Doc 2 §6.2).** "people and place must be jointly composed; the venue is part of the social experience rather than a neutral container."
3. **Acquaintances and weak ties as a valid endpoint (Doc 1 §8).** "Not every worthwhile Occasion relationship should progress toward close friendship."
   - The ladder runs: recognized co-participant, Occasion-bounded exchange, recurring Place or interest contact, warm weak tie.
4. **Fringeships tied to a setting (Doc 1 §8).** "mutually recognized, setting-specific relationships near the edge of friendship." This is a third-place regular's relationship in all but name, and the doc never develops it.
5. **Newcomers and social entry (Doc 1 §9, §17).** The doc names "invited newcomer" and "quiet or peripheral participant," and frames the foothold-plus-contribution idea. The evidence is workplace onboarding. No newcomer-to-a-city study exists in the corpus.
6. **Hosts (Doc 1 §13, §17; Doc 2 §5.1).**
   - "frequent host" is a recruit category.
   - Doc 1 says "Describe why the host chose someone — never invent; render host-authored intent."
   - Doc 2 asks about an invitation that made you feel "specifically wanted."
7. **Residents and visitors, and stewardship (Doc 1 §12).** "communities of attachment can extend beyond residence and may support stewardship." Related items:
   - The recruit "resident who has helped visitors."
   - Doc 2 Q8 on helping a stranger understand a Place.
8. **Public Place contribution with a visible downstream consequence (Doc 1 §12).** "Did someone use it in a real encounter?"
9. **Clubs and practice communities (Doc 2 §8.3).** Strava shows "club or Occasion formation around actual practice." Recorded solo behavior leads to in-person groups.
10. **Persistent communities as distinct from Occasions (Doc 2 §9.1).** "A book club, neighborhood group, and large community do not fit one flat group chat." Pursuit is deferred.
11. **Households and families (Doc 3 §6).**
    - The doc says: "household/family: recurring but authority and safety can be asymmetric."
    - §5 adds power tensions in households.
    - Nothing intergenerational is developed anywhere in the corpus.
12. **Recurring circles and ritual (Doc 3 §6; Doc 7 §3).**
    - Doc 3 has "joint rituals and meaningful outcome history."
    - Doc 7 lists "ritual, relay, recurring-group learning" as open.
13. **Bounded reachability after an event with strangers (Doc 2 §5.3; Doc 1 §7).** The mechanisms are keep-in-orbit and the Occasion-bounded reply. The recruit is "person who recently lost a promising weak tie."
14. **The socially overloaded person (Doc 1 §17).** "highly connected participant with social overload" means the social value on offer is reduction, not addition.
15. **AI as the neutral voice in a group (R7).** The bot "reduced the relational burden of calling on quiet participants." Related ideas:
    - Peer-mediated inclusion that teaches members how to involve each other.
    - Sequential fairness across a trip (R9).

Third places, neighborhoods, intergenerational relationships and loneliness are not addressed by name in any of the seven docs. "Neighborhood group" appears once, as a Geneva example. The closest constructs are fringeships, recurring Place contact and place-attachment stewardship.

## G. Not reflected in the ten directions

The ten directions are all friend-to-friend, mostly pairs, local and present-tense. This list measures the docs against them.

1. **Entry into a group where you know few people.** Foothold plus distinct contribution, and Occasion-relative identity projections (Doc 1 §9, §3.1).
2. **The host's job.** Composing people with a place, the host's own words on "why I invited you," and making a guest feel specifically wanted (Doc 1 §13; Doc 2 §5.1, §12 Q1).
3. **Stranger and acquaintance formation.** "Just show up" designed Occasions where people and place are composed together (Timeleft, 222).
4. **Continuity after an Occasion with someone who is not a friend.** Mutual keep-in-orbit, the Occasion-bounded reply, liking-gap relief, and "let this end here" (Doc 1 §7–§8; Luma).
5. **Weak ties and fringeships as a product outcome.** Setting-specific recognition around a Place, with no messaging cadence (Doc 1 §8).
6. **Plural and transactive memory.** The items are:
   - My memory, Together, Relational and the Retrieval map.
   - "who remembers what."
   - Divergent meanings.
   - Correcting a wrong retelling.

   Direction 10 covers only past experience improving the next time. It does not cover plural authorship or findability (Doc 1 §11).
7. **Contextual identity with a lifecycle.** Audience-specific projections, time collapse, "not me anymore," and expiry (Doc 1 §4–§6).
8. **Public Place contribution and stewardship.** Exchange between residents and visitors, contestable perspective, and visible downstream use (Doc 1 §12; Doc 2 Q8).
9. **Group sizes and types beyond pairs.** The items are:
   - Household or family with asymmetric authority.
   - Recurring circles with rituals.
   - Ephemeral parties.
   - Persistent communities and clubs (Doc 3 §6; Doc 2 §8.3, §9.1).
10. **Group decision mechanics.** The items are:
    - Preference aggregation with protection for the worst-off.
    - Sequential fairness across a trip.
    - Organizer support.
    - Divergent and minority group configurations.
    - Explanations that communicate fairness (R9).
11. **Facilitation.** The items are:
    - When to speak.
    - Private prompts to quiet members.
    - The AI as the neutral voice that says the awkward thing.
    - Peer-mediated inclusion.
    - Four-state policy.
    - Caucus, mandate, repair and relay (R7; Doc 7).
12. **Roles and division of labor.** The items are:
    - Who notices, validates, decides, vetoes and books.
    - Organizer relief.
    - Trace-based role inference that is governed and not punitive (Doc 3 §1; R8).
13. **Authenticity governance in AI-mediated communication.** The role table by communicative function, and claim-level provenance. "Playful exchanges" and "small acts of help" sit directly in the sensitive zone: affection, gratitude and apology stay human-authored (Doc 1 §13).
14. **Multi-user privacy as a source of value.** The items are:
    - A constraint shapes the group's answer and is never exposed.
    - The dignified private handoff.
    - "busy" without details.
    - Revocation that stops future derivations (Doc 3 §5; Doc 7; Howbout).
15. **The right not to act and social overload.** The items are:
    - JOMO.
    - "keep tonight quiet."
    - The clean ending.
    - Attention governance.
    - The highly connected participant (Doc 3 §3–§4; Doc 1 §8, §17).
16. **Real-window activation for a group.** A factual closing window, social convergence, and serendipity framings, which the docs propose to test without FOMO (Doc 3 §3, §9).
17. **Interoperating with existing tools instead of owning the room.** Partiful, Luma and group chat remain elsewhere, and Vesper supplies understanding, consequence and memory (Doc 2 Law 7; Doc 7 §1).
18. **One legible verb and value for one person first.** The ten directions assume a friend graph. The docs warn that value must exist before network density (Doc 2 §3.1, Law 2).
19. **Second-occasion and relationship-scoped recurrence.** Shared memory that survives a change of roster and reopens a relationship at a later Place or Occasion (Doc 7 Gap F; Doc 1 §14; Doc 2 §3.4).
20. **Long trips with a partner or close friend.** This is a distinct social situation and appears as a recruit category (Doc 1 §17).
