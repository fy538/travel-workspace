# DOCUMENT 1: Gathering Psychology, Social Accessibility, and Occasion Continuity (2026-08-23)

## A. Social values and human payoffs

**From §2, the "human goods" table (eleven goods):**
- Belonging: "I am genuinely wanted here."
- Ease and safety: "I understand enough to enter and leave."
- Autonomy: I can participate without surrendering myself, through private Plans, optionality, split/rejoin, and silence.
- Contribution: "I can add something that matters."
- Shared attention: "for a moment, we were really here together."
- Discovery: I meet a new side of a person, a place, or myself.
- Play and spontaneity: something unplanned happened.
- Recognition: something I noticed, felt, or did was seen.
- Care: people looked out for one another, and private constraints became group-safe consequences.
- Continuity: "This did not vanish when the night ended."
- Ritual and memory: "This became part of who we are."

**From §1:**
- Less fear of not belonging before the gathering.
- Less fear of losing a promising connection afterward.
- Together these let a person "stop managing my social position and be present."

**From §3, ten conditions of a great gathering:**
- A deliberate invitation.
- Social legibility: the room is not opaque.
- At least one foothold.
- Participation without performance, where quiet presence is legitimate.
- Shared attention.
- Dimensional encounter.
- Recognized contribution without scoring.
- Agency to move, pause, split, rejoin, or leave.
- Care without accounting.
- A natural continuation.

**From §3.2, invitee "aha" moments:**
- Someone thought of me.
- I know enough to walk in without rehearsing.
- I have one natural way in, without being matched to a person.
- I can be quiet, arrive late, or leave without becoming socially illegible.
- Something private was protected without my having to explain it.
- I can live my own version of the experience.
- The interesting person I met stays findable, with no forced contact exchange.

**From §3.2, host "aha" moments:**
- Guests understood why they were invited and how to enter.
- The host did not personally carry every introduction, reminder, constraint, and transition.
- The host could care for someone without publicly explaining that person's need.
- Guests contributed and connected without the host orchestrating it.
- The Occasion kept meaning after the logistics ended.

**From §3.2, group "aha" moments:**
- One coherent choice without polling everyone.
- Splitting and differing are not group failure.
- "We attended to something together rather than administering togetherness."
- Different honest memories instead of one official recap.
- The next thing the group did emerged from what actually mattered this time.

**Other payoffs:**
- §4: presence during the gathering, the phone serving the Occasion rather than displacing it, and silence as a positive outcome.
- §5 and §7: less epistemic asymmetry for a newcomer. The doc names "reassurance theater" as the thing to avoid.
- §5.1: private help that is not broadcast, such as "Who else is new?", "Help me enter", and "Help me leave".
- §5.1: host stewardship, a quiet cue to the host that a newcomer may need incorporation.
- §5.2: five graded levels of feeling wanted.
- §6: a stranger becomes a dimensional person through experience, not through a profile.
- §6.1: common ground is the ramp, and the deeper value is safe difference. Existing friends discover new dimensions of each other.
- §6.2: what matters is felt responsiveness and one natural opening, not disclosure volume.
- §6.3–6.5: a person controls "what I am sharing." A featured Status serves as a cold opener.
- §6.5: hosts get privacy-safe hospitality intelligence, "not guest dossiers."
- §6.6: a public self organized around perspective, curiosity, and openness, not followers. Plural public Place knowledge grows from it.
- §6.6: cold start is "useful creation, not profile completion."
- §10: connection follows from sharing a world. People can be found by episode ("the person from Lena's dinner").
- §10.5: a private "remember this person" action with no social demand.
- §10.2: the Occasion is the social warrant for reaching out later.
- §12 and §12.1: the next contact is about something in the world (the cinema example), not an abstract coffee chat.
- §13: on trips, people share a temporary life without losing their separate selves.
  - Private time is not rejection.
  - The emotional arc is protected, as on the Capri proposal day.
  - Roles are distributed, narrow, and revocable, so care does not harden into invisible labor.
  - Trip memory stays plural.
- §13.2: consequences of power differences become legible without a hierarchy dashboard.
- §15.2: the compounding goods are perceptual capability, faithful self-understanding, identity through contribution, a relationship with a Place, and trust built through correction and silence.

## B. Named concepts

- **Occasion as "bounded, temporary shared world"** (§1): it has an invitation and reason, plural personal Plans, a participation grammar, plural Outcomes, and a boundary that closes, fades, or stays permeable.
- **Social accessibility / "stairs but no ramps"** (§5.1): fix the environment's routes into participation rather than coaching the person.
- **Invitation ≠ incorporation** (§5).
- **Retreat loop** (§5): uncertainty → wait → reveal less → others assume distance → peripherality feels confirmed.
- **Five levels of feeling wanted** (§5.2): Permission, Inclusion, Recognition, Belonging, Significance. The AI cannot fabricate levels three through five.
- **Human intent vs recipient relevance** (§5.3): a four-layer table of host intent, recipient relevance, mutual context, and participation fit.
- **Five provenance modes** (§5.3): `host_authored`, `self_shared`, `mutually_visible`, `occasion_derived`, `unknown`.
- **Dimensionality ladder** (§6): Category → Attribute → Story → Perspective → Enacted character → Shared history.
- **Natural opening** (§6.2): grounded, permissioned, specific, ignorable, and left for the humans to author.
- **Private world model vs chosen social expression** (§6.3): "remember this for me" is a different permission from "use this to represent me."
- **Four visibility spaces** (§6.4): Private continuity, Relational share, Occasion participation, Friends/public expression. The doc says these are "not a simple widening ladder."
- **Featured share / Status** (§6.5).
- **Contribution-first footholds** (§6.5): inside an Occasion, the default ways in are who is hosting, what people are bringing, and a shared Place or question, not a profile card.
- **Hospitality intelligence** (§6.5).
- **Living public world** (§6.6): a public projection built from chosen relationships with Places, questions, and contributions, not a bio, follower count, or content feed.
- **Place-to-person-to-Place network effect** (§6.6).
- **Relationship opacity and seven powers** (§7): structural, epistemic, status, disclosure, interpretive, continuity, logistical.
- **Minimum truthful topology** (§7): the fewest authorized facts about who knows whom that a newcomer needs for orientation.
- **Situational participation modes** (§8.2): thirteen modes, never shown or stored as fixed labels.
  - Convener/host, careholder, newcomer, quiet participant, social explorer, bond deepener, free agent.
  - Scout/local, connector, meaning-maker, boundary keeper, spontaneous contributor, continuity seeker.
- **Variation axes** (§8.1).
- **Occasion ethos** (§8.3): the Occasion's character, such as intimate or open, structured or spontaneous, familiar or friends-of-friends.
- **Relationship gradient** (§10.1): co-present → recognizable → remembered → contextually reachable → open to another encounter → emerging relationship → friendship.
- **Social warrant** (§10.2).
- **Afterglow** (§10.3): a phase-relative read over the Occasion, not an owner of its records.
- **"Keep in orbit" / remember this person** (§10.5).
- **Export rail** (§10.6): Instagram is where a handle gets exported, not the relationship model.
- **Bounded social permeability** (§11).
- **Trip as ecology** and **temporary shared life** (§13).
- **Expectation contract** (§13.3): the shared understanding a trip group needs about togetherness, budget, pace, and private time, inferred rather than surveyed.
- **Formation and calibration** (§13.4).
- **Group-safe consequence** (§13.6).
- **Distributed responsibility** (§13.7).
- **Six trip phases** (§13.8): formation, calibration, friction, differentiation, rejoin, closure. They are "reasoning aids, not interface states."
- **Five product responsibilities** (§14): Meaningful invitation, Private participation brief, Live Now, Afterglow, Future opening.
- **Intelligence-to-attention ratio** (§4.1).
- **Flywheel and thirteen nested loops** (§15): curiosity deepening, situated identity, meaningful invitation, social foothold, weak-tie continuation, host hospitality, contribution identity, multiperspective memory, artifact consequence, Place→person→Place, return and deepening, correction and agency, silence and trust.
- **Four product moves** (§15.3): Make sense, Open possibility, Help it work, Carry forward.
- **Anti-loops** (§19.1): seven.
- **Permissioned context graph** (§21.1).
- **Contextual introduction engine** (§20, item 20).
- **Relational operating system** (Closing).

## C. Big or ambitious ideas

- **Occasion as a temporary world.** "The Occasion is therefore more than an event record. It is a bounded, temporary shared world." (§1)
- **Strangers, newcomers, and friends-of-friends as first-class users.** "Many gatherings have stairs into participation but no ramps." (§5.1)
- **Hosts and guests.** "The host receives hospitality intelligence, not guest dossiers." (§6.5) The host contributes "intent, composition, norms, welcome, and stewardship." (§3.1)
- **Weak ties.** "Connection should be a consequence of sharing a world, not a request made under time pressure." (§20)
- **Relationship formation as a graded process.** "The product should not skip steps. It should make the intermediate states useful." (§10.1)
- **Identity and public self.** "What am I sharing, with whom, in my words, and for what human opening?" (§1) "Who has made a relevant perspective, question, or form of participation available here?" (§6.6)
- **Places as a corpus of plural perspective.** "the public corpus becomes plural perspective rather than reviews or universal ranking." (§6.6)
- **Trips.** "A trip is therefore not one very large Occasion. It is an ecology of personal Plans and overlapping shared Occasions." (§13) The Portugal example has a partial overlapper, a late joiner, a selectively participating local, and a partner with private meanings.
- **Power.** "Soft power becomes logistical power." (§13.2 heading)
- **AI as a restrained group participant.** "'Vesper correctly stayed silent' is a first-class positive Outcome." (§4.1) The §4.1 interrupt criteria include "a person can quietly care for another in a way they have authorized."
- **Care for something high-stakes.** On the Capri proposal day the product protects "the energy and emotional arc of the Occasion," not the number of attractions. (§13.5)
- **Contribution as identity.** "identity through care, knowledge, attention, and action." (§15.2)
- **Ritual and long arc.** "The second Occasion is where relationship and Place memory compound." (§21.1) The doc also mentions a "recurring circle" (§11), "return and deepening" (§15.2), and family ritual research (§22).
- **A new social graph theory.** "The social graph is better understood as a permissioned context graph." (§21.1)
- **Non-social branch of the flywheel.** It "also works when no gathering occurs." (§15)
- **Closing ambition.** "a relational operating system for shared life in the world."

## D. Deferred, gated, rejected, and open

- **Everything is a hypothesis.** Candidate primitives must be tested "as a storage-neutral fixture" before any schema work, and "Several may be projections rather than new durable objects." (§16.1)
- **Premature ideas (§22).** The reason given is that they are not behavior-backed. The doc's posture is to connect behaviors people already perform. It lists:
  - AI social coaching.
  - Compatibility scoring.
  - Inferred friendship.
  - Extensive proactive reconnection.
  - Emotionally mandatory invitation fields.
  - Complex Occasion workspaces.
- **Differentiated but less directly established (§22):** personal and shared Afterglow, Place-aware interpretation, and partial overlapping Plans.
- **Rejected ideas:**
  - A host-authored "why you" as a required field. The doc calls it "an unsupported chore." (§20, item 17; §5.3)
  - A universal or Occasion-relative profile, because it "would flatten the person and add pre-gathering homework." (§6.5)
  - A compatibility questionnaire for trips. (§13.3)
  - Fixed personality types. (§8)
  - Stranger matching or social discovery as a starting point. Any later extension must arise "from real Place, interest, Occasion, host, or community context with strong consent." (§19, item 10)
- **Trip specialization.** The current Trip/living-itinerary specialization stays a launch guarantee. Personal projection is "a refinement being evaluated." (§16.2)
- **Open founder decisions (§21.3, seventeen in total).** They cover:

  - The minimum facet object.
  - Which inferences are eligible for private curation.
  - How to preview "what Vesper can say about me" without a permissions dashboard.
  - Defaults across the four visibility scopes.
  - How public discovery is governed without follower or popularity ranking.
  - Whether the afterglow window is implied by membership or opt-in.
  - What "remember this person" does before reciprocity exists.
  - When an external handle may appear.
  - What host-authored intent is required versus what may render only as recipient relevance.
  - Which topology facts are safe and useful.
  - How long recognizability persists by default.
  - Whether a host can offer a warm bridge without exposing a private need.
  - Home versus push for a future opening.
  - Which surface owns each afterglow element.
  - Which trip responsibilities are inferred, assigned, or informal.
  - "simplest close ritual that does not become reflection homework."
- **Ambiguity I noticed.** §6.5 says a Status is "not selected by an Occasion," and §1 says "The Occasion does not select a person's identity facet." But §20 item 20 and the coverage ledger describe the Occasion as "a contextual introduction engine that selects one relevant, authorized facet." The main body appears to override the provenance list, but the document does not reconcile the two.

## F. Boundaries and prohibitions

- **The ten constitutional boundaries (§19):**
  - Attention: enhance the world; do not capture the person.
  - Agency: do not imprison people inside an inferred personality, role, or historical Plan.
  - Mediation: never simulate wanting, care, responsiveness, or intimacy in another person's voice.
  - Privacy: private truth may shape a safe consequence without becoming group knowledge, and Occasion membership does not erase scope.
  - Relationship truth: never infer friendship, romantic intent, hierarchy, compatibility, mutuality, or reachability from co-presence.
  - Social pressure: no streaks, read receipts, popularity metrics, response deadlines, or penalties for ignoring an opening.
  - Memory: keep memory plural, and let correction and current intent outrank historical inference.
  - Location: no people tracking.
  - Host power: the host does not own participants' Plans, relationships, memories, or future contact.
  - New-group formation: never start from stranger matching.
- **Never visualize (§7):** "social centrality, mutual counts, popularity, influence, compatibility scores, inferred status, who is 'important,' or a map of private relationships."
- **Never fabricate affection or host intent (§2, §5.3).** Never restate recipient relevance as the host's intention.
- **No diagnosing anxiety, no scoring helpfulness, no single authoritative group story (§2).**
- **Afterglow limits (§10.4):** no psychological profile assembled from private chats, inferred location, or speculation.
- **Co-presence (§11):** it "does not authorize friendship, unrestricted messaging, location access, private memory, or future push."
- **The product "must not become where the gathering happens." (§1)**
- **No fossilizing trip roles (§13.4).** The doc's examples are "the planner," "the tired one," and "the local."
- **Anti-metrics (§18):** group size, contacts created, graph density, popularity, time in app during an Occasion, notifications, disclosure volume, agreement rate, universal participation, memory volume, and AI-authored messages between people.
- **Seven anti-loops (§19.1).**

## G. Not covered by D1–D10

1. **The stranger and newcomer problem.** All ten directions assume existing friends. Doc 1 centers on people who know only the host, or one or two others. Its ideas here are social accessibility, the retreat loop, minimum truthful topology, "who else is new?", and help entering and leaving.
2. **Host as a served user.** This includes hospitality intelligence, stewardship cues, relief from carrying every introduction, and warm bridges the host owns. D7 "happy for company" is the nearest direction, but it has no host-craft layer.
3. **Invitation as a meaning-bearing object.** This covers the five levels of feeling wanted, host intent versus recipient relevance, provenance per statement, and the Occasion's ethos and participation grammar: whether someone may join partly, quietly, late, or not at all.
4. **Meeting someone new and not losing them.** This covers the relationship gradient, the afterglow, "keep in orbit," episodic people search, the Occasion as social warrant, and Instagram as an export rail. None of D1–D10 produces a new relationship.
5. **Governed social identity.** This covers the four visibility spaces, "what I am sharing," the featured Status, and a living public world. It also covers identity through contribution, and the split between "remember this for me" and "represent me to others."
6. **Dimensionality.** People get to know each other through story, perspective, and enacted character. This also applies to existing friends discovering difference. D3 borrows a perspective, but does not deepen knowledge of the person.
7. **Relationship opacity and power.** The doc names seven powers, including logistical power on trips. No D-direction addresses asymmetry.
8. **Groups larger than a pair.**
   - One coherent choice without polling.
   - Split and rejoin as normal topology.
   - Group-safe consequences.
   - Distributed revocable roles, and keeping care from becoming invisible labor.
   D8 touches help during an activity, but only between friends and not as group care.
9. **Trips as temporary shared life.** This covers the ecology of Plans, partial overlaps, the expectation contract, calibration, trip phases, protecting the emotional arc, and couple-specific privacy such as the proposal.
10. **Plural memory.** Personal, pair, group, and Place memories are separate, with no synthetic recap. D10 uses the past to improve the next time, but does not address whose memory it is.
11. **Presence and silence as the product goal.** This covers the intelligence-to-attention ratio and the phone returning people to the room.
12. **The contribution taxonomy beyond help.** It includes intent, presence, perspective, initiative, situated judgment, practical labor, care, boundaries, evidence, meaning, and continuation. It also includes recognition without scoring. D4 covers only offers and requests.
13. **Public Place perspective from non-friends.** Examples are a local, a historian, or a traveler with the same question, and the Place→person→Place loop.
14. **Ritual, recurring circles, and return to Places.** These are long-arc loops.
15. **Correction and agency as a social value.** People can correct, narrow, retire, forget, leave, or revoke.

# DOCUMENT 2: Differentiated Social-Multiplayer Strategy (2026-08-21)

## A. Social values and human payoffs

- **Knowing one another through attention.** "through how they attend to the world" (Executive decision).
- **Building a shared life.** "across anticipation, planning, presence, memory, and return" (Executive decision).
- **Being thought of.** "I noticed this here, it made me think of you" (Executive decision). Recognition is the value, not the quality of the media (§5.1).
- **Experiencing a Place through an absent person.** The examples are a friend from last week, "a parent who knew a neighborhood decades ago," and an absent local host (§1.3).
- **The Place as a way to relate without discussing the relationship directly.** It is something to "notice through, learn through, remember through, and return to together" (§1.1).
- **Borrowed lens.** A human's situated judgment combined with Vesper's current operational truth (§5.2).
- **Ordinary glimpses sustain closeness.** Imperfect captures can carry more relational value than highlights (§6.2).
- **Low-pressure reach-out.** It overcomes hesitation, and non-response is a valid result (§6.4, §8.4).
- **Responses that extend, not react** (§6.5).
- **Shared anticipation as lived value.** "planning is part of the lived social experience" (§6.6).
- **Togetherness over isolated optimization** (§6.7).
- **One small decision without polls.** This protects quiet members and private reasons (§6.8, §5.4).
- **A pair's "our version" of a Place.** Related: recurring-circle chapters and rituals, and returns that visibly use shared history (§2.2).
- **Mutual-interest reveal with no disclosure on decline** (§5.3).
- **Plural memory.** One shared occurrence with separate personal meanings (§4.3, §6.9).
- **Less organizer burden.** Async context reduces synchronous planning work (§3, §9.2).
- **Value for thin participants.** "personal value retained by thin participants" (§9.2).
- **Public realm value.** Place questions get answered by positioned, disagreeing contributors. Contributors see what became of their contribution. Storytelling supports belonging to a Place community (§2.3, §5.5, §6.12).
- **Reciprocal value for residents and contributors.** This comes through "stewardship, community support, recognition, relationships, or paid expertise" (§2.3).
- **Feeling recognized, not watched.** "more recognized and more present—not watched, scored, exposed, or socially managed" (§7).
- **Trusted silence** (§7.2, §9.2).

## B. Named concepts

- **Ambient relational multiplayer** (Why this document exists; §1.4): nobody is organizing, and one person leaves attention, perspective, memory, or curiosity for particular people to encounter later.
- **Deliberate occasion multiplayer** (same sections): people intentionally shape, coordinate, live, repair, and remember something together.
- **Three tests** (Why this document exists): differentiated, appropriate, efficient.
- **Place as "third thing"** (§1.1): person A ↔ Place/Occasion/Encounter ↔ person B.
- **Trace, not a post** (§1.2): a trace is an observable cue, a situated perspective, an intended audience, a Place/time/Occasion, and a governed consequence.
- **Asynchronous shared perception** (§1.3): the Place is "a mailbox for attention."
- **AI mediates relevance, not intimacy** (§1.5): "make the opening easy; keep the expression human."
- **Three governed product realms** (§2): Personal, Friends and family, Public Place realm. The doc distinguishes these from Doc 1's four visibility spaces.
- **Social units** (§2.2): the pair, the recurring circle, and the ephemeral party. The pair is the "purest and likely highest-frequency unit."
- **Public-to-group ladder** (§2.3): public perspective → reciprocal or repeated interaction → bounded invitation → explicit shared Occasion → governed group.
- **Demand discipline** (§3): begin from behaviors people already perform, not from an abstract new chore.
- **Minimal relational trace contract** (§4.1): includes triggers (now, arrival, return, plan_state, manual) and continuations (none, reply, notice, compare, invite, move).
- **Seven core gestures** (§4.2): Keep for me, Leave for you, See this here, What do you notice?, Open together, Carry this forward, Contribute to this Place.
- **One Occasion, plural projections** (§4.3): shared core, plus personal, relational, group, and public projections. Related terms are My Shape and Together.
- **Private caucus; group-safe resolution** (§2.2, §8.4): named but not defined in this doc. It attributes them to the canonical Multiplayer Product Strategy.
- **Seven independent permission axes** (§7.1): media visibility, retention, inference, recall and reuse, naming, action, delivery context.
- **Combined lifecycle** (§1.4): notice → preserve → address → encounter → respond → possibility → invite → shape → decide → live → repair → plural memory → return, ritual, or new opening.
- **Phases A–D** (§8.4): 1:1 addressed attention, pair and circle continuity, deliberate Occasion bridge, public Place contribution.
- **Reference ambient compounding slice** (§8.3): a 1:1 trace followed across time as one behavioral prototype.
- **Prototype matrix** (§8.5): Menu trace, Sorrento handoff, Night-out bridge, Group repair, Public Place question.
- **Attention budget** (Directional decisions): one budget shared by personal and social proactivity.

## C. Big or ambitious ideas

- **Places as social medium.** "The Place is not merely the topic. It is the situated medium through which the relationship becomes more legible." (§1.1)
- **Time-shifted, intergenerational co-experience.** "a parent who knew a neighborhood decades ago… can still change the current encounter." (§1.3)
- **Family.** A "family neighborhood" can hold a story or ritual (§1.1). The realm is named "friends and family," but families get no dedicated treatment.
- **Public Place realm and communities.** "What can people who know, inhabit, or encounter this Place appropriately contribute to one another?" (§2.3)
- **Residents and experts with reciprocity.** "Residents and contributors are not free 'authenticity' suppliers for visitors." (§2.3)
- **Plural public truth.** A resident, a worker, a historian, and an operator give "different, positioned answers," and Vesper does not manufacture one "locals say" answer (§5.5).
- **Belonging to a Place community.** "neighborhood storytelling as a pathway through which occupants can become members of a Place-based community." (§6.12)
- **New-group formation from the public realm.** It proceeds through the public-to-group ladder under concept B, never through stranger matching (§2.3).
- **Public invitations.** "explicit invitations to a walk, event, workshop, or shared Occasion." (§2.3)
- **Contributor outcome loop.** Vesper "later returns the outcome to contributors when allowed." (§5.5)
- **AI as governed group participant.** It "removes infeasible options, privately protects sensitive constraints, proposes one legible choice." (§5.4)
- **Rituals and long arc.** "a later Place, return, ritual, or invitation can reopen the thread." (Executive decision)
- **Core sentence.** "Vesper lets people leave attention, memory, and perspective in the world for others to encounter." (§14)

## D. Deferred, gated, rejected, and open

- **Public Place realm (Phase D).** It comes "only after private and bounded-group trust is proven." Moderation, contributor supply, identity, reciprocity, and launch readiness are not established (Evidence boundary table; §8.4).
- **Arrival-triggered or Place-timed delivery.** This is a later "consent and attention-policy experiment, not a Phase-A dependency." It needs recipient-side permission (§7.1, §8.3). Phase A has "no background arrival trigger."
- **Unproven demand (§3).** Six items "must be tested rather than assumed":
  - Automatic delivery on arrival.
  - A persistent "our Place" view.
  - AI-timed resurfacing preferred over ordinary messages.
  - AI inferring whom an object should be sent to.
  - Reflecting relationship patterns back to groups.
  - Residents or experts wanting to contribute.
- **Not authorized to build (§11).** The reason given is that these would make the product feel like "another network demanding maintenance":
  - Friend activity feed.
  - Public live-location broadcasting.
  - Nearby-stranger discovery.
  - Follower or creator economy.
  - Generic reactions and engagement loops.
  - Universal group profile.
  - Collaborative itinerary editing as the main product.
  - Preference questionnaires or poll-heavy planning.
  - Automatic shared memory.
  - Autonomous emotional outreach in a user's voice.
  - Permanent current facts from private captures.
  - One synthetic "local" perspective.
  - A new root navigation item for every social mode.
- **Rejected starting points (§8.1):** a feed, a social root tab, a generic inbox, a creator profile, and a collaborative workspace. Also rejected: a universal social-memory table or a graph rewrite (§8.2).
- **Group travel stays the launch wedge.** The ambient lane runs alongside it and does not supersede it (Evidence boundary).
- **Falsification conditions (§10.2).** The strategy should be reconsidered if:
  - Place-timed traces are experienced as "creepy more often than caring."
  - Ordinary traces create no more value than standard media sharing.
  - Users do not understand the difference among sending, retaining, inferring, recalling, and acting.
  - Async context does not reduce planning burden.
  - Vesper's mediation feels like being socially managed.
  - Personal and shared projections repeatedly confuse or expose users.
  - Public contribution becomes status performance or resident extraction.
  - The second occasion shows no felt advantage over cold-start chat and maps.
- **Open questions (§12).** There are eight:
  - Whether a handoff arrives immediately, on Place relevance, or by sender choice.
  - Which response action deepens connection without creating obligation.
  - How to make "our version of a Place" visually legible without becoming "an archive, score, or shared profile."
  - When Vesper may suggest a recipient, and how it explains that suggestion.
  - The minimum density and consent comprehension for relational Home or push openings.
  - Which recurring-circle patterns are worth confirming explicitly.
  - What reciprocal value sustains locals and experts.
  - When a relational thread deserves an Occasion and when it should stay an unfinished trace.

## E. Research evidence

- **Overall stance (§6 opener).** The research "does not prove this product will work, but it gives the strategy credible mechanisms and specific tests." There are no per-study strength ratings. The studies:
  - Shared attention amplifies experience: Boothby, Clark, and Bargh (2014), plus the psychological-distance follow-up (§6.1).
  - Ambient awareness and digital closeness: Levordashka and Utz; Lehmann and Utz on friends' groups (§6.2).
  - Directed communication beats broadcast for well-being: Burke and Kraut, plus the private-communication study. The doc calls this "more consistently associated" (§6.3).
  - Surprise of Reaching Out, the liking gap, and underestimated compliments (§6.4). The caveat is that these "do not justify relationship reminders or pressure."
  - Liu, Kang, and Wei (§6.4): help with a relational act from AI or a human lowers perceived effort and relationship satisfaction. Outsourcing authorship weakens the act.
  - Capitalization: active, constructive responses to good news (§6.5).
  - Anticipation: Kumar, Killingsworth, and Gilovich. Novel shared activities: Aron et al. (§6.6).
  - Togetherness over option quality: Garcia-Rada et al. (§6.7).
  - Shared decision-making, and preference-clarity distraction (§6.8).
  - Autobiographical memory functions, remembering in conversation, and socially shared retrieval-induced forgetting (§6.9).
  - Photography and enjoyment: Diehl et al.; Barasch et al. on intention to share; the "documenting penalty" (§6.10).
  - Context collapse, self-presentation, geomedia, and locational privacy (§6.11).
  - Ball-Rokeach, Kim, and Matei on neighborhood storytelling (§6.12). The doc bounds this explicitly as "evidence about community storytelling and belonging, not proof that a Vesper public Place realm will work."

## F. Boundaries and prohibitions

- **No inferred intimacy.** Never infer intimacy, speak emotionally on a user's behalf, auto-publish, or treat contact frequency as relationship truth (§1.5).
- **Permissions stay separate.** Never collapse the seven permission axes (§7.1).
  - Sending media does not authorize publication, inference, location exposure, future invitations, or arrival resurfacing.
  - A sender's wish never creates a right to interrupt the recipient.
- **No obligation mechanics (§7.3):**
  - Read-receipt anxiety.
  - Response deadlines.
  - Streaks or contribution quotas.
  - Automatic resurfacing of every nearby friend's memory.
  - Penalties for declining or ignoring.
  - Inferred reciprocity from one send.
  - Notification storms near dense Places.
  - Location disclosure as the price of relevance.
- **No averaging.** Never average a pair or circle into one taste profile or vector (§2.2, §8.4).
- **No single group narrative.** Never compress memories into one authoritative group story (§6.9, §7.4).
- **Public realm limits (§7.5).** Never extract resident knowledge, create anonymous consensus, or promote a temporary observation into permanent Place truth.
- **No stranger matching.** Not from inferred location or interests (§2.3).
- **Anti-metrics (§9.3):** posts, followers, likes, streaks, reach, time spent, message count, notifications sent, public contribution volume, percentage of captures retained, social-graph growth, maximum consensus, and a synthetic group-satisfaction score.
- **The §11 "not authorized to build" list applies here too.**

## G. Not covered by D1–D10

1. **Place-anchored asynchronous encounter.** D1 and D3 cover seeing moments and borrowing a perspective. They do not cover leaving something at a Place for a named person to meet on arrival or return (§1.3).
2. **Absent and intergenerational presence.** Examples are a parent's neighborhood from decades ago and an absent host. This is the family time arc (§1.3, §1.1).
3. **The Public Place realm in full (§2.3, §5.5, §6.12, §7.5).** It includes positioned residents, workers, historians, and operators, contestable plural answers, and contributors seeing the outcome of their contribution. It also includes paid expertise and stewardship as reciprocal value, and neighborhood belonging through storytelling.
4. **Group formation from public interest.** See the public-to-group ladder under concept B (§2.3). Public walks, workshops, and events are invitations.
5. **A pair's "our version" of a Place.** Related: recurring-circle Place chapters and rituals. This is a durable relationship-place object. D10 improves the next time, but it does not make "our place" visible or create return promises and open loops (§2.2, §8.4).
6. **Shared anticipation as its own value.** The run-up to an event is part of the experience. D5 treats planning as conversion (§6.6).
7. **Response grammar of extension (§6.5).** Examples are notice, compare, carry, return, and open-together. D9 "playful exchanges" is adjacent but not the same.
8. **Governed group decision.** This covers private caucus, one legible choice, quiet members protected, live repair under authority, and ephemeral-party repair (§5.4, §8.5). D8 is only partial.
9. **Double-blind mutual-interest reveal.** No disclosure on decline (§5.3). This is a possible mechanic for D5 and D6 that those directions do not name.
10. **Human voice and effort preservation.** The Liu, Kang, and Wei finding that outsourced authorship weakens the act (§6.4).
11. **The permission model as experienced value.** The seven axes, including the recipient's consent to the delivery context (§7.1).
12. **Togetherness over optimization** as a ranking principle (§6.7).
13. **One Occasion, plural projections.** Related: value retained by thin participants (§4.3, §9.2).
